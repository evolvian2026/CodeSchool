## Appendix D. Assessment rubrics and report-card format

CBSE asks for continuous, competency-based assessment for CT&AI, including written tests with CT puzzles, group activities, practical work, projects and a **Teacher Observation Journal**, but it has not published a template or report-card format. CodeSchool provides the following, mapped to CBSE's competency areas.

### Competency rubric for the observation journal

| Competency | Beginning | Developing | Secure | Extending |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Abstraction | Needs help to ignore unimportant details | Picks out key details with prompts | Identifies the key details on their own | Explains which details matter and why |
| Pattern recognition | Spots simple repeating patterns with help | Continues patterns with one change | Finds and extends patterns with two or more changes | Creates and explains new patterns |
| Decomposition | Needs help to break a problem into steps | Breaks a familiar problem into parts | Breaks new problems into clear parts | Plans the parts and their order independently |
| Algorithmic thinking | Follows a short sequence with help | Follows and fixes simple sequences | Writes sequences with loops and conditions | Designs, tests and improves their own algorithms |
| AI literacy (Classes 6-8) | Names everyday uses of AI | Explains that AI learns from data | Trains or tests a model and explains its results | Identifies bias and suggests fairer data |

### Project rubric (used for every Class 6-8 project)

| Criterion | 1 Beginning | 2 Developing | 3 Secure | 4 Extending |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Problem definition | Problem unclear | Problem stated but vague | Clear problem and goal | Clear problem, goal and success measure |
| Data or algorithm | Little data or no clear steps | Some data or steps, with gaps | Complete data or working algorithm | Well-organised data or efficient, tested algorithm |
| Testing and improvement | Not tested | Tested once | Tested with new cases and improved | Systematic testing with recorded improvements |
| Ethics and fairness | Not considered | Mentioned | Discussed with an example | Changed the work to make it fairer |
| Communication | Hard to follow | Partly clear | Clear and organised | Clear, engaging and well supported by evidence |
| Teamwork | Little contribution | Some contribution | Shared work fairly | Led and supported others |

### Term summary for the report card (example)

| Area | Grade | Teacher comment |
|---|---|---|
| Computational thinking (overall) | Secure | Breaks problems into steps and writes loops confidently |
| Coding (build levels completed) | 12 of 15 levels | Ready for conditionals with variables |
| AI literacy (Classes 6-8) | Developing | Trained a classifier; working on explaining bias |
| Project | 3 (Secure) | Clear charts; testing could be more systematic |

## Appendix E. Sample lesson: "Robo makes choices" (Class 4, Level B3)

This lesson pairs with the Class 4 NCERT chapter *Hide and Seek* and teaches if-then-else choices. It runs in one 40-minute period with no devices (a projector is optional). A full printable version with the student sheet is available on request.

| Step | Minutes | Teacher does | Students do |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Predict and run | 8 | Draws a row of five squares (START, PUDDLE, plain, PUDDLE, plain) and shows a program: repeat 4 times, move forward, *if* on a puddle *then* say "Splash!" *else* say "Step" | Write what Robo will say, then one student acts as Robo while the class reads the program aloud (answer: Splash, Step, Splash, Step) |
| 2. Investigate | 8 | Asks four tracing questions, including "what if the if-else were outside the loop?" | Discuss in pairs and explain why the check happens every time |
| 3. Modify | 8 | Draws a new row with STAR squares | Change only the if-else so Robo says "Star!" on star squares |
| 4. Parsons puzzle | 8 | Hands out jumbled blocks, one of which is a trick | Put the blocks in order so Robo reaches the goal without hitting a wall |
| 5. Make and exit ticket | 8 | Invites pairs to invent their own rule | Draw five squares, write "If Robo is on a ___ square, then ___, else ___", swap and predict; answer a two-question exit ticket |

**Common mistakes the script prepares teachers for:** thinking both branches run; thinking the "if" is checked only once.
