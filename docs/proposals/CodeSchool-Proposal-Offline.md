## 2. Why this programme

From 2026-27, CBSE schools are asked to teach Computational Thinking and AI: **50 hours a year in Classes 3-5**, inside Mathematics and The World Around Us, and **100 hours a year in Classes 6-8** (40 hours of CT, 20 hours of AI and 40 hours of projects). CBSE's free student and teacher handbooks are a strong start. They provide maths puzzle banks that follow the NCERT maths chapters, plus a few unplugged activities.

Teachers still have to fill several gaps on their own:

- **No coding.** Students follow and compare algorithms, but never write one.
- **No project materials.** Classes 6-8 have 40 project hours a year with no briefs, rubrics or examples. CBSE has announced a project list with rubrics, but it has not yet been published.
- **Thin AI support for teachers.** The AI chapters in the teacher books contain answer keys, not lesson scripts. Hands-on AI appears only in Class 8 and needs computers, webcams and internet.
- **No assessment format.** CBSE asks for a Teacher Observation Journal but provides no template or report-card format.
- **Hard puzzles with no on-ramps.** The handbook puzzles are demanding, with no hints or easier steps for students who struggle.

The Offline Programme fills these gaps on paper, so it works in any classroom.

> [!good] **Our approach:** we do not replace CBSE's free books. We sit on top of them, chapter by chapter, and add what is missing: levels with hints, "now you build it" coding on paper, project kits with rubrics, AI teacher scripts and a ready-made observation journal.

## 3. Objectives

1. Help teachers deliver CBSE's CT&AI hours with confidence, using ready scripts that fit one period.
2. Move students from **following** algorithms to **building** them, starting in Class 3.
3. Give Classes 6-8 complete, assessable project kits for their 40 project hours.
4. Make AI understandable through hands-on, unplugged activities in every year from Class 6.
5. Give the coordinator a simple, competency-based record for report cards.

## 5. Programme design

### Levels that follow the maths chapters

Every lesson is tied to the NCERT maths chapter the class is studying, so teachers find "today's" material in one place. Within each chapter, students move through short levels: a warm-up, a guided level, a build level and a challenge. A student who finishes early moves ahead; a student who struggles gets a hint card. The teacher keeps the class together.

### One routine for every lesson (about 40 minutes)

| Step | What happens | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Predict and run | Students predict what a short set of instructions will do, then act it out | 8 |
| 2. Investigate | Pairs answer tracing questions about why it worked | 8 |
| 3. Modify | Students change the instructions to do something new | 8 |
| 4. Parsons puzzle | Students put jumbled instruction cards in the right order | 8 |
| 5. Make | Pairs create their own rule or program and swap with another pair | 6-8 |

This structure is based on research on teaching programming (the PRIMM approach with Parsons problems), and it works without a computer. A complete sample lesson for Class 4 ("Robo makes choices") is available on request.

### Class-by-class outline

| Class | Computational thinking and coding | AI | Projects |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | Sequences, repeat loops and events on floor grids; secret codes (ciphers); path puzzles from the maths chapters | Sorting objects by features (enrichment) | Short class challenges |
| 4 | If-then-else choices ("Robo makes choices"); nested loops; error-finding with parity cards | Spotting patterns in pictures (enrichment) | Short class challenges |
| 5 | Variables and inputs on paper; searching and sorting strategies; a capstone game designed on paper | How examples teach a machine (enrichment) | Short class challenges |

Project examples follow the topics CBSE presented in its April 2026 webinar, and will be aligned to CBSE's official project list when it is published.

## 8. Teacher support and training

- **Orientation (2 hours, at school):** the lesson routine, a live demonstration lesson, and how to use the journal.
- **Scripts in every lesson:** timings, what to say, answers and the mistakes students usually make.
- **Weekly check-in during the pilot:** 15 minutes by phone or WhatsApp, plus a shared question channel.
- **Short how-to videos:** optional, for teachers who want to see an activity before running it.
- **No specialist needed:** the programme is designed so that a maths or EVS teacher can run it.

## 9. Assessment and reporting

- **Level checks:** a short check at the end of each chapter's levels, marked with an answer key in minutes.
- **Observation journal:** a one-page checklist per class mapped to CBSE's CT&AI competencies (abstraction, pattern recognition, decomposition and algorithmic thinking, plus AI literacy for Classes 6-8).
- **Project rubrics:** clear criteria and score bands for every project, for teacher and peer assessment.
- **Term summary:** a simple grade per competency, ready for the report card.
- **Optional baseline:** a 15-minute puzzle check at the start and end of the pilot to show progress.

## 11. Child safety and data protection

- **No student accounts and no student data.** The offline programme works entirely on paper.
- **No photos or recordings of students** are taken by CodeSchool.
- **Pilot results are shared only as class-level summaries** (for example, the share of students who passed a level check), with the school's permission.
- Student names stay in the school's own records and journal. If any data is ever needed, it will be handled under India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, with the school in control.

## 15. Risks and how we manage them

| Risk | How we manage it |
|---|---|
| Teachers have little time to prepare | Every lesson has a 5-10 minute script; orientation covers the routine once |
| Lessons run over a period | Every lesson is designed for 40 minutes, with an optional extension |
| Puzzles too hard for some students | Hint cards and warm-up levels in every chapter |
| CBSE publishes its own project list or rubrics | Project kits will be aligned to CBSE's topics when they are published |
| Pilot classes miss periods for events or exams | The plan allows 8-10 weeks and can pause and resume |

## 16. Offline or online: choosing the right fit

| Question | Offline Programme | Online Programme |
|---|---|---|
| Student devices needed? | No | Yes, shared devices (about 1 per 2 students during the lesson) |
| Where students practise | Paper workbook and classroom activities | App or browser, with automatic checking |
| Coding | On paper, with cut-out blocks | Block coding on screen |
| AI | Unplugged card labs; projector demonstration | Hands-on model training in the browser with supplied datasets |
| Teacher reporting | Printable journal | Live class dashboard and report-card export |
| Student data | None | School-managed accounts using roll numbers |

Many schools may prefer a **hybrid**: the Offline Programme in Classes 3-5 and the Online Programme in Classes 6-8, where the computer lab is available. See the separate Online Programme proposal.
