## 2. About CodeSchool

CodeSchool is a level-based programme being developed by **Bullseye Knowledge System Limited** to help schools teach computational thinking, coding and AI in Classes 3-8. The programme is being designed specifically for CBSE's 2026-27 CT&AI curriculum and for real Indian classrooms, where many schools have few computers and teachers have little preparation time.

Our design is based on:

- a detailed review of **all twelve CBSE CT&AI student and teacher handbooks** for Classes 3-8 and CBSE's April 2026 webinar;
- international computer-science standards, including the **2026 CSTA PK-12 standards** and the **AI4K12 "Five Big Ideas in AI"**, which show the order in which children learn these ideas best;
- research on teaching programming, including the **PRIMM** lesson structure (predict, run, investigate, modify, make), **Parsons puzzles** and **unplugged** activities; and
- conversations with principals, coordinators and teachers at partner schools, which continue through the pilot.

## 6. Programme design

### Levels that follow the maths chapters

Every lesson is tied to the NCERT maths chapter the class is studying, so teachers find "today's" material in one place. Within each chapter, students move through short levels: a **warm-up** (with hints), a **guided level**, a **build level** (where they create their own instructions) and a **challenge**. A student who finishes early moves to the challenge; a student who struggles uses a hint card. The teacher keeps the whole class on the same chapter. The full chapter-by-chapter map is in **Appendix A**.

### One routine for every lesson (about 40 minutes)

| Step | What happens | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Predict and run | Students predict what a short set of instructions will do, then act it out | 8 |
| 2. Investigate | Pairs answer tracing questions about why it worked | 8 |
| 3. Modify | Students change the instructions to do something new | 8 |
| 4. Parsons puzzle | Students put jumbled instruction cards in the right order | 8 |
| 5. Make | Pairs create their own rule or program and swap with another pair | 6-8 |

A complete sample lesson is in **Appendix E**.

### Coding without computers

Students learn real programming ideas (sequences, loops, events, conditions, variables and functions) using paper code cards and cut-out blocks that look like the blocks used in Scratch-style coding. Classmates or the teacher "run" the program by following it exactly, which makes mistakes visible and fun to fix. In Class 8, students read and trace short programs written in Python on paper. If a projector is available, the teacher can show the same program on screen.

### Every child can take part

- **Different paces:** warm-up levels and hint cards for students who need support; challenge levels for those ready to go further.
- **Language:** teacher scripts and key student pages can be provided in Hindi as well as English (to be confirmed for the school's medium of instruction).
- **Collaboration:** most activities are done in pairs or small groups, so students who find reading or writing difficult can contribute through discussion and acting out.
- **Inclusion:** physical and visual activities (floor grids, cards) help students with different learning needs; teachers can adapt timings.

- **Level checks** at the end of each chapter's levels, marked with an answer key in minutes.
- **Observation journal** with a four-level competency rubric (beginning, developing, secure, extending) for abstraction, pattern recognition, decomposition and algorithmic thinking, plus AI literacy in Classes 6-8.

- **Term summary** for the report card, with a grade and comment per area.
- **Baseline and end puzzle checks** (15 minutes each) to show progress over the pilot and the year.

## 23. Frequently asked questions

**Does this replace CBSE's free handbooks?** No. It works alongside them, chapter by chapter, and adds what they do not include.

**Do we need a computer lab?** No. Every lesson works on paper. A projector helps for some demonstrations but is optional.

**Who teaches it?** Your own maths and EVS teachers in Classes 3-5, and your subject and computer teachers in Classes 6-8, as CBSE suggests. No specialist is needed.

**Will it take extra periods?** No. It uses the hours CBSE has already allocated to CT&AI.

**Is coding really possible without computers?** Yes. Students learn the same ideas (sequences, loops, conditions, variables) by writing and "running" programs on paper, which research shows is an effective start. Schools can add screen-based coding later through the Online Programme.

**How is it assessed?** Through level checks, the observation journal, project rubrics and a term summary for the report card (Appendix D).

**What if CBSE changes the curriculum?** The programme is built around skills, with CBSE mapping kept separate, so it can be updated quickly.

**Can we stop after the pilot?** Yes. There is no obligation to continue.

## 25. Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| CT&AI | Computational Thinking and Artificial Intelligence, CBSE's subject for Classes 3-8 from 2026-27 |
