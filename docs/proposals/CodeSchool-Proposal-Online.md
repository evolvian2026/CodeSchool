## 2. Why this programme

From 2026-27, CBSE schools are asked to teach Computational Thinking and AI: **50 hours a year in Classes 3-5**, inside Mathematics and The World Around Us, and **100 hours a year in Classes 6-8** (40 hours of CT, 20 hours of AI and 40 hours of projects). CBSE's free handbooks provide maths puzzle banks and a few unplugged activities. They leave several gaps:

- **No coding.** Students follow and compare algorithms, but never write one.
- **No project materials.** Classes 6-8 have 40 project hours a year with no briefs, rubrics or examples; CBSE's announced project list with rubrics has not yet been published.
- **Hands-on AI only in Class 8**, and only with computers, webcams and internet.
- **No progress tracking.** CBSE asks for a Teacher Observation Journal but gives no template or report-card format.
- **Hard puzzles with no feedback.** Students get no hints and teachers have no quick way to see who is stuck.

The Online Programme uses the school's devices to add what paper cannot: instant checking, automatic hints, block coding on screen, AI models students train themselves, and live progress for the teacher.

> [!good] **Our approach:** we do not replace CBSE's free books. We sit on top of them, chapter by chapter, and add what is missing. Every online lesson also has a projector and paper fallback, so a lesson never stops because of a device or network problem.

## 3. Objectives

1. Let every student practise CT and coding at their own level, with instant feedback and hints.
2. Move students from **following** algorithms to **building** working programs, starting in Class 3.
3. Give Classes 6-8 hands-on AI every year: train a model, test it and find its bias.
4. Give Classes 6-8 complete, assessable project kits for their 40 project hours.
5. Give teachers and the coordinator a live view of progress and a report-card-ready summary.

## 5. Programme design

### Levels that follow the maths chapters

Each lesson is tied to the NCERT maths chapter the class is studying. Students move through short levels: a warm-up, a guided level, a build level and a challenge. The platform checks answers instantly, offers a hint after repeated mistakes, and lets fast students move ahead. A short placement check at the start puts each student at a suitable level, while the teacher can keep the whole class on the same chapter.

### One routine for every lesson (about 40 minutes)

| Step | What happens | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Predict and run | Students predict what a short program will do, then run it | 8 |
| 2. Investigate | Tracing questions, checked automatically | 8 |
| 3. Modify | Students change the program to do something new | 8 |
| 4. Parsons puzzle | Drag jumbled blocks into the right order | 8 |
| 5. Make | Students build their own program and share it with a partner | 6-8 |

Steps 1-4 are checked automatically; step 5 is creative work the teacher can view on the dashboard.

### Class-by-class outline

| Class | Computational thinking and coding | AI | Projects |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | Sequences, loops and events in block coding; cipher and path puzzles from the maths chapters | Sorting by features (enrichment) | Short in-app challenges |
| 4 | If-then-else choices; nested loops; debugging | Spotting patterns in examples (enrichment) | Short in-app challenges |
| 5 | Variables and inputs; search strategies; a capstone game | Training a simple picture sorter (enrichment) | Short in-app challenges |
| 6 | Custom blocks (functions); lists and data from the Data Handling chapter | What AI is; train and test a classifier with a supplied dataset | Two 20-hour projects (for example, weather data across states) |
| 7 | Data tables, charts and prediction | Classification, clustering and bias: rebalance a dataset and retrain | Two 20-hour projects (for example, a weekly food planner) |
| 8 | Reading and changing simple Python alongside blocks | The AI project cycle; no-code model building; a guided version of CBSE's Class 8 labs | Two 20-hour projects (for example, sports participation data) |

Project examples follow the topics CBSE presented in its April 2026 webinar, and will be aligned to CBSE's official project list when it is published.

## 8. Teacher support and training

- **Orientation (2 hours, at school):** the lesson routine, the teacher dashboard, teacher mode on a projector, and what to do when devices or internet fail.
- **Scripts in every lesson:** timings, what to say, answers and common mistakes.
- **Dashboard prompts:** which students are stuck and which level the class should do next.
- **Weekly check-in during the pilot:** 15 minutes by phone or WhatsApp, plus a shared question channel.
- **Short how-to videos** inside the teacher view.
- **No specialist needed:** maths and EVS teachers can run Classes 3-5; the computer teacher leads AI and projects in Classes 6-8, as CBSE suggests.

## 9. Assessment and reporting

- **Automatic level checks:** every level is scored instantly, with mastery shown per chapter.
- **Progress journal:** competency records mapped to CBSE's CT&AI competencies (abstraction, pattern recognition, decomposition and algorithmic thinking, plus AI literacy for Classes 6-8), with space for teacher observations.
- **Project rubrics:** criteria and score bands for every project; teachers score on screen or on paper.
- **Report-card export:** a term summary per student, ready to copy into the school's report card.
- **Baseline and end check:** a 15-minute puzzle check at the start and end of the pilot to show progress.

## 11. Child safety and data protection

CodeSchool is designed to work as a **data processor for the school**. The school decides what data is used and remains in control of it, in line with India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and its Rules, whose obligations for children's data take full effect in May 2027.

| Principle | What it means in practice |
|---|---|
| Minimum data | Accounts use the school's roll number, class and section. No photos, addresses, Aadhaar numbers or dates of birth |
| School in control | The school creates and removes student accounts; students cannot sign up on their own |
| Parent consent | Collected by the school using its admission records, with a notice and form supplied by CodeSchool in English and Hindi |
| No tracking or advertising | No advertising of any kind, no third-party trackers, and no behavioural profiling of students |
| Learning records only | We record scores on curriculum levels that teachers can see, not behaviour |
| No AI training on student data | Student work is never used to train AI models |
| Data in India | Data is stored in an Indian cloud region and protected by access controls and encryption |
| Deletion | Student data is deleted at the end of the agreement, or earlier on the school's request |
| Breach response | Prompt notice to the school, and reporting as Indian law requires |
| Written agreement | A data processing agreement is signed with the school before any student data is shared |

## 15. Risks and how we manage them

| Risk | How we manage it |
|---|---|
| Devices are shared or some do not work | Pair work, teacher mode on a projector, and a printable version of every lesson |
| Internet is slow or fails | Lessons download in advance, work offline and sync later |
| Teachers have little time to prepare | Every lesson has a short script and dashboard prompts |
| Students use the app but do not progress | Weekly usage and mastery reports to the pilot lead, with suggested next steps |
