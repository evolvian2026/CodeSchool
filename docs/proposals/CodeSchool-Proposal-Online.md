## 2. About CodeSchool

CodeSchool is a level-based programme being developed by **Bullseye Knowledge System Limited** to help schools teach computational thinking, coding and AI in Classes 3-8. The programme is being designed specifically for CBSE's 2026-27 CT&AI curriculum and for real Indian classrooms, where devices are usually shared, internet can be unreliable and teachers have little preparation time.

Our design is based on:

- a detailed review of **all twelve CBSE CT&AI student and teacher handbooks** for Classes 3-8 and CBSE's April 2026 webinar;
- international computer-science standards, including the **2026 CSTA PK-12 standards** and the **AI4K12 "Five Big Ideas in AI"**, which show the order in which children learn these ideas best;
- research on teaching programming, including the **PRIMM** lesson structure (predict, run, investigate, modify, make), **Parsons puzzles** and block-based coding;
- India's **Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023** and its Rules, which shape how we handle children's data from the start; and
- conversations with principals, coordinators and teachers at partner schools, which continue through the pilot.

## 6. Programme design

### Levels that follow the maths chapters

Every lesson is tied to the NCERT maths chapter the class is studying. Students move through short levels: a **warm-up**, a **guided level**, a **build level** and a **challenge**. The platform checks answers instantly, offers a hint after repeated mistakes, and lets fast students move ahead. A short placement check at the start of the year puts each student at a suitable level, while the teacher can keep the whole class on the same chapter. The full chapter-by-chapter map is in **Appendix A**.

### One routine for every lesson (about 40 minutes)

| Step | What happens | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Predict and run | Students predict what a short program will do, then run it | 8 |
| 2. Investigate | Tracing questions, checked automatically | 8 |
| 3. Modify | Students change the program to do something new | 8 |
| 4. Parsons puzzle | Drag jumbled blocks into the right order | 8 |
| 5. Make | Pairs build their own program and share it with another pair | 6-8 |

Steps 1-4 are checked automatically; step 5 is creative work the teacher can view on the dashboard. A complete sample lesson (shown in its paper form, which is also the fallback) is in **Appendix E**.

### Pair work on shared devices

Lessons are designed for two students per device. Partners swap the "driver" and "navigator" roles at each step, so both students type, think and explain. Each student signs in with their own roll-number account at the start of the period on shared devices, or the pair's work is credited to both partners, as the school prefers.

### Every child can take part

- **Different paces:** placement checks, hints and challenge levels mean no student is left waiting or left behind.
- **Language:** the app interface and teacher scripts can be provided in Hindi as well as English (to be confirmed for the school's medium of instruction).
- **Accessibility:** large touch targets, read-aloud instructions for younger classes, and colour choices that work for students with colour-vision differences.
- **Inclusion:** the teacher can assign easier or extension levels to individual students from the dashboard.

## 16. Child safety and data protection

CodeSchool is designed to work as a **data processor for the school**. The school decides what data is used and remains in control of it, in line with India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and its Rules, whose main obligations, including those for children's data, are expected to take full effect in May 2027.

| Principle | What it means in practice |
|---|---|
| Minimum data | Accounts use the school's roll number, class and section. No photos, addresses, Aadhaar numbers or dates of birth |
| School in control | The school creates and removes student accounts; students cannot sign up on their own |
| Parent consent | Collected by the school using its admission records, with a notice and form supplied by CodeSchool in English and Hindi |
| No tracking or advertising | No advertising of any kind, no third-party trackers, and no behavioural profiling of students |
| Learning records only | We record scores on curriculum levels that teachers can see, not behaviour |
| No AI training on student work | Student work is never used to train AI models |
| No open chat or sharing | Students cannot message each other or anyone outside the class; shared work is visible only within the class |
| No camera or microphone needed | AI activities use supplied datasets, so no images or voices of students are collected |
| Data in India | Data will be stored in an Indian cloud region and protected by access controls and encryption |
| Deletion | Student data is deleted at the end of the agreement, or earlier on the school's request |
| Breach response | Prompt notice to the school, and reporting as Indian law requires |
| Written agreement | A data processing agreement is signed with the school before any student data is shared |

## 24. Frequently asked questions

**Does this replace CBSE's free handbooks?** No. It works alongside them, chapter by chapter, and adds what they do not include.

**How many devices do we need?** About one device for every two students during the lesson. A class of 40 needs about 20 devices, which can be the computer lab or a shared tablet trolley.

**What if the internet goes down?** Lessons are downloaded in advance and keep working offline; results sync later. Every lesson also has a projector and paper version.

**Who teaches it?** Your own maths and EVS teachers in Classes 3-5, and your subject and computer teachers in Classes 6-8, as CBSE suggests. No specialist is needed.

**Will it take extra periods?** No. It uses the hours CBSE has already allocated to CT&AI.

**Is it safe for children?** Students sign in with school-created roll-number accounts, there is no chat, advertising or tracking, no camera or microphone is used, and a data processing agreement is signed before the pilot.

**How is it assessed?** Through automatic level checks, the progress journal, project rubrics and a term summary for the report card (Appendix D).

**Can students use it at home?** Not during the pilot. Any future home access would be optional and agreed with the school first.

**What if CBSE changes the curriculum?** The programme is built around skills, with CBSE mapping kept separate, so lessons can be updated centrally and quickly.

**Can we stop after the pilot?** Yes. There is no obligation to continue, and pilot data will be deleted.

## 26. Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| CT&AI | Computational Thinking and Artificial Intelligence, CBSE's subject for Classes 3-8 from 2026-27 |
| Computational thinking | Solving problems in steps a person or computer can follow: abstraction, pattern recognition, decomposition and algorithms |
| Algorithm | A precise set of steps to solve a problem |
| Block coding | Programming by snapping together visual blocks instead of typing |
| Loop | An instruction to repeat steps |
| Conditional | An if-then-else choice in a program |
| Variable | A named store for a value that can change, such as a score |
| Function | A named set of steps that can be reused |
| Parsons puzzle | Putting jumbled lines of code in the right order |
| PRIMM | A lesson routine: predict, run, investigate, modify, make |
| Classifier | An AI model that sorts examples into groups it has learned |
| Bias (in AI) | Unfair results caused by unbalanced or incomplete data |
| Data processor | An organisation that handles data only on another organisation's instructions (here, the school's) |
