## 1. Why teachers come first

CodeSchool is a **teacher-led** programme. In the Offline Programme there are no student devices: the teacher, the workbook, the classroom kit and the teacher book do all the work. If a teacher is unsure, the CT&AI period is the first one to be skipped or turned into a maths period. So the aim of training is not just to explain the books. It is to make every teacher **confident enough to teach the next lesson tomorrow**, and to keep them confident all year.

Most teachers who will teach CT&AI were never taught it themselves. In Classes 3-5 it will usually be the class teacher or the maths teacher; in Classes 6-8, the maths teacher or the computer teacher. Many will worry about three things: "I am not a coder", "AI is too new for me", and "When will I find the time?". The plan answers each one:

| Teacher's worry | What we do about it |
|---|---|
| "I am not a coder" | CT is taught unplugged, through maths chapters they already teach. Training lets them *do* each activity as a student first. |
| "AI is too new for me" | Classes 6-8 AI chapters are taught with cards and paper, not software. A short AI-literacy module covers just what they need, with plain-language answers to common student questions. |
| "When will I find the time?" | Every period is already scripted in the teacher book. Training is short, in school hours where possible, and counts towards the 50 hours of yearly professional development that NEP 2020 expects of teachers. |
| "How do I assess this?" | The assessment books give a one-page test per chapter with marking guides; training shows how to turn scores and observations into journal levels. |

### Principles

- **Do, then discuss.** Teachers experience each activity as students before they teach it.
- **Just in time.** Train for the next unit, not the whole year at once.
- **Close to the classroom.** Cluster sessions near schools, in school hours where possible, with follow-up in their own classrooms.
- **Peer-led over time.** Grow champion teachers who support colleagues, so support does not depend on a few trainers.
- **Measured.** We track confidence, classroom practice and student results, and change the training when they show a gap.

## 2. Who we are training

| Group | Typical role | Starting point | What they most need |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary teachers (Classes 3-5)** | Class teacher or maths teacher | Strong at activity-based teaching; little CT background | The CT vocabulary (algorithm, loop, condition, variable, debugging), managing kit activities, the observation journal |
| **Middle-school maths teachers (Classes 6-8)** | Maths teacher | Strong maths; little CT or AI | Seeing the CT idea inside each maths chapter, the AI chapters, running 20-hour projects |
| **Computer teachers (Classes 6-8)** | ICT or computer science teacher | Knows software and some coding | Teaching *unplugged* and through maths; the AI chapters as concepts rather than tools; project assessment |
| **Academic coordinators and heads of department** | Plan timetables, observe lessons | Varies | The programme map, timetabling the hours, observing and supporting CT&AI lessons |
| **Principals** | Approve adoption and timetable | Varies | Why CT&AI matters, CBSE's expectations, what support the school will get, how progress is reported |
| **Champion teachers** (from year 1) | Experienced CodeSchool teachers | Secure in the programme | Coaching colleagues, running school-level sharing sessions |

## 3. Teacher competency framework

Training is built around five competency areas. Each has three levels. A teacher should reach **Practitioner** in the areas for their classes during the first year.

| Area | Starter (after orientation) | Practitioner (end of year 1) | Mentor (champion) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. CT concepts** | Explains algorithm, pattern, decomposition, loop and condition with an everyday example | Traces and debugs the programs in the workbook; links each CT idea to its maths chapter | Explains misconceptions and designs new examples |
| **B. AI literacy and ethics** (Classes 6-8) | Tells AI from automation; explains learning from examples | Teaches the four AI chapters for their class; handles bias, privacy and safety questions | Leads discussion of new AI news and tools with colleagues |
| **C. Unplugged teaching** | Runs one kit activity using the teacher-book script | Runs every period type smoothly: warm-up, guided activity, pair work, exit check; manages groups and materials | Adapts activities for large classes and mixed abilities |
| **D. Assessment** | Uses an exit check and records one observation | Uses the chapter tests, marking guides and journal levels; writes term summaries | Moderates marking across sections and coaches others |
| **E. Projects** (Classes 6-8) | Knows the project steps and rubric | Runs both 20-hour projects, including group work and presentations | Helps other teachers plan and assess projects |

Area A applies to all teachers; B and E to Classes 6-8; C and D to all.

## 4. The training model

Training runs in four layers across the year, rather than as one event.

| Layer | What | When | Length | Format |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Orientation** | The programme, the books and kit, and the first unit, taught hands-on | 22 March-15 April 2027, before the first CT&AI period | 2 hours (minimum, in the launch plan) | Cluster or in-school session |
| **2. Foundation workshop** | Competencies A-E in depth for each band, with practice teaching | April-May 2027, or as a second session in a school's in-service days | 1 day (6 hours) | Cluster workshop, by class band |
| **3. In-term support** | Teach-ahead sessions before each block of units; monthly practice circles; classroom visits; a help line | All year | About 1-2 hours a month | Cluster, online call or school visit |
| **4. Growth and certification** | Certificate levels, champion teachers, an annual teacher meet | From term 2 | Ongoing | Portfolio and observation |

> [!good] **Hours per teacher.** Orientation (2 h) + foundation workshop (6 h) + four teach-ahead sessions (4 x 1.5 h) + six practice circles (6 x 1.5 h) adds up to about **23 hours** in the first year: almost half the 50-hour yearly expectation, all of it used directly in class.

### Why not one long training before the session?

Long one-off trainings are forgotten by the time a topic comes up in class. A short orientation gets teachers started on the first unit; the foundation day and teach-ahead sessions then arrive just before teachers need them.

## 5. Session designs

### 5.1 Orientation (2 hours, all teachers of a school or cluster)

| Time | Activity | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00-0:15 | Welcome; "What is computational thinking?" warm-up: teachers write the steps to make tea, then swap and follow each other's steps exactly | Feel what an algorithm and a bug are |
| 0:15-0:35 | The programme map: CBSE's CT&AI curriculum, hours for each class, how the chapters follow NCERT maths | Know what they will teach and when |
| 0:35-1:15 | **Model lesson**: the trainer teaches period 1 of chapter 1 for each band; teachers take part as students, using the workbook and kit | Seen and done one full period |
| 1:15-1:35 | Tour of the teacher book: the five-period plan, scripts, common mistakes, exit check, answer key; the assessment book | Can find everything for any lesson |
| 1:35-1:50 | Classroom routines: handing out and collecting kit pieces, pair roles, noise signal, what to do with fast finishers | Ready to manage the class |
| 1:50-2:00 | Plan the first week; questions; join the support group (optional, with consent) | A dated plan for the first CT&AI period |

### 5.2 Foundation workshop (1 day, by band: Classes 3-5 or 6-8)

| Session | Classes 3-5 | Classes 6-8 |
|---|---|---|
| 1. CT ideas in depth (75 min) | Sequences, loops, conditions, variables and debugging through the term-1 chapters | Functions, lists, searching and sorting, number bases, through the term-1 chapters |
| 2. AI literacy (60 min) | Optional short session: what AI is, using the Class 5 data chapters | Supervised, unsupervised and reinforcement learning; data, bias and ethics, using the four AI chapters |
| 3. Practice teaching (90 min) | In small groups, each teacher teaches one 10-minute part of a lesson; peers give feedback with the observation checklist | Same, plus one AI activity |
| 4. Assessment (45 min) | Marking the chapter tests together; agreeing journal levels | Same, plus the project rubrics |
| 5. Projects (45 min) | Not needed | Planning the two 20-hour projects: groups, timeline, data sheets, presentations |
| 6. Planning (30 min) | Term-1 timetable and the first teach-ahead date | Same |

### 5.3 Teach-ahead sessions (90 minutes, before each block of chapters)

Four times a year, at the start of each half-term: teachers work through the next 3-4 chapters as students, look at the common mistakes in the teacher book, and share how the last block went. For Classes 6-8, one session is about the AI chapters and one is about each project.

### 5.4 Practice circles (90 minutes, monthly, about six a year)

Small groups of 8-15 teachers from nearby schools, led by a trainer or a champion teacher, using the same structure every time:

1. **Share** (20 min): one success and one problem from the last month.
2. **Look at student work** (30 min): marked tests or project work; agree on what "Secure" looks like.
3. **Solve** (25 min): pick one common problem, such as a large class or a weak reader, and plan a fix.
4. **Commit** (15 min): each teacher picks one thing to try before the next circle.

Circles can be held online where travel is hard.

### 5.5 Classroom visits

A trainer or champion visits each new teacher at least **once a term** to watch a CT&AI period, using a short observation checklist (clear objective, students active, kit used well, exit check done, journal kept). The feedback has two strengths, one next step and a follow-up date. Visits are supportive, not inspections, and the notes go to the teacher, not the school management, unless the teacher agrees.

### 5.6 Leaders' briefing (45 minutes, principals and coordinators)

What CT&AI is and why it matters; CBSE's expectations; timetabling (one or two periods a week for Classes 3-5; the 100-hour structure for Classes 6-8); what support teachers get; how to observe a lesson; how progress is reported to parents.

## 8. Capacity, scaling and certification

### Capacity check

| Assumption | Number |
|---|---|
| Adopting schools (the range used in the launch plan) | 10-100 |
| CT&AI teachers per school (about one per class, 3-8, shared across sections) | 4-6 |
| Teachers to train | 40-600 |
| Teachers per orientation | 20-30 |
| Orientations needed | 2-30 |

One trainer can run two orientations a day in the 3.5-week window, so **one trainer covers up to about 30 orientations**. Beyond about 50 schools, a second trainer or a few experienced freelance trainers will be needed for the March-April peak, and practice circles should move to champion teachers.

### Growing champion teachers

From term 2, pick one champion in each school or cluster: a teacher who is Practitioner level in every area for their band and wants to help others. Champions get a 2-day training (coaching, running circles, observing lessons), a certificate and recognition in their school, and they lead circles and new-teacher support in year 2. This is what lets the programme grow without a large trainer team.

### Certificates

| Certificate | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **CodeSchool CT&AI Teacher: Starter** | Orientation attended |
| **Practitioner** | Foundation workshop, at least four circles or teach-ahead sessions, one positive classroom observation, a short portfolio (one marked test with journal levels; for Classes 6-8, one project rubric) |
| **Mentor (champion)** | Practitioner, plus champion training and one term leading a circle |

Certificates show the hours of training, so schools can count them towards teachers' yearly professional-development hours. If CBSE or a state body later recognises outside training providers, we will apply.

## 9. Measuring whether training works

| Level | Measure | How | Target, year 1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reach | Teachers oriented before their first CT&AI period | Attendance | 100% of adopting teachers |
| Reaction | Session rating | Two-minute form at the end | Average 4 out of 5 or higher |
| Confidence | "I can teach next week's CT&AI lesson" | Same short survey before orientation, after the workshop and at year end | Most teachers rate themselves confident by the end of term 1 |
| Practice | Lessons that meet the observation checklist | Classroom visits | Most observed lessons meet at least 4 of the 5 points |
| Student results | Average chapter-test scores and journal levels | Class record sheets (shared without student names) | Average at or above 6 out of 10 |
| Continuation | Teachers and schools continuing in year 2 | Renewals | High renewal; any drop explained |

Teachers' personal data (names, phone numbers, survey answers) is collected only with consent, stored privately, never in this public repository, and handled in line with the Digital Personal Data Protection Act. Student results are used only as class averages.

## 10. Requirements for the future training platform

The platform will come later. Building the training this way now means its content moves straight across. The platform should:

- **Follow the competency framework** (section 3): learning paths for each band (Classes 3-5, 6-8) and for computer teachers, coordinators and champions.
- **Teach chapter by chapter**: for each chapter, a short video model lesson, the key idea in two minutes, common mistakes and a five-question self-check, released just before the chapter is due.
- **Work on a phone with low bandwidth**: short videos, downloadable PDFs, offline viewing; English and Hindi first, other languages later.
- **Support practice and feedback**: teachers upload a short clip or photo of student work and get feedback from a trainer or champion.
- **Run live sessions**: schedule practice circles and teach-ahead sessions, take attendance and send reminders, with teachers' consent.
- **Issue certificates and track hours**: automatic hour logs and certificates linked to the levels in section 8.
- **Give a trainer dashboard**: who has completed what, confidence scores, observation results, and which schools need a visit.
- **Link to the Online Programme**: one sign-in with the online teacher dashboard when schools take the Online Programme.
- **Protect privacy**: collect the minimum data, ask for consent, let teachers see and delete their data, and keep student data out of the training platform.

| Phase | When | What |
|---|---|---|
| **0. Now** | 2027-28 session | PDFs, model-lesson videos (shared privately with teachers), forms for feedback, video calls for circles |
| **1. First version** | Ready for 2028-29 orientations | Learning paths, chapter videos, self-checks, certificates, hour logs |
| **2. Full platform** | During 2028-29 | Practice uploads with feedback, live-session scheduling, trainer dashboard, link to the Online Programme |

## 12. Main risks

| Risk | Early warning | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Schools cannot release teachers for training | Few sign-ups for workshops | Run sessions inside school in-service days or after school; offer the foundation day as two half-days or online |
| Teacher turnover in schools | New teachers mid-year | Short catch-up orientation, online or by a champion; recorded model lessons |
| One trainer is not enough | More than about 50 schools adopt | Contract freelance trainers in February; move circles to champions earlier |
| Training feels like extra work | Low attendance at circles | Keep sessions short and practical; count hours towards professional development; recognise good teaching publicly |
| Teachers are not confident with AI | AI questions dominate sessions | AI explainer, extra AI teach-ahead, and a question bank with simple answers |
| Visits feel like inspections | Teachers avoid them | Keep notes with the teacher; make visits supportive and agreed in advance |
