# Review of the free CBSE CT&AI handbooks

**Reviewed:** 5 November 2026 · **Why:** these free books are what every CBSE school will compare CodeSchool with. A principal will ask why they should pay for something CBSE already gives away.

## What I read

All handbooks are listed at https://cbseacademic.nic.in/ct-ai.html, a student book and a teacher book for each of Classes 3-8 (First Edition, March 2026). I read two of them in full:

| Book | Pages | URL |
|---|---|---|
| Class 4 Student Handbook | 52 | https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/CurriculumMain27/CTAI_Pri4SH_2026-27.pdf |
| Class 7 Teacher Handbook | 98 | https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/CurriculumMain27/CTAI_Pri7TH_2026-27.pdf |

**Not yet read:** Classes 3, 5, 6 and 8 (student and teacher books), the Class 4 Teacher Handbook and the Class 7 Student Handbook. The two I read share the same structure, so the others very likely do too, but the Class 6 and 8 projects and the Class 8 AI chapters still need checking.

## Summary

The books are **printed banks of maths puzzles, in multiple-choice form, arranged to follow the NCERT maths textbook chapter by chapter**. There are a few unplugged classroom activities and, from Class 6, a short set of AI exercises. They contain **no coding, no computer use and no project materials**.

| | Class 4 (Student Handbook) | Class 7 (Teacher Handbook) |
|---|---|---|
| Title | "Computational Thinking", with no AI | "Computational Thinking and Artificial Intelligence" |
| Structure | 14 chapters, one per maths chapter | Part 1: 8 CT chapters, one per maths chapter. Part 2: 4 AI chapters |
| Each CT chapter | About 10 multiple-choice puzzles plus one "Thinking Spot" puzzle | The same, with full worked solutions |
| Unplugged activities | 2: "Coin Flip Magic" (odd/even and error detection) and a floor "Sorting Network" | 2: punched-card binary sorting, and logic puzzle punch cards (true/false and if-then reasoning) |
| AI content | None | About 9 pages: exercises and answer keys only (multiple choice, fill in the blanks, true/false, short answers) |
| Coding or Scratch | None | None |
| Projects | Not applicable | None in the book, though the curriculum allots 40 hours to "two required projects" |
| Answers | Not in the student book | Full solutions |

## Findings

### 1. The books are built around the maths lesson

The Class 4 book says it is "designed as a companion to the Mathematics textbook" and "follows the same chapter sequence". Its 50 hours a year are "not added as an extra subject but is integrated into existing Mathematics and 'The World Around Us' (TWAU) periods".

The chapter titles appear to match the new NCERT maths textbooks one for one:
- **Class 4:** Shapes Around Us, Hide and Seek, Patterns Around Us, Thousands Around Us, Sharing and Measuring, Measuring Length, The Cleanest Village, Weigh It Pour It, Equal Groups, Elephants Tigers and Leopards, Fun with Symmetry, Ticking Clocks and Turning Calendar, The Transport Museum, Data Handling.
- **Class 7:** Large Numbers Around Us, Arithmetic Expressions, A Peek Beyond the Point, Expressions using Letter-Numbers, Parallel and Intersecting Lines, Number Play, A Tale of Three Intersecting Lines, Working with Fractions.

**So what:** the unit a CBSE teacher plans by is the **NCERT maths chapter**, not a CT concept. Anything CodeSchool sells for Classes 3-8 should be **indexed by NCERT maths chapter** so the teacher can find "today's" material in one tap.

### 2. The puzzles are hard

The questions are aptitude- and olympiad-style. Class 4 examples include:
- statements like "Is M = N?" where students must decide whether the information given is enough to answer
- choosing which faces are opposite on a folded cube
- ordering people in a queue from clues
- tracing a button-press sequence

They come with few on-ramps: the student book gives no hints and no answers, and there's no easier-to-harder progression within a chapter.

**So what:** this is where a **level-based structure adds real value**. The same chapter's puzzle skills can be broken into easier warm-ups, hints and worked examples, with harder levels for students who are ready. That supports the core hypothesis that schools will pay for scaffolding and practice, not for content.

### 3. Rule-following and paths are already in Class 4, but in puzzle form

The Class 4 book contains algorithm-style puzzles:
- **Hide and Seek, Q1:** walk through blocks and collect an item from each, "except from the block which comes immediately after a GREY BLOCK" (a rule with an exception).
- **Hide and Seek, Q3:** reaching places in a set number of turns on a street grid.
- **The Cleanest Village, Q7:** pressing coloured buttons in order (Red, Green, Blue, Green) and tracing what happens to the balls in a container (tracing an algorithm step by step).
- **Measuring Length, Q9:** finding the shortest path through tiles.

These are exactly the skills a block-coding level teaches. The books never take the step to *writing* the instructions; students only *follow* them.

**So what:** CodeSchool's Band B levels can be presented as "**the puzzle in Chapter 2, now you build it**". Level B3 (Robo makes choices) fits naturally with *Hide and Seek* and *The Cleanest Village*.

### 4. Nothing supports the coding competency

The CBSE curriculum lists "block-based coding (e.g. Scratch)" as a Class 3-5 competency, and Class 4 goal CG-3 is to "demonstrate understanding of basic concepts of computers and knowledge of hardware and software". The Class 4 book has **no content for either**. The Class 7 teacher book says the approach should "apply multidisciplinary foundational knowledge on Coding, Data Analysis and Artificial Intelligence tools", but it **contains no coding at all**.

**So what:** schools are told to teach a coding competency with nothing to teach it from. **This is the clearest gap CodeSchool can fill.**

### 5. Projects are 40% of Class 7 time and have no materials

Class 7 time is 40 hours of CT, 20 hours of AI, and "40 hrs. total (20 hrs. allocated to each of the two required projects)". The teacher handbook has no project briefs, rubrics or examples. Assessment includes "Written Tests and Practical Examinations" and "Project presentations, assignments, and reflective journals".

**So what:** **ready-made, assessable project kits for Classes 6-8** cover the biggest unsupported block of time in the curriculum. They are also where coding and AI can be used for real. This should be tested as a top buying reason in interviews (see hypothesis H10 in the interview plan).

### 6. The AI part is thin, with no hands-on work

The Class 7 AI section is 4 chapters of exercises with answer keys:
1. AI Domains and Applications
2. AI in Industries
3. Data Visualisation and Analysis
4. Ethics and AI Bias Awareness

There are no explanations to teach from, no activities and no tools. Yet the learning outcomes include "Apply basic predictive approaches/techniques to a small dataset" and "create bar charts, line graphs and pie charts".

**So what:** **hands-on AI activities** are a second gap. Examples are sorting a small dataset into groups (classification), predicting a value from past data (regression), and making the charts. They have to work on the school's real equipment, which could be paper, a projector or a lab.

### 7. Assessment is continuous and nothing tracks it

Both books list continuous, competency-based assessment: written tests with CT puzzles, group activities and a **Teacher Observation Journal**. Neither gives a template, rubric or way to record it.

**So what:** a simple **observation journal and progress record**, mapped to the CBSE competencies and printable for report cards, is a small but concrete feature to test.

## What this means for CodeSchool

**Don't compete with the books. Sit on top of them.**

| CBSE gives (free) | CodeSchool could give (paid) |
|---|---|
| Puzzle banks by maths chapter | The same chapter skills as **levels**, with hints, easier on-ramps and auto-checked practice, on phone, projector or paper |
| No coding | **Block-coding levels** tied to each chapter's puzzles ("now you build it"), covering the Scratch competency |
| 2 unplugged activities per class | More unplugged activities with printable kits and a teacher script |
| No project materials | **Project kits for Classes 6-8** (the 40 hours): briefs, rubrics, examples and assessment |
| 9 pages of AI exercises | **Hands-on AI activities** on small datasets |
| A "Teacher Observation Journal" with no template | A **progress journal and dashboard** mapped to the CBSE competencies |

This sharpens the positioning in the research report: CodeSchool is **the easiest way to deliver the parts of CBSE CT&AI that CBSE didn't provide**, namely coding, projects, hands-on AI and assessment. It works alongside the free books instead of replacing them.
