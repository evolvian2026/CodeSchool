# Win booklists through principals, not ministries

For an April 2027 launch, CodeSchool's buyers are the principals and managements of private CBSE schools, not head offices or government systems. In a standalone private school, the maths coordinator (for Classes 3-5), the computer teacher (for the Classes 6-8 AI strand) and the academic coordinator propose a title after seeing specimens. The principal signs the booklist, and the trust or society signs off anything that costs money. Parents then pay through ordinary booksellers. Only three private chains have documented central control of books and curriculum: DAV, Podar and Orchids. They and other likely central buyers (Narayana, Sri Chaitanya, Lighthouse Learning, Manav Rachna) run 6-12-month head-office cycles, so they are 2027-28 pilot and 2028-29 adoption targets rather than January 2027 wins. Federated networks such as the DPS Society, Bharatiya Vidya Bhavan and Chinmaya can circulate an introduction, but their schools decide one by one. KVS, NVS, Sainik, AWES, RMS, AEES, NESTS and state schools have no booklist route. They take in outside programmes through head-office MoUs, CSR-funded foundations and teacher-training tie-ups, and they will lean first on CBSE's free CT&AI handbooks. The fastest multiplier is CBSE's own network: 250-plus Sahodaya clusters and about two dozen Centres of Excellence. CBSE has made "Computational Thinking and Understanding AI" its 2026-27 teacher-training theme. Each school's official email and phone are in its CBSE Mandatory Public Disclosure, not in SARAS. Outreach to those addresses is defensible under the DPDP Act's publicly-available-data exclusion if every record carries its source. Cold WhatsApp messages, unregistered telemarketing and any commission to a school are not defensible.

> **How to use the contacts in this report.** The repository is public, so this report lists only organisational contacts: designations, offices, official websites and office mailboxes or switchboards. It names no individual. Every contact was collected on or around 10 November 2026, and many come from secondary pages. **Re-check each one on the organisation's own website before using it.** Status labels: **Official** means seen on the organisation's own site or document during research. **Secondary** means it came from a member school's page, an older government page or a search snippet. **UNVERIFIED** means it came from a directory, an aggregator or background knowledge. **†** marks a mobile number or Gmail address that the organisation itself publishes as an office contact; treat it as organisational and re-verify it. This is research, not legal advice.

## Teachers propose, principals list, managements pay

CBSE has fixed who teaches the content, and that tells CodeSchool who proposes the title. The CT&AI curriculum gives Classes 3-5 to "Mathematics and other subject teachers". In Classes 6-8 it gives CT to subject teachers and AI literacy to computer teachers, who also assess the projects ([CBSE CT&AI curriculum, section 8.1](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/CurriculumMain27/CTAI_Pri_2026-27.pdf)). Its status is no longer in doubt. Circular Acad-15/2026 (1 April 2026) launched the curriculum for Classes III-VIII from 2026-27 and "advised" schools to implement it ([CBSE Acad-15/2026](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/Circulars/2026/15_Circular_2026.pdf)). Acad-18/2026 (9 April 2026) states that "the curriculum mandates CT + AI + Ethics + Unplugged Learning" and tells schools to use free resource books that follow the NCERT maths chapter order ([CBSE Acad-18/2026](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/Circulars/2026/18_Circular_2026.pdf)). The time budget is **50 hours a year in Classes 3-5 and 100 hours in Classes 6-8** (40 CT, 20 AI, 40 projects). All three facts were checked against the official PDFs ([CodeSchool CBSE verification, 5 Nov 2026](../cbse-verification.md)). A principal therefore isn't deciding whether to teach CT&AI; that is settled. The choice is whether to buy anything beyond the free handbooks.

In a standalone private school, the formal frame is the School Managing Committee under Chapter 8 of CBSE's Affiliation Bye-Laws. It works "subject to overall control of the Society/Trust/Company", with a separate Manager role for the trust's representative ([CBSE: School Management Committee](https://www.cbse.gov.in/cbsenew/sch_mgmt.html); the fetch was blocked, so this is from a search snippet). The working sequence is older and better documented. Publishers send specimen copies, teachers and heads of department choose a book for each subject, and the principal issues the list ([Millennium Post](https://www.millenniumpost.in/bengal/booklist-delay-leaves-semester-iv-students-uncertain-about-textbooks-632681); [Oswal Publishers](https://oswalpublishers.com/specimen-copy/)). Lists go out under the principal's name, with title, publisher and ISBN for each book ([DPS Bhopal 2025-26](https://dpsbhopal.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Book-List-25-26-I-to-XII-DPS-Bhopal.pdf); [EPS Delhi 2026-27](https://epsdelhi.com/pdf/BOOK%20LIST%202026-27.pdf)). Schools also post a "Declaration for List of Books as per section 2.4.7(B)" of the bye-laws ([Birla High School Mukundapur](https://bhs-mukundapur.birlahighschool.com/book-list-2025-26)). CBSE's 2024 revision reportedly requires that declaration to be signed by both the manager and the principal ([Careers360, Aug 2024](https://news.careers360.com/cbse-mandates-ncert-textbooks-for-classes-9-12-in-schools-revises-guidelines/amp)). That dual signature is the best evidence that management, not only the principal, owns the final list. Booklist economics are also a management matter: press reports allege publisher commissions of 15-40% paid to managements ([Tribune India](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/archive/chandigarh/how-nexus-between-private-publishers-schools-thrives-753952)). No source documents a formal booklist committee or a fixed calendar. The December-January window comes from upload dates (DPS lists posted in February for an April session) and from CodeSchool's own plan, which puts the adoption deadline at about **29 January 2027** ([launch plan](../../plan/April-2027-launch-plan.md)).

## Three chains buy centrally; most others still sell school by school

The evidence for central control is strongest in three places. The DAV Centre for Academic Excellence, "a wing of DAVCMC", prescribes books "uniformly, in DAV Public Schools PAN India" ([DAV CMC specimen notice](https://davcmc.net.in/HighlightNewsDoc/a3ce64fe-96cf-402f-bb75-b835b2b5f55d_Specimen%20Copy%20of%20Punjabi_Books__Atlas%20(1).pdf)). Its schools' syllabi cite DAV's own Publication Division titles ([DAV Bistupur Class VII](https://davbistupur.org/File/6704/FAV_J04817_2026-27_DAV%20BISTUPUR_SYLLABUS_CLASS-STD-VII.pdf)). Podar describes a "centrally designed curriculum", with a Chief Academics Officer function and a Purchase Department under a Chief Procurement Officer ([Podar Education Network](https://www.podareducation.org/about-podar-education-network)). Orchids runs in-house books, kits and a coding curriculum under K12 Techno Services ([Orchids DEL](https://www.orchidsinternationalschool.com/digital-enhanced-learning-del)). All three already publish their own content, so CodeSchool should approach them as a licensing or co-publishing partner, not as one more booklist title. Federated networks are the opposite case. DPS booklists differ from school to school ([DPS Bhopal](https://dpsbhopal.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Book-List-25-26-I-to-XII-DPS-Bhopal.pdf) and [DPS Rau](https://dpsrau.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/DPS-Rau-Book-List-2025-26-I-to-XII.pdf)), and the central body can only open doors. No chain except Podar publishes a vendor or partnership desk. For every other chain, the route is the general corporate inbox, asking for the academics head and procurement.

## 250-plus Sahodayas reach more CBSE principals than any trade show

Sahodaya School Complexes are CBSE-recognised voluntary clusters of 5-10 or more neighbouring schools. Their member principals elect a President, a Secretary and a Treasurer for 2-3-year terms, and complexes are expected to meet monthly and run an annual action plan. CBSE counted "more than 250" in 2022, and since then only complexes registered on its micro-site are recognised (the micro-site address in that circular, sahodayaschools.org, redirected to an unrelated domain on 10 November 2026, so do not use it) ([CBSE Circular Acad-40/2022](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/Circulars/2022/40_Circular_2022.pdf)). Some are large: Indore's reportedly has over 100 members ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/amp/indore/indore-schools-welcomes-new-changes-in-sahodaya-movement-of-cbse)). CBSE publishes no directory of office bearers. The route is therefore the Sahodaya's own website, or the office of the school that holds its presidency, reached through that school's official address. The 2026-27 training theme makes the offer obvious. CBSE's own plan is one-day district and regional workshops (6 CPD hours), half-day lead-school sessions and online Board sessions on "Computational Thinking and Understanding AI" ([CBSE CT&AI webinar slides, 28 Apr 2026](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/CurriculumMain27/CTAI_WebinarPPT_2026-27.pdf)). A free, CBSE-aligned teacher workshop that maps chapter by chapter to the CBSE handbooks is what a Sahodaya secretary needs to fill that slot. It also puts the specimen in front of the maths coordinators and computer teachers who propose titles. It must never imply CBSE endorsement, because CBSE stresses that the curriculum is "without dependence on any platform" ([Acad-18/2026](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/Circulars/2026/18_Circular_2026.pdf)).

The other bodies differ in reach and fit. NPSC is all-CBSE and Delhi-centred, and it co-hosted the 2019 national Sahodaya conference. NISA reaches budget schools at scale but mostly on state boards. The state management associations are mainly fighting fee regulation and RTE dues in 2026, which makes them poor sales channels and better forums for a compliance-framed talk.

## Government systems enter through MoUs, CSR and training, not booklists

The Ministry of Education's CT&AI rollout from Class 3 is being run "in close coordination with CBSE, NCERT, KVS, NVS and state education departments" ([Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/students-learn-ai-computational-thinking-from-class-3-starting-2026-27-cbse-ncert-frame-curriculum/amp)). The central systems helped frame the programme and will use the free CBSE handbooks first. The documented precedents for outside programmes are society-level MoUs, mostly CSR-funded. NESTS renewed an MoU with Learning Links Foundation to run Amazon Future Engineer (computer science and AI) across EMRS from April 2026 to March 2028 ([Devdiscourse](https://www.devdiscourse.com/article/law-order/3930890-nests-expands-digital-education-initiative-across-emrs-network)). NVS signed with IBM for Vigyan Jyoti as part of a ministry batch of 106 MoUs ([MoE/PIB](https://dsel.education.gov.in/sites/default/files/update/PIB1944173.pdf)). In the states, AI and coding have arrived through no-cost or CSR partnerships rather than open tenders. Examples are Telangana with Amazon in July 2026 ([Elets](https://digitallearning.eletsonline.com/2026/07/amazon-telangana-partner-to-bring-ai-education-to-government-schools/)), Samagra Shiksha Assam's six MoUs on 4 September 2026 ([Sentinel Assam](https://www.sentinelassam.com/topheadlines/ai-push-in-assam-schools-ssa-signs-six-mous-for-technology-enabled-learning)), and Andhra Pradesh with IIT Madras and Bodhan AI ([Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/andhra-pradesh-government-inks-7-agreements-at-ai-summit-focusing-on-education-skilling-nielit-iit-madras-ibm-stem)). Companies book this work under Schedule VII item (ii), "promoting education", of the CSR rules ([Cisco India CSR](https://www.cisco.com/c/dam/global/en_in/about/docs/india-cash-grant-projects.pdf)). For a for-profit content company, the practical door is to supply content and training to a CSR-funded implementing NGO that holds the MoU. Kerala is the exception that proves the rule. KITE builds its own curriculum and distributed 29,000 open-hardware robotics kits itself, which makes Kerala a co-development prospect at best ([NewKerala](https://www.newkerala.com/news/o/29000-robotic-kits-distributed-state-run-kerala-schools-333)).

## Each school's contacts sit in its CBSE disclosure, not in SARAS

SARAS (saras.cbse.gov.in) is the authoritative list of CBSE-affiliated schools. A live school record shows the affiliation number, address, website, head of institution, school type and status, affiliation period and managing trust. It has **no email or phone field** ([SARAS record](https://saras.cbse.gov.in/SARAS/AffiliatedList/AfflicationDetails/2730312)). Official contact details are in the school's own **Mandatory Public Disclosure** (Appendix IX). Schools must post it prominently on their websites, with a visible home-page icon, under CBSE Circular 3 of 5 March 2021 ([CBSE Circular 3](https://saras.cbse.gov.in/saras/Circulars/Circular3.pdf)). SARAS now generates the document in a standard "SARAS 6.0/7.0" format ([example, May 2026](https://globalindianschool.org/dubai/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Mandatory-Disclosure-Details-_-SARAS-7.0-2.pdf)). CBSE revised Appendix IX in January 2026 ([Circular 1/2026 copy](https://psbbmillenniumschool.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/circular-1.pdf)) and set a 15 February 2026 deadline for schools to complete their disclosures "or face action" ([Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/cbse-schools-class-10-12-results-fees-student-teacher-details-online-by-february-15-2026-affiliation-rules-public-disclosures/amp)). Compliance was about 40% complete in one earlier analysis ([Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/how-many-schools-violate-cbse-regulations-on-mandatory-disclosures)), so many records will need the school's own contact page instead. The exact 2026 field list could not be read, because the circular PDF is a scanned image.

The other sources rank below the disclosure. CISCE's official locator (about 3,244 schools) shows head and admin contacts, though it refused automated fetches ([CISCE locator](https://devlocate.cisce.org/?page=22)). Each KV has an official site at &lt;name&gt;.kvs.ac.in that hosts its CBSE disclosure ([KV Kutra](https://kutra.kvs.ac.in/sites/default/files/CBSE%20AFFLIATION%20CERTFICATE.pdf)). Each JNV has a page under navodaya.gov.in/nvs/nvs-school/&lt;district&gt;/. UDISE+ is the source to avoid. It collects each school's phone and email ([UDISE+ DCF 2023-24](https://educationforallinindia.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/data-capture-format-2023-24-DOSEL-MoE-India.pdf)), but there is no evidence it publishes them. Contacts lifted from a logged-in portal would not be "made publicly available", so they fall outside the DPDP exclusion discussed in the next section. No SARAS bulk export or terms of use were found, so build the list by hand or with light, polite tooling, and ask the lawyer before scraping anything.

Keep the prospect list **private**. The repository's `.gitignore` excludes `*.private.*` and `docs/research/interviews/`. On 10 November 2026, `git check-ignore -v docs/research/prospects.private.csv` confirmed the rule matches. Use a file such as `docs/research/prospects/launch-schools.private.csv`, run `git check-ignore -v` on it before the first save, and never paste rows into a public report, a commit message or an issue. The record should stay at institution level:

| Field | Rule |
|---|---|
| School name, affiliation no., city, district | From SARAS |
| School type, classes offered, affiliation valid to | From SARAS and the disclosure; drop lapsed schools |
| Official school email, office phone, website | From the disclosure or the school's contact page; prefer office@, principal@ or info@ at the school's domain |
| Head's designation | Record the role; record a name only if the disclosure publishes it and you need it for a letter |
| Source URL and date captured | **Required for every contact**: this is the DPDP provenance record |
| Segment | Standalone, federated chain, Sahodaya member (and which Sahodaya), central chain |
| Contact log, opt-in status, suppression flag | One line per touch; any "stop" moves the school to suppression permanently |
| Excluded | Personal mobiles, personal email addresses, purchased lists, anything from UDISE+ or a logged-in portal, and interview notes (keep those in the ignored interviews folder) |

## Email the school office; never cold-WhatsApp or pay a commission

**Data protection.** Section 3(c)(ii) of the DPDP Act excludes personal data made public by the person themselves, or by someone under a legal obligation to publish it ([India Briefing, 10 Sep 2026](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/india-dpdp-act-publicly-available-personal-data-46899.html/)). A principal's official contact in a CBSE-mandated disclosure plausibly fits the second limb. No court has tested this, commentators warn that reuse for commercial profiling is unsettled ([Law School Policy Review, Jan 2026](https://lawschoolpolicyreview.com/2026/01/13/publicly-available-data-under-the-dpdp-act-the-limits-of-exemptions-in-ai-driven-processing/)), and firms are told to document how each item became public. The DPDP Rules were notified in November 2025. Their main obligations (notice, security, erasure and breach reporting) start on **13 or 14 May 2027**; sources differ on the date ([Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/amp/digital/breaking-govt-notifies-final-dpdp-rules-sets-staggered-rollout-with-key-obligations-for-data-fiduciaries-84201.htm)). The CRM built in December will still be running then. The source-URL rule, a retention limit (12-24 months after last contact is a suggested policy, not a legal requirement) and a permanent suppression list should be in place from the start.

**Calls and SMS.** TRAI's February 2025 amendments to TCCCPR require commercial senders to register on the telcos' DLT platform. Promotional calls must use the **140** series and service calls the **1600** series, and telemarketing from ordinary 10-digit numbers is banned ([TRAI PR 11/2025](https://cms.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-02/PR_No.11of2025.pdf)). Five complaints in ten days can bar a sender's outgoing services for 15 days. A repeat brings one year's disconnection and blacklisting ([FoneArena](https://fonearena.com/blog/?p=446114)). Whether this covers B2B calls is before the Delhi High Court in IndiaMART's challenge. Notice was issued on 23 December 2025, and no outcome was found ([Bar & Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/news/delhi-high-court-seeks-centres-response-to-indiamarts-plea-against-spam-call-and-message-regulations)). Assume it applies. Keep phone use to scheduling calls with school offices that have already replied, and route any bulk SMS or scripted calling through DLT-registered infrastructure.

**Email and WhatsApp.** India has no standalone anti-spam statute ([iPleaders](https://blog.ipleaders.in/overview-laws-spamming-india/)). Email to official school addresses is the lawful default, under DPDP and consumer-protection limits. Good practice is an authenticated company domain (SPF, DKIM and DMARC), the company's name and postal address, a factual subject line that does not suggest CBSE endorsement, low volumes and a one-click opt-out that is honoured permanently. WhatsApp needs "opt-in permission from the recipient" before any business-initiated message. Messages outside the 24-hour window must use approved templates, and an organisation-wide ban is the penalty for unauthorised messaging at scale ([WhatsApp Business Messaging Policy](https://whatsappbusiness.com/policy/)). Use WhatsApp only after a school ticks a box on a CodeSchool form or replies "yes, WhatsApp me". Keep that record.

**No inducements.** Commissions now carry criminal exposure. In Jabalpur, a 2024 "books commission" case led to FIRs against 51 people, and in April 2026 the Madhya Pradesh High Court refused to quash them ([SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2026/04/29/mp-hc-no-relief-to-private-schools-for-unauthorized-fee-hike-and-book-selling/)). Delhi barred schools on 1 April 2026 from forcing purchases from specific vendors ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/india/story/delhi-govt-orders-private-schools-to-stop-forcing-students-to-buy-books-uniforms-from-specific-vendors-523625-2026-04-02)). CBSE told schools in 2017 to stop "coercing parents" into buying from selected vendors ([CBSE circular, 19 Apr 2017](https://www.cbse.gov.in/cbsenew/Examination_Circular/2017/30_CIRCULAR.pdf)). CodeSchool's ground rule is already written into its distributor brief: no commissions, gifts or donations to schools or staff ([distributor enquiry](../supplier-quotes/03-distributor-terms-enquiry.md)). The offer should be a published price, a written list of what the school receives (kits, teacher books, orientation), and at least two booksellers plus online. Specimens should be modest and logged by school, to the coordinator or teacher, with the principal's knowledge.

The lawyer should answer these questions before outreach begins in December:

| # | Question for an Indian lawyer | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Does a principal's contact in an Appendix IX disclosure fall within DPDP s.3(c)(ii), given that the school's duty exists to inform parents, not vendors? | Decides whether the prospect list needs a lawful basis and a notice from May 2027 |
| 2 | Does compiling and reusing such contacts for sales count as purpose drift that takes them outside the exclusion? | Aggregation risk |
| 3 | Do TCCCPR 2025 rules cover one-to-one calls from a staff mobile to a school landline, and what happened in IndiaMART v. TRAI after March 2026? | Sets the phone policy |
| 4 | Are free specimens, a free pilot, free kits for adopting sections, or a transparent institutional discount lawful under CBSE bye-laws, state fee Acts and the conduct rules for government school staff? Where is the line? | Separates normal publisher practice from an inducement |
| 5 | Do the SARAS and CISCE terms permit systematic collection, and does the IT Act's computer-access provision limit tooling? | List-building method |
| 6 | From May 2027, what notice, retention and erasure duties apply to prospect data held as a Data Fiduciary? | CRM design |

## A five-wave sequence fits the 29 January booklist close

The interview weeks set the first constraint. The plan promises schools that interviews from 16 November to 11 December are research only. Interviewers can ask at the end whether a school would like a specimen follow-up, and they should record the answer, but they should not pitch. Sales outreach waits for specimens (15 December) and the go/no-go check (18 December) ([launch plan](../../plan/April-2027-launch-plan.md)). The second constraint is that only the private-school rows of the decision map can adopt by 29 January. Everything aimed at chains' head offices and government is planting for 2027-28 pilots.

A target of 10 or more adopting schools by 29 January, the plan's own early-warning line, is achievable only through waves 2-4. That makes the Sahodaya workshops the decisive bet. One well-run workshop puts specimens before the proposers in 5-100 schools at once, inside a CBSE-sanctioned training theme. A cold email reaches one office that may never forward it. The IPSC conclave (4-6 January) is useful for credibility with elite heads but too small for volume. The EducationWorld conclave in October comes after booklists close.

## Conclusion

The research turns the question of contacts into a question of timing. The documented gatekeepers for an April 2027 booklist are a few thousand principals and managements. CBSE has handed CodeSchool the reason to meet them, a mandated curriculum with a training theme, and the address book, a disclosure every school must publish. The bodies with the grandest reach (KVS, NVS, the state Samagra Shiksha offices and the large central chains) are the slowest. They buy through MoUs and CSR rather than specimens, and their 2027-28 value is the evidence they would lend CodeSchool, not this season's revenue. CodeSchool should spend this winter in Sahodaya training rooms and school offices, and send head-office letters only once the January-March pilot gives them something to say.

The less obvious implication is that compliance is now a selling point, not just a cost. With MP's commission FIRs, Delhi's vendor directive and DPDP duties starting in May 2027, a principal who adopts a new title is taking a small personal risk. A published price, open-market availability, no commission, a logged specimen trail and a provenance-tracked contact list are exactly what a cautious principal or a government office needs to see. Building the private prospect list correctly in November is the first piece of that offer, not paperwork that precedes it.
