# Indian schools teach thinking before code

"Around 4th standard" is right about when many Indian children first meet Scratch. It is wrong as a statement of policy. Private-school computer textbooks introduce Scratch at Class 3, and Kerala's state ICT textbooks put Scratch games at Class 4. Official policy, though, has four different starting points. NEP 2020 starts unplugged computational thinking (CT) at the Foundational Stage. CBSE formally starts CT at Class 3 from 2026-27, and its framework also lists block coding (Scratch) as a Class 3-5 competency. NEP places "activities involving coding" in Classes 6-8. Graded, examined text-based programming appears only as an elective: from Class 9 in ICSE (Java), but not until Class 11 in CBSE (Python, in Computer Science 083 and Informatics Practices 065). CBSE's Class 9-10 Computer Applications teaches office tools and HTML, not programming. Internationally, Class 4 is late for computational thinking: England, Australia, Finland and the US model standards start at ages 5-7. It is typical for formal block coding in East Asia, such as Singapore's upper-primary Code for Fun and Korea's Grades 5-6 requirement. Almost every system switches to a text language at ages 11-14. A level-based structure tailored to each grade is feasible and fits the evidence. In programming education, "levels" are the one game mechanic with a significant measured learning effect (d ≈ 0.4). The major frameworks already group content in multi-year bands. The recommended design is five bands across Classes 1-12, made up of mastery-gated levels that are separate from the child's class, with placement tests and "Express" catch-up tracks. On the market side, parent-paid live-class coding collapsed. BYJU's paid about $200M for Tynker and sold it for about $2.2M. Companies selling bundled curricula to schools, such as LEAD Group (Rs 387 Cr revenue in FY26), earn steady revenue. CBSE's new Class 3-8 CT and AI curriculum makes CT something schools must deliver, mostly through non-specialist teachers. The product should therefore be sold to schools, Android-first and multilingual, and positioned as "CT and AI literacy, with coding as the tool." It needs syllabus mapping, teacher tooling and assessment. Policy is still unsettled: CBSE "advises" rather than requires the Class 3-8 curriculum, and it has already deferred the Class 9 modules.

> **Verification update, 5 November 2026.** The CBSE claims in this report were rechecked against the full official circulars and syllabi; see [CBSE facts checked against official documents](../cbse-verification.md). Two corrections are made below. First, CBSE Computer Applications 165 (Classes 9-10) contains **no programming**, so CBSE's first programming subject is in Class 11. Second, the CT&AI curriculum document **names no AI tool**, although the Class 8 handbooks use Teachable Machine for three hands-on labs (the earlier draft was right about the handbooks). Also confirmed: CBSE's Classes 3-5 CT is maths-puzzle work tied to the NCERT maths chapters, and explicit if-then logic first appears as a **Class 7** outcome. A follow-up [review of the free CBSE handbooks](../cbse-handbook-review.md) found that they are printed puzzle banks, chapter by chapter alongside NCERT maths, with **no coding and no project materials in any of the 12 books (Classes 3-8)**. The Classes 6-8 student books teach AI concepts in text, but the teacher books carry only answer keys, and only Class 8 has hands-on AI (three Teachable Machine labs). That points to where CodeSchool can add value: coding levels, project kits, hands-on AI and assessment.

Paid market-research figures are labelled **low-quality estimates**. Recommendations and design proposals are labelled **hypotheses** or **recommendations**.*

## Class 4 is when children meet Scratch, not when policy starts coding

The question "when does programming start?" has four different answers in India, depending on whether you mean thinking, blocks, policy "coding" or examined programming. Treating them as one thing is the main source of confusion.

The first answer is **unplugged computational thinking**. NEP 2020 para 4.25 says "mathematics and computational thinking will be given increased emphasis throughout the school years, starting with the foundational stage, through a variety of innovative methods, including the regular use of puzzles and games." The Foundational Stage covers ages 3-8, which means preschool through Class 2 ([NEP 2020](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/userfiles/NEP_Final_English_0.pdf), official). The first binding curriculum is CBSE's **Computational Thinking and AI curriculum for Classes III-VIII**, launched by Circular Acad-15/2026 on 1 April 2026 for the 2026-27 session ([CBSE Acad-15/2026](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/Circulars/2026/15_Circular_2026.pdf), official). For Classes 3-5 it embeds CT in Mathematics and The World Around Us. It is worth **50 hours a year**, taught through workbooks. In Classes 6-8 it rises to **100 hours a year**: 40 hours of advanced CT, 20 hours of introductory AI and about 40 hours of interdisciplinary projects ([CBSE CT&AI curriculum](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/CurriculumMain27/CTAI_Pri_2026-27.pdf), official). Secondary summaries of the CBSE handbooks describe the Class 3-5 activities as unplugged. Examples are a Caesar cipher in Class 3, "Coin Flip Magic" parity tricks and a floor sorting network in Class 4, and finding a fake weight among nine with a balance in Class 5 ([EdTechReview](https://www.edtechreview.in/trends-insights/insights/cbse-ai-computational-thinking-curriculum-classes-3-8/), secondary).

The second answer is **block coding**. The same CBSE document lists a Class 3-5 computer competency covering "parts of a computer, input/output devices, file management, internet safety, educational software, and **block-based coding (e.g. Scratch)**" ([CBSE CT&AI curriculum](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/CurriculumMain27/CTAI_Pri_2026-27.pdf), official). Some secondary explainers say the framework names no coding tool for Classes 3-8. The research team's reading of the official PDF contradicts them, so the official text wins. Block coding is still a single competency inside a mostly unplugged, maths-embedded framework. It is not a programming course, and it depends on devices that many schools lack. Actual practice runs ahead of policy. Orange Education's Touchpad Class 3 books include "Stepwise Thinking & Scratch Programming" ([Touchpad Prime Class 3](https://www.storytel.com/tv/books/touchpad-prime-ver-2-1-class-3-2579557), secondary). Tekie's ICSE coding books use Scratch 3.0 in Grades 4-5 ([Tekie ICSE Grade 4](https://issuu.com/tekie-app/docs/tekie_icse_coding_grade_4_p1_ebook), secondary). Kerala's KITE textbooks, released in August 2025, introduce logical games at Class 3 and simple Scratch games at Class 4 ([Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/kerala-launches-new-ict-textbooks-for-classes-3-to-10-integrating-coding-animation-and-music), news). The founder's "Class 4" belief is really describing this layer.

The third answer is **"coding" as policy uses the word**. NEP para 4.25 ends: "**Activities involving coding will be introduced in Middle Stage**", which is Classes 6-8 ([NEP 2020](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/userfiles/NEP_Final_English_0.pdf), official). The word is "activities", not a subject with set hours. NEP is a policy rather than a syllabus, so it binds no board directly. CBSE's 2026-27 Classes 6-8 strand applies "foundational knowledge on **coding**, data analysis, and AI tools." Python appears only in the curriculum's rationale and is not a prescribed Class 3-8 topic ([CBSE CT&AI curriculum](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/CurriculumMain27/CTAI_Pri_2026-27.pdf), official). Before that, CBSE offered an optional 12-hour Coding module for Classes 6-8 from 2021-22, built with Microsoft ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/latest/trends/story/cbse-data-science-coding-school-curriculum-from-2021-22-298001-2021-06-04), news, older). In 2023 it added 33 optional skill modules for Classes 6-8, including coding and AI ([Onmanorama](https://onmanorama.com/career-and-campus/top-news/2023/05/04/cbse-introduces-33-new-subjects-for-classes-6-8.html), news, older).

The fourth answer is **graded, text-based programming**. It arrives late, and only as an elective. In CBSE it doesn't arrive until **Class 11**. CBSE's Classes 9-10 options include Computer Applications 165 (an optional subject) and the skill subjects IT 402, AI 417 and Data Science 419. The 2026-27 Computer Applications syllabus, however, contains **no programming language**: Class 9 covers IT basics, cyber safety and office tools, and Class 10 covers networking, HTML/CSS and cyber ethics ([CA 165 Class IX](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/CurriculumMain27/SecPart1/Computer_Applications_SecP1IX_2026-27.pdf), [Class X](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/CurriculumMain27/SecPart1/Computer_Applications_SecP1X_2026-27.pdf), official). Python first appears in Classes 11-12, in Computer Science 083 and Informatics Practices 065. CS 083 Class 11 gives 45 of its 70 theory marks to "Computational Thinking and Programming" in Python, and Class 12 adds functions, files, stacks and SQL. IP 065 covers Python, NumPy, Pandas, Matplotlib and MySQL ([CS 083](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/CurriculumMain27/SecPart2/Computer_Science_SecP2_2026-27.pdf), [IP 065](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/CurriculumMain27/SecPart2/Informatics_Practices_SecP2_2026-27.pdf), official). AI 843 and Data Science 844 are also offered ([CBSE 844 curriculum](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/CurriculumMain27/SrSec/844-DATASCIENCE.pdf), official). The content of skill subjects AI 417 and IT 402 was not checked. **No CBSE programming subject is compulsory for every student at any class.** Vocational education is compulsory in Classes 9-10, but a student can satisfy it without choosing a computing subject ([CBSE Acad-70/2026](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/Circulars/2026/70_Circular_2026.pdf), official). ICSE's formal programming is Computer Applications in Java with BlueJ, an elective from Class 9 ([Shaalaa syllabus](https://www.shaalaa.com/syllabus/cisce-computer-applications_3279), secondary).

Second, **devices limit what is possible.** UDISE+ 2024-25 shows only **64.7%** of schools with "computer facilities". A "computer facility" is not the same as a working lab with enough devices per pupil. This is why CBSE and NCERT designed early CT to work unplugged. The block-coding competency on paper is a privilege of private schools in practice.

## Peer systems start thinking at five and switch to text by fourteen

Compared with other countries, "around Class 4" is late for computational thinking, on time for structured block coding in Asian systems, and early for text. The Anglo and Nordic systems start algorithms at ages 5-7. East Asian systems formally start in Grades 3-6. Almost everyone moves to a text language at ages 11-14. Two patterns matter for product design. First, the sequence is the same everywhere: **unplugged and tangible work at ages 5-7, blocks at ages 7-11, text at ages 11-14, exam-level CS at 14-16**. England's statutory requirement for a textual language in KS3 and Australia's switch to a general-purpose language in Years 7-8 show where the block-to-text boundary sits. India's Class 6-8 "coding" band falls in the same age range, but India gives it no text requirement. Indian Class 1 starts at about age 6, so Indian Class *n* corresponds roughly to England Year *n+1*. Class 3 matches England's Year 4 (KS2), and Class 6 matches Year 7, the start of KS3. Second, **AI education is now being layered on top of CS everywhere, and it is being reversed in places.** The UAE mandates AI from KG in public schools. Beijing requires eight AI hours a year at every grade. Singapore bolted AI modules onto Code for Fun. Korea, on the other hand, stripped its AI textbooks of official status within six months. India's CBSE deferral of Class 9 CT & AI belongs to the same pattern: AI mandates are announced fast and adjusted on the fly. Indian Class 4 Scratch is roughly in step with Singapore and China. A Class 1 CT strand would put India in line with England, Australia, Finland and the US.

## Five bands and about thirty levels can span Classes 1-12

A level-based structure is not only feasible; it is the best-supported way to apply game mechanics to programming. Costa's 2023 multi-level meta-analysis of game concepts in programming learning found a significant gain from gamification (**Cohen's d ≈ 0.4**), largely due to **levels**. Badges, points, avatars and leaderboards showed no significant effect on learning ([Costa 2023](https://repositorio.iscte-iul.pt/bitstream/10071/29213/1/article_95533.pdf)). The major frameworks already think in bands rather than single years. The 2017 CSTA standards use levels 1A to 3B across 2-3-year spans (background knowledge, [CSTA](https://csteachers.org/k12standards/)). The 2026 revision uses a PK/K band, one band per grade for Grades 1-5, a middle-school band and high-school specialty tiers ([CSTA PK-12](https://csteachers.org/pk12standards/)). Bebras sets task difficulty in two-year age groups ([Bebras USA](https://bebraschallenge.org/)). Code.org's six grade-aligned courses, A-F, for K-5 come with "Express" versions that let older beginners cover the same concepts quickly ([Code.org CSF](https://code.org/en-US/curriculum/computer-science-fundamentals); Express is background knowledge, [Code.org Express](https://studio.code.org/s/express)). The structure the founder wants therefore exists, in pieces, in the most widely used curricula in the world.

The real developmental seams fall at about **age 7-8**, from structured or tangible tools to open-ended blocks, and at about **age 10-12**, from concrete to abstract (variables, parameters, syntax). They do not fall at every grade boundary. A meta-analysis found that structured virtual and tangible coding helped preschoolers and first graders, while unstructured Scratch-type coding paid off for older students ([Computers & Education 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0360131523002385)). ScratchJr is designed for ages 5-7 and Scratch for ages 8-16 ([ScratchJr](https://www.scratchjr.org/about/info); [Scratch](https://scratch.mit.edu/about)). No controlled study ties a particular concept, such as variables, to a particular age. The age placements below rest on curriculum consensus and developmental theory, not on readiness experiments.

### The recommended framework (recommendation)

The concept order follows the sequence shared by Code.org, CSTA and the Raspberry Pi Foundation's curriculum: sequencing, then loops, events, conditionals, nested and while loops, variables, functions with parameters, lists and data, and finally text syntax, modular programs and algorithms. Data, safety and networks, and AI and society run as parallel strands, not as levels. Level counts and hour figures are design hypotheses to test in pilots. They are not taken from any source.

Band A deliberately sits below CBSE's Class 3 start. That is commercially awkward, because no board requires it. It fits NEP's Foundational Stage intent and international practice, and it gives the app a pipeline into Class 3. It should be cheap to deliver, mostly printable and teacher-led, and should not be the paid core of the product.

### Levels decouple from grade through placement and Express tracks

Each class gets a **default entry level and an age-appropriate "skin"**: themes, reading load and interface style. The level a child is on comes from what they can show, not which class they are in. The architecture the research supports has five parts. First, a **10-15 minute adaptive placement check** when a child joins. Second, **"challenge to skip" tests** at each level boundary. Third, **mastery gating**, at roughly 80-90% of check items, with spaced review of earlier levels. Fourth, a **teacher override**, so a class can stay together on a period plan. Fifth, three **Express tracks** modelled on Code.org's. A Junior Express lets a Class 4 or 5 newcomer cover Band A in a few sessions. A Middle Express lets a Class 6-8 beginner cover the core of Bands A and B in about a term, presented in a Class 7 style rather than a Class 2 one. A Senior Express takes a Class 9 beginner through blocks briefly and straight into the Python or Java track. No empirical study of placement tests for mixed-ability school programming classes was found, so the placement design has to rely on general testing practice and pilot data. The evidence suggests simple per-strand mastery tracking and good hints will do more than sophisticated knowledge-tracing models. An implementation warning applies as well: in Cognitive Tutor Algebra, students were often moved on without reaching mastery, because teachers faced pacing pressure ([Mastery Learning in Practice](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.08616)).

Every level should share one lesson template that a non-specialist teacher can run. The template, built on PRIMM with a Parsons bridge, has five steps: **predict and run a worked example, investigate it with tracing questions, modify it with guided changes, solve a Parsons or fill-in-the-blank problem, then make something open-ended.** Steps one to four can be auto-graded. Step five protects against puzzle-only progression. A district study of Code.org in Baltimore found that completing *extra* lessons and additional CS activities was linked to better outcomes, but completing the required grade-level lessons was not ([Outlier Research](https://outlier.uchicago.edu/TimeforCS/findings)). That is an argument for letting fast learners run ahead on self-paced levels, which this design allows naturally.

They also answer the backlash against "teaching young kids to code on screens" that hurt the pandemic-era market ([afaqs](https://www.afaqs.com/news/mktg/coding-for-kids-how-this-edtech-sub-segment-became-a-pandemic-era-star), news, older).

## Schools, not parents, pay for coding in India

### B2C live classes burned out; school bundles survived

The Indian evidence points one way. **WhiteHat Jr** peaked in 2020 at about 150,000 paid students and 40,000 one-to-one classes a day ([afaqs](https://www.afaqs.com/news/mktg/coding-for-kids-how-this-edtech-sub-segment-became-a-pandemic-era-star), news, older). It accounted for **26.73% of BYJU's FY21 loss of Rs 1,118.25 Cr** ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/byjus-mulls-closing-down-its-loss-making-entity-whitehat-jr), news, older). It then went through layoffs, a reported rebrand and lawsuits. User complaints from 2023-24 describe unpaid refunds and EMI payments still being deducted after classes stopped ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/whitehatjr.com?page=4), unverified user posts). No formal shutdown announcement was found, so "effectively dormant" is an inference, not a confirmed fact. BYJU's itself entered insolvency in July 2024 ([Business Standard](https://bsppd.business-standard.com/companies/news/nclt-admits-insolvency-plea-against-byju-s-rejects-firm-s-arbitration-plea-124071600021_1.html), news). Its US coding asset **Tynker sold for about $2.2M against roughly $200M paid** ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2025/06/edtech-byju-us-epic-tynker-sold-pennies-dollar), news). That is the clearest measure of what a parent-facing kids-coding brand is worth at exit. **BrightChamps** reported FY23 revenue of Rs 20.6 Cr against a net loss of Rs 159.5 Cr ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/company/brightchamps/financials/), news, older). Indian edtech funding fell from $4.1B in 2021 to about $321M in 2023 ([Business Standard](https://business-standard.com/companies/news/india-s-edtech-sector-sees-30-drop-in-funding-to-215-mn-amid-slowdown-124090600742_1.html), news). That removed the capital that paid-acquisition models depended on.

School-facing businesses tell the opposite story. **LEAD Group**, which sells an integrated curriculum and platform to affordable private schools and has added coding and AI, reported FY26 operating revenue of **Rs 387 Cr** ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2026/08/edtech-lead-fy26-revenue-rises-387-crore-losses-narrow-over-20-percent), news). It had earlier reported EBITDA breakeven and **Rs 415 Cr ARR** for AY 2025-26 ([Tribune/ANI](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/business/lead-group-achieves-ebitda-breakeven-secures-arr-of-rs-415-cr-for-ay-25-26), news). Eupheus Learning reported about Rs 225 Cr in FY24 ([YourStory](https://ts.yourstory.com/2025/02/eupheus-learning-loss-narrows-to-rs-9-crore-edyech-platform), news) and Extramarks Rs 233 Cr ([Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/fintrackr/extramarks-losses-drop-by-85-to-rs-48-cr-in-fy24-revenue-slips-37-8713664), news). In all three, coding is a feature inside a larger school bundle, not a standalone product. The steelman for B2C is real. Codingal holds a Trustpilot score of about 4.8 ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/codingal.com)), and PlanetSpark grew to Rs 81.2 Cr in FY25 ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/company/planetspark/financials)). Both, however, depend on live teachers costing **INR 700-2,833 per class** ([Codingal](https://www.codingal.com/en-us/llms-full.txt); [BrightChamps](https://brightchamps.com/en-in/courses/coding-classes-for-kids/beginner-coding-course-grade-1)). That price excludes most Indian families, which is why several players moved toward US and Middle East customers. An app-only B2C product avoids the teacher cost, but it competes directly with free tools.

The recommendation is **B2B2C**. Sell to private CBSE and ICSE schools for a per-student annual fee collected through school fees. Add a free parent view and an optional paid home-practice tier. Treat government sales (B2G) as a second stage, run through Atal Tinkering Lab and ICT-lab vendors on the government's GeM procurement portal. B2G money is real: Samagra Shiksha allows up to Rs 6.4 lakh one-time plus Rs 2.4 lakh a year per school for ICT labs ([DoSEL](https://dsel.education.gov.in/scheme/ict-samagra), official). But it arrives in lumps and is tied to grants. The same DoSEL page lists 120,614 ICT labs sanctioned but only 78,498 working, and 82,120 smart classrooms sanctioned but only 9,833 working. School sales follow the academic calendar: book lists close around December-January for an April start ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/article/specials/publishing-battle-over-textbooks-heats-up-117010901411_1.html), news, older). A product that is not ready to sell by December misses a full year.

### Free basics push the paid value into alignment, assessment and teachers

Nobody can charge for basic block coding. Code.org claims 155M+ students reached ([Code.org](https://code.org/about)), Scratch is free, and CBSE distributes free CT & AI student and teacher books ([CBSE Acad-18/2026](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/Circulars/2026/18_Circular_2026.pdf), official). Global school licences anchor the low end: Minecraft Education costs **$5.04 per user per year** for institutions ([Minecraft Edu FAQ](https://edusupport.minecraft.net/hc/en-us/articles/360047119092-FAQ-Availability-Pricing-and-Licensing)), and Khanmigo for Teachers is free in English in 49 countries ([Microsoft](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/education/blog/?p=6378)). The paid value therefore has to sit in the gaps schools struggle with, and the research documents six of them. The first is **mapping to the syllabus**: a principal needs to justify the purchase against CBSE CT & AI, CISCE and state syllabi. A vendor needs no CBSE "affiliation", which applies only to schools; this is an inference, since no rule on vendor approval was found. The second is **assessment and reporting** for a new subject that is internally assessed and has no board exam. The third is **support for non-specialist teachers**: period-by-period scripts, answer keys, notes on common misconceptions and a class dashboard showing who is stuck. The fourth is **Android-first, offline delivery**. ASER 2024 found about 90% of rural 14-16-year-olds have a smartphone at home, but only about 31% own one ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/india/education/in-rural-india-82-2-teens-aged-14-16-know-how-to-use-smartphone-but-only-57-use-it-for-studies/2467560), news). The fifth is **regional languages**: one study cited by Tinkerly says only 17% of children are in English-medium schools against 49% in Hindi-medium ones ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2021/11/startup-bharat-jaipur-based-tinkerly-coding), news, older; the figure was not independently verified). The sixth is **a structured bridge from blocks to text** for Classes 6-8, which neither the CBSE books nor Scratch provides.

Competitors each cover part of this. Next Education claims 12,000+ private schools ([Next Education](https://nexteducation.in/about-us.html), company claim). STEMpedia's PictoBlox is used in Kerala's textbooks ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/india/kerala-includes-artificial-intelligence-ai-learning-in-school-textbooks/2107933)). CuriousJr took the phone-first, vernacular route ([CXO Today](https://cxotoday.com/press-release/curiousjr-initiates-a-new-plan-for-coding-in-vernacular-languages/), older). None of the sources shows anyone combining full Class 1-12 level progression, CBSE CT & AI mapping, teacher tooling and multilingual offline delivery.

### Positioning and pricing hypotheses

Position the product as **"CT and AI literacy, with coding as the tool."** Sell it to schools as the easiest way to deliver and evidence CBSE's CT & AI curriculum. Sell it to parents as visible progress tied to the school syllabus. Avoid outcome promises. ASCI found 5 of 7 WhiteHat Jr ads it examined in potential violation, including the fictional "Wolf Gupta" child-prodigy ads ([Scroll](https://scroll.in/article/979338/ever-since-its-acquisition-by-byjus-whitehat-jr-has-been-in-the-news-for-all-the-wrong-reasons); [BOOM](https://boomlive.in/fact-file/the-curious-case-of-wolf-gupta-and-the-whitehat-jr-conundrum-10812), news, older). "Your child will build the next app" is now both a legal risk and out of step with what parents say they want.

The research team's own bottom-up illustration is 5M private-school students at Rs 500-1,500 a year, or **Rs 250-750 Cr of annual revenue across the market**. It rests on an unsourced base of "about 250M+ K-12 students" and is a planning scenario, not a measurement. The real sizing exercise is schools × enrolled students in Classes 3-8 × realised price, built from UDISE+ enrolment data and pilot conversion rates.

### AI literacy is replacing "learn to code" as the pitch

The field has shifted from syntax to AI literacy. Code.org rebranded Hour of Code as **Hour of AI** in December 2025 ([THE Journal](https://thejournal.com/articles/2025/10/02/codeorg-reinvents-hour-of-code-as-hour-of-ai.aspx)). CBSE named its mandate "Computational Thinking and AI", not "coding". NCERT's Grade 9-12 module aims to produce "careful, responsible users and evaluators of AI" rather than AI engineers ([EducationWorld](https://educationworld.in/ncert-develops-20-hour-ai-module-for-classes-ix-xii/), news). Parent surveys point the same way, but none of them is Indian. A survey of 300+ US and Canadian parents found they prioritised critical thinking and "digital defense" over coding lessons ([Marketing AI Institute](https://www.marketingaiinstitute.com/blog/parent-ai-education-gaps?hs_amp=true)). A vendor-run survey of 364 parents ranked AI fundamentals, project building and responsible use highest ([CodeYoung](https://www.codeyoung.com/blog/parents-kids-ai-survey); the vendor has a stake in the answer). For product design, this means each band needs an AI strand: what AI is and how it learns from data in Band B, building and critiquing no-code classifiers in Band C, and reading, testing and correcting AI-generated code in Bands D and E. Coding stays the tool that makes AI understandable rather than magical. AI literacy itself carries policy risk. Korea's reversal on AI textbooks and CBSE's deferral of Class 9 modules show that AI content is the most volatile part of any curriculum.

## Policy churn and empty labs are the biggest risks

The plan rests on three assumptions that could fail: that CBSE's Class 3-8 curriculum holds and is enforced, that schools have devices, and that principals will pay for something CBSE gives away free.

| Risk | Why it matters | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **Policy volatility** | CBSE "advises" rather than mandates Classes 3-8 and deferred Class 9 CT & AI in October 2026; Korea reversed its AI textbooks; England's curriculum changes in 2027-28 | Make the framework concept-based and keep board mapping in a separate layer that can be re-mapped quickly; avoid depending on a single circular |
| **Devices and labs** | 64.7% of schools have computer facilities; Bihar government schools 16.5%; about 35% of sanctioned ICT labs and about 88% of smart classrooms not working | Unplugged-first Bands A and B, printable kits, a teacher-projected mode, offline Android, low-spec devices |
| **Free substitutes** | Code.org, Scratch and the CBSE books cover basic content at no cost | Sell alignment, assessment, teacher tooling and language support, not content |
| **Teacher capacity** | Classes 3-5 are taught by maths and subject teachers; teacher readiness moderates outcomes | Scripted PRIMM template, micro-training videos, auto-grading, dashboard |
| **Shallow puzzle progression** | Points and badges raise quantity, not quality; Bers warns about puzzle-only coding | A make-something step in every level; a capstone project in each band |
| **Advancement without mastery** | Pacing pressure undermined mastery in Cognitive Tutor | Mastery gates, with teacher overrides recorded and remediation queued |
| **Trust and advertising** | WhiteHat Jr's ASCI findings, defamation suits and refund failures damaged trust across the sector | Monthly billing, a clear refund policy, no outcome claims |
| **Sales cycle** | Book lists close around December-January | A pilot-ready product and sales materials by about September for an April start |
| **Children's data and privacy** | The product collects data on minors | Get a legal review of Indian child-data consent obligations before launch (not researched here) |

| Open question | Ask whom | How to validate |
|---|---|---|
| Did schools actually implement CBSE CT & AI in 2026-27, and what are they using? | Principals, CBSE coordinators | Structured interviews with 15-20 CBSE and ICSE private schools across fee tiers and states |
| Will principals pay for a product that sits alongside free CBSE books, and how much per student? | Principals, school owners, finance heads | Pricing interviews with three anchors; letters of intent before December |
| Can a non-specialist maths teacher run a level in one period without training? | Class 3-5 maths and TWAU teachers | Classroom usability test of the PRIMM template with paper prototypes |
| What devices are available per class: a lab, one projector, or phones at home? | Teachers, IT staff, parents | Device audit in pilot schools; low-spec Android test matrix |
| Which language mix is needed first? | Parents, teachers in non-English-medium schools | Survey across 2-3 states; A/B test Hindi and one regional language |
| Do parents want coding, AI literacy or school-grade support? | Parents of Class 3-8 children | Indian parent survey (no Indian data exists); concierge test of a parent progress report |
| Does CISCE or any state board adopt the Class 3 CT & AI plan? | CISCE, SCERT officials | Track circulars; interview ICSE school heads |
| Does placement plus Express work in mixed-ability classes? | Pilot students, teachers | Run the placement test and Middle Express in 2-3 Class 6-7 sections; measure time to reach mastery |

The next research steps fall into two groups. The first is **document verification**. The CBSE circulars, the CT&AI curriculum and the CA 165, CS 083 and IP 065 syllabi have now been checked ([verification note](../cbse-verification.md)). Still to do: read the NCF-SE 2023 PDF directly for the CT goal wording, get the CISCE Classes I-VIII Computer Studies guidelines and the AI 417 / IT 402 syllabi, get the IAP 2022 screen-time table for ages 5-10, and review the free CBSE CT&AI student and teacher handbooks themselves. Collect UDISE+ 2025-26 device figures and Class 3-8 enrolment by board for a bottom-up market model. Check Google Play ratings for PictoBlox, CuriousJr and Codingal. The second group is **validation**. Run a December-cycle pilot offer to a small number of private schools. Run a paper-prototype study of Bands A and B with maths teachers. Test the block-to-text bridge (levels C4-C6) with Class 7 students, because no published study covers that age.

## Conclusion

The founder's instinct is right about the classroom and wrong about policy, and the difference matters for the product. Class 4 is when children meet Scratch. The paying customer, a CBSE school with a new CT & AI subject and no specialist teacher, has an obligation that starts at Class 3, is mostly unplugged and is assessed internally. So the product's core is not "teach kids to code from Class 4." It is "help a maths teacher deliver and evidence computational thinking and AI literacy from Class 3, with coding as the tool." Band A, below Class 3, and the block-to-text bridge in Band C are where the product can go beyond the mandate and stand out. The Class 9-12 exam tracks are a separate, later business.

The research also changes how risk should be weighed. The research supports the level-based structure: levels are the gamification element that actually improves learning, and decoupling level from grade has working precedents. The fragile part is the market's foundation, a single advisory circular from one board, already partly rolled back. Free content puts the price of basic coding at zero, and labs are often idle. The product that survives builds its value on concepts, so board mapping can be swapped as policy changes. It works without devices when it must, and it earns its fee through teacher time saved and progress made visible, not through content that someone else gives away.
