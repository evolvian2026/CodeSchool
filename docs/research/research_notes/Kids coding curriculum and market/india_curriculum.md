# At which class does coding begin in Indian schools? Board-by-board status (as of Nov 2026)

Research date: 5 Nov 2026. Where something is pre-2026 it is marked [OLDER]. Reliability tags: [OFFICIAL] = government or board document I read directly; [NEWS] = mainstream news; [SECONDARY] = edtech/publisher/blog (lower reliability).

**Bottom line for the founder's "it starts around Class 4" belief:** It is **partly right in practice, but wrong as a statement of official policy.**
- **Official national policy (NEP 2020):** coding *activities* are placed in the **Middle Stage (Classes 6-8)**. Computational thinking (CT) is meant to run "throughout the school years, starting with the foundational stage" through puzzles and games.
- **Newest official mandate (CBSE, from 2026-27):** CT & AI starts at **Class 3**. In Classes 3-5 it is mostly *unplugged* CT, folded into Maths and The World Around Us (TWAU). Block coding (e.g., Scratch) appears only as a computer competency for Classes 3-5, where schools have the equipment. It is not a programming course.
- **Board-examined programming subjects** start at **Class 9** (CBSE Computer Applications / AI skill subject; ICSE Computer Applications in Java) and at **Class 11** (CBSE Computer Science / Informatics Practices in Python; ISC Computer Science).
- **In practice:** private-school computer textbooks (e.g., Orange Education's Touchpad) and Kerala's state ICT textbooks introduce **Scratch at Class 3-4**. So "Class 4" is a fair description of what many children actually meet, not of what the law requires.

## 1. NEP 2020: exact wording on coding and computational thinking

### Takeaway
NEP 2020 never says "coding from Class 6" in those words. Para 4.25 says "Activities involving coding will be introduced in Middle Stage." The Middle Stage is Grades 6-8 under the 5+3+3+4 structure. Para 4.23 lists "digital literacy, coding, and computational thinking" among skills all students should learn. Para 4.25 says CT gets emphasis "starting with the foundational stage" through puzzles and games.

### Cited Findings
- **Para 4.23 (verbatim, extracted from the official NEP PDF):** "While students must have a large amount of flexibility in choosing their individual curricula, certain subjects, skills, and capacities should be learned by all students ... these skills include: ... problem solving and logical reasoning; vocational exposure and skills; **digital literacy, coding, and computational thinking**; ethical and moral reasoning; ..." — [NEP 2020 PDF (PIB mirror)](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/userfiles/NEP_Final_English_0.pdf) [OFFICIAL]. The Ministry of Education copy is [here](https://www.education.gov.in/sites/upload_files/mhrd/files/NEP_Final_English_0.pdf) (it returned 403 to the fetcher).
- **Para 4.24 (verbatim):** "Concerted curricular and pedagogical initiatives, including the introduction of contemporary subjects such as Artificial Intelligence, Design Thinking, Holistic Health, Organic Living, Environmental Education, Global Citizenship Education (GCED), etc. at relevant stages will be undertaken to develop these various important skills in students at all levels." — [NEP 2020 PDF](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/userfiles/NEP_Final_English_0.pdf) [OFFICIAL]. Note that 4.24 names AI but **not** coding.
- **Para 4.25 (verbatim):** "It is recognized that mathematics and mathematical thinking will be very important for India's future and India's leadership role in the numerous upcoming fields and professions that will involve artificial intelligence, machine learning, and data science, etc. Thus, mathematics and computational thinking will be given increased emphasis throughout the school years, starting with the foundational stage, through a variety of innovative methods, including the regular use of puzzles and games that make mathematical thinking more enjoyable and engaging. **Activities involving coding will be introduced in Middle Stage.**" — [NEP 2020 PDF](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/userfiles/NEP_Final_English_0.pdf) [OFFICIAL]
- **Para 4.26** (the next paragraph): "Every student will take a fun course, during Grades 6-8, that gives a survey and hands-on experience of a sampling of important vocational crafts ..." This vocational course became the vehicle CBSE later used for coding and AI skill modules in Classes 6-8. — [NEP 2020 PDF](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/userfiles/NEP_Final_English_0.pdf) [OFFICIAL]
- **Middle Stage definition:** NEP describes it as three years building on the Preparatory Stage "with the introduction of subject teachers". This is Grades 6-8 under 5+3+3+4. — [NEP 2020 PDF](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/userfiles/NEP_Final_English_0.pdf) [OFFICIAL]
- **Parliamentary answer:** A Ministry of Education reply in the Rajya Sabha (8 Dec 2021) cites para 4.23 for "digital literacy, coding, and computational thinking" and para 4.25 for coding in the Middle Stage. — [Rajya Sabha PQ 255, 08-12-2021](https://rsdebate.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/722047/1/PQ_255_08122021_U1149_p184_p184.pdf) [OFFICIAL, OLDER; seen via search snippet only, because the link now redirects]

### Inferences
- The popular "coding from Class 6" line is a paraphrase of para 4.25. The policy word is "activities", not a subject or a mandate with hours. NEP is a policy, not a syllabus, so it binds no board directly.
- Under NEP, CT (unplugged, math-based) is meant to start at the Foundational Stage, well before Class 4. Coding as such is meant for Classes 6-8.

### Gaps
- None material for the NEP wording; it was verified against the official PDF text.

## 2. NCF-FS 2022 / NCF-SE 2023: where computational thinking appears; NCERT materials

### Takeaway
In NCF-SE 2023, CT sits mainly inside the **Mathematics** curricular goals:
- a Preparatory (Grades 3-5) goal on problem solving framed "as a step towards developing computational thinking";
- a Middle Stage (Grades 6-8) goal, **CG-8**, to develop "basic skills and capacities of computational thinking", including algorithmic thinking;
- a Secondary (Grades 9-10) goal, **CG-9**, on decomposition and algorithmic problem solving.

NCERT has no standalone CT textbook for Classes 3-8. CBSE-issued, NCERT-aligned resource books exist, and NCERT published a 20-hour introductory CT & AI module for Grades 9-12 in October 2026.

### Cited Findings
- **NCF-SE 2023 Mathematics goals.** These come from an unofficial transcription of the goals, which cites the official NCF-SE PDF (ncert.nic.in/pdf/NCFSE-2023-August_2023.pdf). [SECONDARY transcription of an OFFICIAL doc; verify against the PDF.] — [PractEZ mirror of NCF math goals](https://full-hextra.pages.dev/docs/math-curr-ncf)
  - Preparatory Stage (Grades 3-5), CG-4: problem solving "as a step towards developing computational thinking".
  - Middle Stage (Grades 6-8), CG-8: "Develops basic skills and capacities of computational thinking". C-8.1 covers reformulating problems "into series of ordered steps (i.e., algorithmic thinking)". C-8.2 "learns to devise and follow algorithms, with an eye towards understanding correctness".
  - Secondary Stage (Grades 9-10), CG-9: "Develops computational thinking, i.e., deals with complex problems". C-9.1 to C-9.4 cover decomposition, analysing sequences of instructions, generalising solutions, and "algorithmic problem solving".
- **The CBSE CT & AI curriculum (2026-27) reuses the NCF-SE CG-8 wording for Classes 6-8.** Its goal for those classes reads: "Develops CT skills: decomposition, pattern recognition, data representation, generalisation, abstraction, and algorithms to solve problems where such techniques of CT are effective." — [CBSE CT&AI Curriculum Classes 3-8, 2026-27 (PDF)](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/CurriculumMain27/CTAI_Pri_2026-27.pdf) [OFFICIAL]
- **NCERT Secondary module.** "An Introductory Module on Computational Thinking and Artificial Intelligence for the Secondary Stage (Grades 9-12)" (code UN514, Oct 2026). It is about 20 hours, activity-based, and works with or without devices. NCERT says it is not meant to produce AI engineers but careful, responsible users and evaluators of AI. — [NCERT PDF](https://www.ncert.nic.in/pdf/An_Introductory_Module_AI_CT_UN514.pdf) [OFFICIAL; content summarised via search]; [EducationWorld](https://educationworld.in/ncert-develops-20-hour-ai-module-for-classes-ix-xii/) [NEWS]; [Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/ncert-develops-introductory-ai-computational-thinking-module-classes-9-12) [NEWS]
- **No NCERT CT textbook for Classes 3-8.** CBSE instead released free Student and Teacher resource books for each class from 3 to 8 (Circular Acad-18/2026, Apr 2026). The handbooks were reportedly developed with IIT Gandhinagar's Centre for Creative Learning and described as companions to NCERT Maths textbooks. — [CBSE Circular Acad-18/2026](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/Circulars/2026/18_Circular_2026.pdf) [OFFICIAL]; [EdTechReview](https://www.edtechreview.in/trends-insights/insights/cbse-ai-computational-thinking-curriculum-classes-3-8/) [SECONDARY]; [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/education/cbse-rolls-out-ct-ai-resource-books-for-classes-38-to-boost-early-tech-learning) [NEWS]
- **Ministry of Education announcement (31 Oct 2025).** AI & CT will be introduced "from Class III onwards" from the **2026-27 session**, within the NCF-SE 2023 framework.
  - The Department of School Education & Literacy (DoSE&L) is supporting CBSE, NCERT, KVS, NVS and States/UTs. A consultation was held on 29 Oct 2025.
  - An NCERT-CBSE Coordination Committee under NCF-SE will handle "seamless integration".
  - CBSE set up an expert committee chaired by Prof. Karthik Raman (IIT Madras).
  - Secretary Sanjay Kumar said "education in AI should be treated as a basic universal skill."
  - Sources: [Tribune, 31 Oct 2025](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/india/ai-curriculum-to-be-introduced-in-all-schools-from-class-iii/amp) [NEWS]; [PIB release PRID 2184211](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2184211&reg=48&lang=2) [OFFICIAL; title seen in search, page returned 403]; [Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/students-learn-ai-computational-thinking-from-class-3-starting-2026-27-cbse-ncert-frame-curriculum/amp) [NEWS]
- The NCERT Regional Institute of Education (RIE) Bhopal has a paper on "Computational Thinking and its importance in School Education". — [RIE Bhopal PDF](https://riebhopal.nic.in/IQAC\Computational_Thinking_and_its_importance_in_School_Education.pdf) [OFFICIAL; not read in full]

### Inferences
- NCF-SE puts **algorithmic thinking and programming-style reasoning formally at Grades 6-8**, consistent with NEP 4.25. Grades 3-5 get "steps towards" CT.
- The headline "AI from Class 3" (Oct 2025) is about CT and AI literacy, not programming. Classes 3-5 are explicitly unplugged and math-integrated.

### Gaps
- I did not read the NCF-SE 2023 PDF directly, so the stage-goal wording comes from a transcription. NCF-FS 2022 (Foundational Stage, ages 3-8) wording on CT was not verified. I found no evidence that NCF-FS names coding.
- No "Kaushal Bodh" CT/coding content was verified. Kaushal Bodh is NCERT's vocational workbook series for Classes 6-8. I did not confirm whether it contains coding units.
- I could not confirm whether States/UTs (SCERTs) have formally adopted the Class 3 AI & CT curriculum. The Ministry framed it as "all schools", but only CBSE issued a binding circular.

## 3. CBSE: mandates vs. optional skill subjects, with subject codes

### Takeaway
- **Classes 3-8 (from 2026-27):** CT & AI is now the closest thing to a mandate. Schools were officially "advised" to implement it, inside existing periods, with internal assessment only. Classes 3-5 get about 50 hours a year of mostly unplugged CT in Maths and TWAU, with block coding (Scratch) listed as a competency. Classes 6-8 get about 100 hours of CT, AI literacy and projects.
- **Classes 9-10:** the planned CT & AI modules were **deferred** for the 2026-27 Class IX batch by Circular Acad-70/2026 (29 Oct 2026).
- **Classes 9-12:** programming remains **optional/elective**: AI 417, IT 402, Computer Applications 165 in 9-10; Computer Science 083, Informatics Practices 065, AI 843 and Data Science 844 in 11-12.

### Cited Findings
- **Circular Acad-15/2026 (1 Apr 2026)**, "Launch of CBSE Curriculum Framework for Computational Thinking (CT) and Artificial Intelligence (AI) for Classes III-VIII (Session 2026-27)" (verbatim, extracted from the official PDF): "CBSE introduces the Computational Thinking (CT) and Artificial Intelligence (AI) Curriculum for Classes III-VIII from the academic session 2026-27 ... All schools are advised to take necessary steps for effective implementation of the curriculum." — [CBSE Circular Acad-15/2026](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/Circulars/2026/15_Circular_2026.pdf) [OFFICIAL]
- **Curriculum document, verbatim extracts:**
  - Classes 3-5: "The curriculum recommends embedding CT in all the subjects, such as TWAU (The World Around Us) and Mathematics, with focused learning experiences through workbooks. Time allocation: 50 hours annually."
  - Classes 6-8: "CT is again incorporated into all the school subjects through worksheet- and project-based learning, along with an introduction to foundational concepts of AI (AI literacy). Time allocation: 100 hours annually."
  - Classes 6-8 split: Advanced CT 40 h, Introductory AI concepts 20 h, Interdisciplinary projects (the remainder, about 40 h).
  - **Classes 3-5 computer competency (CG-3 / C-6):** "Develops familiarity with parts of a computer, input/output devices, file management, internet safety, educational software, and **block-based coding (e.g. Scratch)**."
  - **Classes 6-8:** "Hands-on and real-world problems ... to solidify and apply multidisciplinary foundational knowledge on **coding**, data analysis, and AI tools."
  - Python is mentioned only in the rationale ("various tools of technology, such as Python programming"). It is **not** a prescribed Class 3-8 topic.
  - Source: [CBSE CT&AI Curriculum 2026-27 (PDF)](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/CurriculumMain27/CTAI_Pri_2026-27.pdf) [OFFICIAL]
- **Class-wise activity examples (secondary summary of the handbooks):**
  - Class 3: CT puzzles incl. a Caesar cipher (unplugged).
  - Class 4: "Coin Flip Magic" (parity, error detection) and a floor sorting network (unplugged).
  - Class 5: find the fake weight among nine with a balance.
  - Class 6: everyday AI, basic data, ethics.
  - Class 7: AI domains (classification, regression, clustering, computer vision, NLP), data visualisation, bias.
  - Class 8: AI project lifecycle with no-code tools (Teachable Machine, Machine Learning for Kids).
  - Source: [EdTechReview](https://www.edtechreview.in/trends-insights/insights/cbse-ai-computational-thinking-curriculum-classes-3-8/) [SECONDARY]
- **Teaching and assessment:** Classes 3-5 are taught mainly by maths and other subject teachers; Classes 6-8 by a collaboration among subject teachers. Assessment is alternative and internal (puzzles, group activities, projects, journals). The curriculum was prepared by a 10-member committee under Karthik Raman (IIT Madras). — [Careers360, 2 Apr 2026](https://news.careers360.com/cbse-board-syllabus-ai-computational-thinking-subjects-part-of-maths-twau-class-3-4-5-6-7-8-no-exams-curriculum-iit-madras-ncert) [NEWS]
- **Mandatory or advisory? Sources conflict.**
  - The circular itself says "advised".
  - Some outlets and blogs call it "mandatory/compulsory". — [ThinkingJuggernaut blog](https://thinkingjuggernaut.in/blog/cbse-computational-thinking-ai-curriculum-classes-2026-27) [SECONDARY]
  - EdTechReview reports the 2026-27 secondary curriculum document says schools are "directed to introduce" it within existing periods. — [EdTechReview](https://www.edtechreview.in/trends-insights/insights/cbse-ai-computational-thinking-curriculum-classes-3-8/) [SECONDARY]
- **Circular Acad-70/2026 (29 Oct 2026)**, "Scheme of Studies for Class IX 2026-27" (verbatim): "To align the introduction of this curricular area with the implementation schedule, the subject 'Individuals in Society' and the 'Modules of CT&AI' in Class IX (2026-27) & the subject 'Environmental Education' in Class X (2027-28) shall not be the part of the curriculum for these batches."

  - Source: [CBSE Circular Acad-70/2026](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/Circulars/2026/70_Circular_2026.pdf) [OFFICIAL]

- **Subject codes, Classes 9-12:**
  - **AI 417** (Class 9-10 skill subject, introduced 2019). — [CBSE Circular 14/2019](https://www.cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/Circulars/2019/14_Circular_2019.pdf) [OFFICIAL]
  - **AI 843** (Class 11-12 skill subject). — [CBSE Notification 64/2024](https://www.cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/Notifications/2024/64_Notification_2024.pdf) [OFFICIAL]

- **Scale:** CBSE has about 33,227 affiliated schools. — [EdTechReview](https://www.edtechreview.in/trends-insights/insights/cbse-ai-computational-thinking-curriculum-classes-3-8/) [SECONDARY]

### Inferences
- In CBSE, the first class where **block coding (Scratch)** is officially named is **Class 3**, but only as a "where equipped" computer competency inside an advisory CT framework. The first where **"coding"** is officially expected is **Classes 6-8**. The first where **text-based programming** is a full, examined subject is **Class 9** (optional), and in practice **Class 11** for Python CS/IP.
- No CBSE programming subject is compulsory for all students at any class. Vocational education is compulsory in 9-10, but students can satisfy it without a computing subject.

### Gaps
- I did not verify the exact current syllabus content of Computer Applications 165 (believed to cover Python basics and HTML) or CS 083 / IP 065 (Python, SQL) from official PDFs. Treat these as unverified.
- I found no official data on how many CBSE schools actually implemented CT & AI in 2026-27.
- The claim that CT & AI becomes compulsory in Class 9 from 2027-28, with a Class X board exam in 2028-29, comes only from a secondary source ([EdTechReview](https://www.edtechreview.in/trends-insights/insights/cbse-ai-computational-thinking-curriculum-classes-3-8/)). It is not confirmed.

## 4. CISCE (ICSE/ISC)

### Takeaway
- **Classes 1-8:** CISCE publishes **guidelines rather than a prescribed, examined syllabus**. I could not find an official CISCE document that mandates Scratch or programming at a specific class.
- **In practice:** ICSE-aligned "Computer Studies" textbooks teach Scratch from about Class 3-4 and HTML/Python/QBasic-type content in the upper primary classes.
- **Formal, examined programming** starts at **Class 9** with ICSE Computer Applications (Java with BlueJ, an elective).
- **Recent additions:** ICSE Robotics & AI (Classes 9-10) and ISC Robotics/AI (Classes 11-12, from about 2025-26, with IIT Delhi).

### Cited Findings

- **Kerala** is the clear leader. Its state ICT textbooks (KITE) introduce logical games at Class 3, Scratch games by Class 4, game design by Class 6, AI from Class 7 and Python in Classes 9-10. Robotics is mandatory in Class 10 (from 2025). Little KITEs IT clubs are expanding to Classes 5-12.
- **Tamil Nadu** has AI, coding and online tools for Classes 6-9, running as a pilot (TN SPARK) in about 5,000 government schools.
- **Karnataka** offers AI as a vocational subject in Classes 9-10 in 1,642 government high schools and is exploring AI from Class 6.
- I found nothing reliable for Maharashtra, Andhra Pradesh/Telangana or Delhi.

## 6. Atal Tinkering Labs (AIM, NITI Aayog)

### Takeaway
ATLs serve **Classes 6-12**. About 10,000 were operational by late 2025, and the Budget 2025-26 targets 50,000 more in government schools over five years. They are maker/STEM labs (electronics, robotics, IoT, 3D printing), not a curriculum mandate.

## 7. Private-school textbook publishers: when Scratch, Logo and Python start

### Takeaway
Commercial computer textbooks used in CBSE and ICSE private schools commonly introduce **Scratch at Class 3**. They move to Scratch game creation, HTML and sometimes Python by about **Class 6**. This is the main source of the "coding from Class 3-4" experience in private schools.

### Cited Findings
- **Orange Education "Touchpad" series:**
  - Touchpad Plus Ver 2.1, Class 3: Paint 3D, Word, Internet, "Stepwise Thinking", "Let's Learn Scratch". — [Storytel listing](https://www.storytel.com/sg/books/touchpad-plus-ver-2-1-class-3-2579506) [SECONDARY]
  - Touchpad Prime Ver 2.1, Class 3: "Stepwise Thinking & Scratch Programming". — [Storytel](https://www.storytel.com/tv/books/touchpad-prime-ver-2-1-class-3-2579557) [SECONDARY]
  - Touchpad iPrime Ver 2.1, Class 6 ("as per new ICSE syllabus"): "Scratch Programming - Game Creation", HTML, mail merge, cyber security. Another Class 6 edition has "Introduction to Python" with HTML5/CSS3. A Ver 3.1 Class 6 edition teaches BASIC-256. — [Bookmate listings](https://en.bookmate.com/books/n4HvDSua) [SECONDARY; version-to-content mapping uncertain]
- **ICSE-aligned books:** Tekie ICSE Coding uses Scratch 3.0 in Grades 4-5. — [Issuu Grade 5](https://issuu.com/tekie-app/docs/tekie_icse_coding_grade_5_p1_ebook) [SECONDARY]
- **KVS (Kendriya Vidyalaya) school:** a KV Shalimar Bagh resource ("Technofun 3") shows Class 3 computer content in use. — [KVS PDF](https://shalimarbagh.kvs.ac.in/sites/default/files/TECHNOFUN%20%203.pdf) [SECONDARY; contents not read]
- **School syllabus plans:** a 2026-27 DPS Haridwar Class III computer syllabus exists. — [DPS Haridwar PDF](https://resources.edunexttechnologies.com/web-data/dpshardwar/pdf/CLASS-III-COMPUTER-SYLLABUS-2026-27.pdf) [SECONDARY; contents not read]

### Inferences
- **Typical private-school progression** (inferred from the listings above; not a formal standard):
  - Classes 1-2: computer parts, Paint, typing.
  - Class 3: "stepwise thinking" plus intro Scratch (some older books use Logo).
  - Classes 4-5: Scratch projects.
  - Classes 6-8: Scratch games, HTML, BASIC-256/QBasic or intro Python, plus AI modules.
  - Class 9+: Python (CBSE) or Java (ICSE).

### Gaps

## 8. Adoption data: computer labs and devices (UDISE+)

### Takeaway
- Nationally, **64.7%** of schools had "computer facilities" in UDISE+ 2024-25, up from 57.2% (2023-24) and 47.7% (2022-23).
- The gap between states is very wide: about 25% in Bihar and West Bengal vs. 100% in Delhi, Chandigarh and Lakshadweep.
- Government schools lag far behind private schools; in Bihar, 16.5% of government schools vs. 72% of private ones.
- So even "official" Class 3 plugged activities (Scratch) are infeasible in a large share of government schools.

### Cited Findings
- National 64.7% (2024-25), 57.2% (2023-24), 47.7% (2022-23). Bihar 25.2% (government 16.5%, private 72%). West Bengal 25.1% (government 21.3%, private 53.3%). — [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/india/education/govt-report-flags-digital-divide-in-schools-25-in-bihar-bengal-have-computer-facilities-65-nationally/2731591) [NEWS]; [Ministry of Education parliamentary annexure](https://www.education.gov.in/sites/upload_files/mhrd/files/parliament_annexure_en/447_parl.pdf) [OFFICIAL]; [UDISE+ 2024-25 state-wise facilities table](https://educationforallinindia.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/State-wise-Percentage-of-Schools-by-Facilitiy-UDISEPlus-2024-25.pdf) [SECONDARY host of official data]
- Over 23% of Maharashtra schools lack functional computers. — [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/pune/over-23-of-maharashtra-schools-lack-functional-computers-udise-data-reveals) [NEWS]
- About 63% of schools have internet. — [Education for All in India](https://educationforallinindia.com/63-percent-of-indian-schools-now-online-yet-over-25000-languish-without-electricity/) [SECONDARY]
- A UDISE+ 2025-26 PIB release (ID 2282141) appears to exist. — [Education for All in India](https://educationforallinindia.com/pib-press-on-udise-2025-26/) [SECONDARY; figures not verified]

### Inferences
- "Computer facility" is not the same as a functional lab with a usable pupil-to-device ratio, so real access for coding is lower than 64.7%.
- This is why CBSE and NCERT designed Classes 3-5 CT as unplugged, and why the NCERT 9-12 module states it can be done without devices.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve UDISE+ 2025-26 figures on computers or labs.
- No national data on the share of schools that actually teach Scratch or Python at a given class.
