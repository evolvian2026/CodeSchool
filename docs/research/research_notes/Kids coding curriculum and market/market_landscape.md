# Market Landscape: Kids' and School Coding Education (India first, global second), as of Nov 2026

Research date: 5 Nov 2026. Dates are given for every item. Items older than 2025 are marked **[older]**. Figures from paid "market research" publishers (IMARC, Spherical Insights, market.us, The Business Research Company, Ken Research, TechSci, Raysolute and similar) are marked **[LOW-QUALITY ESTIMATE]**: their methods are not public, their scopes differ, and they often circulate through press-release syndication.

## 1. Market size (Indian K-12 edtech, the Indian kids-coding segment, the global kids-coding market)

### Takeaway
No reliable, methodology-backed figure exists for the Indian kids-coding market. The common "$14B" number has no source, and every global "online coding for kids" figure comes from paid-report vendors whose 2025 estimates run from about $0.5B to $6.5B, with CAGRs of 8 to 21%. The policy signals are more dependable for planning: CBSE's compulsory Computational Thinking (CT) and AI curriculum, the 50,000 new Atal Tinkering Labs, and the Samagra Shiksha ICT norms. A founder should size the market bottom-up (schools × students × price per student) and not rely on these reports.

### Inferences
- Bottom-up check (my estimate, not sourced): India has about 250M+ K-12 students. If a coding or CT curriculum product reached even 5M private-school students at Rs 500–1,500 per student per year, that would be a Rs 250–750 Cr annual revenue pool. That is far below the "$14B" claim, but it is credible and B2B-driven.
- The CBSE mandate (Classes 3–8 from 2026–27) is the biggest structural demand change since NEP 2020. It turns coding from a parent-paid "optional extra" into a school compliance need, which favours B2B curriculum suppliers that sell textbooks plus a platform.

### Gaps
- No RedSeer, KPMG/Google, or HolonIQ figure for the Indian kids-coding segment was found in this pass. The KPMG-Google "Online Education in India 2021" report (2017) is too old to be useful.
- No count was found of how many CBSE schools buy third-party coding curricula, and no figure for spending per school.

## 2. Indian players (model, price, grades, traction)

### Takeaway
B2C 1:1 live-class coding (WhiteHat Jr, Camp K12, BrightChamps, Codingal) peaked in 2020–21 and has since shrunk, been folded into parents, or plateaued. Survivors are small (Codingal) or have pivoted to multiple subjects (BrightChamps, PlanetSpark). B2B school-curriculum companies (LEAD, Eupheus, Next Education, Extramarks) and ATL/robotics lab vendors (Tinkerly, STEMpedia) earn steadier revenue, mostly by bundling coding inside wider school offerings.

### Inferences
- Revenue is concentrated in B2B companies, which earn Rs 225–390 Cr a year (LEAD, Eupheus, Extramarks), not in pure kids-coding B2C companies, none of which has visible revenue above about Rs 100 Cr. Coding is a feature inside larger school bundles.
- 1:1 live-class unit costs (INR 700–2,800 per class) put coding out of reach for most Indian families. This keeps pure B2C coding a niche for metros and NRIs, which is why several players shifted to US and Middle East customers.
- From background knowledge, not verified in this pass: PlanetSpark is mainly a communication and public-speaking business, so it is a template for live-class unit economics, not a direct coding competitor.

## 3. Global players (pricing, school vs parent channel)

### Takeaway
Globally, free nonprofit platforms (Code.org, Scratch) dominate the school channel and set a price ceiling of zero for the basics. Paid players sell to parents through cheap subscriptions ($60–$300 a year) and to schools through quote-based or per-seat licences (Minecraft Education at about $5 per user per year). BYJU's $200M Tynker purchase returning about $2.2M in 2025 is the clearest sign of how little a B2C kids-coding brand is worth at exit.

### Cited Findings
- **Code.org**: the site claims 155M+ students reached, 3M+ teachers, 2B+ hours of learning and 190 countries. ## 4. Business models: B2C vs B2B vs B2G in India; procurement cycles

### Takeaway
In India, B2B to private schools (curriculum, textbooks and platform bundles sold per student per year and paid through school fees) is the model that has produced durable revenue (LEAD Group: Rs 387 Cr in FY26, EBITDA breakeven). B2C live classes burned capital, and B2G is lumpy, tied to grants and run through GeM, with a large gap between sanctioned and working labs. Buying decisions follow the academic calendar: book lists close around December–January and orders run until February–March for April session starts.

### Cited Findings
- **B2B economics**: LEAD reports Rs 415 Cr ARR for AY 2025–26, recognised over the year, which matches annual per-school/per-student contracts — [Tribune/ANI](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/business/lead-group-achieves-ebitda-breakeven-secures-arr-of-rs-415-cr-for-ay-25-26). A per-student add-on example is an AI tutor at about Rs 2,000 per student per year — [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2025/08/edtech-unicorn-lead-group-narrows-losses-stabilises-core-business)
- **Textbook bundling**: private publishers supply enriched content beyond NCERT and state books to private schools — [All About Book Publishing](https://allaboutbookpublishing.com/13041/changing-landscape-of-k12-publishing-in-india/)
- **Procurement timing**: most school book orders arrive by December–January, with some in February–March; schools set book lists by January, so a curriculum offered mid-year misses the session **[older, 2016–2020]** — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/article/specials/publishing-battle-over-textbooks-heats-up-117010901411_1.html); [Indian Printer & Publisher](https://indianprinterpublisher.com/blog/2020/07/school-textbook-production-and-the-covid-19-pandemic)
- Regional differences: textbook buying peaks January–March in Delhi and April–June in Mumbai — [BookMandee](https://bookmandee.com/?p=11463) (consumer blog, weak source)
- **B2G, Samagra Shiksha ICT**: Rs 6.4 lakh one-time plus Rs 2.4 lakh a year per school for 5 years — [DoSEL](https://dsel.education.gov.in/scheme/ict-samagra). Undated DoSEL figures: 120,614 ICT labs sanctioned but only 78,498 working; 82,120 smart classrooms sanctioned but only 9,833 working — [DoSEL](https://dsel.education.gov.in/scheme/ict-samagra). Internet charges can be paid from recurring grants — [Lok Sabha reply](https://eparlib.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/2979423/1/AU2223_4aS3eh.pdf)
- **B2G, ATL**: the grant is reported as Rs 20 lakh per school over 5 years (Rs 10 lakh setup plus Rs 2 lakh a year), from a secondary source to be checked against AIM guidelines — [Oliveboard explainer](https://www.oliveboard.in/blog/?p=228225). Vendors sell through GeM (e.g. Tinkerly) — [CXO Today](https://cxotoday.com/newsletter/top-5-companies-that-seek-to-offer-learning-through-atl-and-other-services-with-the-aid-of-ai-and-robotics/). AIM guidelines — [AIM PDF](https://www.aim.gov.in/pdf/ATL-Application_Guidelines-2018.pdf) **[older]**
- **B2G example**: the Delhi government ran a CODE-A-THON with HT for government-school students (Oct 2020) — [afaqs](https://www.afaqs.com/news/mktg/coding-for-kids-how-this-edtech-sub-segment-became-a-pandemic-era-star) **[older]**
- **CBSE affiliation**: the CT/AI subject for Classes 3–8 is compulsory from 2026–27 and is taught through CBSE/NCERT resource books integrated with existing subjects — [Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/students-learn-ai-computational-thinking-from-class-3-starting-2026-27-cbse-ncert-frame-curriculum/amp). Commentators question how clear the implementation is — [Vision IAS summary of Indian Express](https://visionias.in/current-affairs/upsc-daily-news-summary/article/2026-04-10/the-indian-express/polity-and-governance/cbses-ai-curriculum-lofty-goals-little-clarity)

### Inferences
- A third-party coding curriculum does not need CBSE "affiliation" (affiliation applies to schools). It needs credible **mapping** to the CBSE CT/AI framework, the NCERT books and state syllabi, so a principal can justify adopting it. This is inferred; no rule on vendor approval was found.
- The best wedge is likely B2B2C: sell to schools before December–January for an April start, charge per student through the school fee, and upsell parent-facing practice. B2G should be a second stage through ATL and ICT vendors on GeM, because payments are delayed and labs often go unused.
- Typical B2B pricing for coding or CT add-ons is probably Rs 300–2,000 per student per year (inferred from the LEAD AI-tutor price and the ICT norms). No primary rate card was found.

### Gaps
- No primary data was found on typical INR per-student prices for coding curricula from Eupheus, Next Education or LEAD.
- Recent (2024–26) state tender examples for coding or CT curricula, and typical payment cycles, were not found.

## 5. Lessons from failures (WhiteHat Jr, refunds, churn, the post-COVID downturn, the 2022–24 funding crash)

### Takeaway
WhiteHat Jr shows how fear-based marketing ("Wolf Gupta", child-prodigy claims), legal pressure on critics, and high-ticket EMI sales with weak refunds can destroy trust. The 2022–24 funding crash (Indian edtech down from $4.1B in 2021 to about $0.3B in 2023) then removed the capital that kept paid-acquisition models alive.

### Cited Findings
- ASCI processed complaints against 7 WhiteHat Jr ads and found 5 in potential violation; it asked for withdrawal, including the "Wolf Gupta" ads — [Scroll](https://scroll.in/article/979338/ever-since-its-acquisition-by-byjus-whitehat-jr-has-been-in-the-news-for-all-the-wrong-reasons); [BOOM](https://boomlive.in/fact-file/the-curious-case-of-wolf-gupta-and-the-whitehat-jr-conundrum-10812) **[older, 2020]**
- WhiteHat Jr admitted in its own lawsuit that "Wolf Gupta" was fictional — [BOOM](https://boomlive.in/fact-file/the-curious-case-of-wolf-gupta-and-the-whitehat-jr-conundrum-10812) **[older]**
- It filed a Rs 20 Cr ($2.6M) defamation suit against critic Pradeep Poonia (Nov 2020) and won an interim injunction in the Delhi HC, then dropped the suit in May 2021. It also sued Dr Aniruddha Malpani — [The Wire](https://thewire.in/law/delhi-hc-pradeep-poonia-whitehat-defamation); [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/latest/corporate/story/whitehat-jr-drops-rs-20-cr-defamation-suit-against-critic-pradeep-poonia-294974-2021-05-04); [The News Minute](https://www.thenewsminute.com/amp/story/atom/whitehat-jr-s-online-crackdown-how-edtech-startup-went-after-its-critics-138601) **[older]**
- The Ministry of Education warned that some edtech companies mislead customers — [Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/some-edtech-companies-are-misleading-customers-education-ministry/amp) **[older]**
- Refund and EMI complaints (2023–2024): "after 6 months and 100 mails I am still waiting", EMIs deducted after stopping classes, and "company closed and not informed parents" — [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/whitehatjr.com?page=4); [Voxya](https://voxya.com/consumer-complaints/refund-not-received/207404); [Voxya](https://voxya.com/consumer-complaints/whitehat-jr-no-reply-from-whitehat-support-team/232451) (unverified user posts)
- Even in 2020, aggressive promotion "created a negative sentiment toward teaching young kids to code on screens". Parents saw CS as "optional", and RedSeer found supplementary education is used mainly to fill classroom gaps — [afaqs](https://www.afaqs.com/news/mktg/coding-for-kids-how-this-edtech-sub-segment-became-a-pandemic-era-star) **[older]**
- **Funding crash (Tracxn)**: Indian edtech peaked at $4.1B (2021). 2023 funding was about $321M. H1 2024 was $215M. Late-stage funding in 2023 was down 94% from 2021 — [Business Standard, Sep 2024](https://business-standard.com/companies/news/india-s-edtech-sector-sees-30-drop-in-funding-to-215-mn-amid-slowdown-124090600742_1.html); [Entrepreneur India](https://india.entrepreneur.com/news-and-trends/indian-edtech-sees-slowdown-in-funding-smart-integration/480350)
- 2022: BYJU's raised $1.2B, almost 50% of all edtech funding that year — [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/latest/corporate/story/total-start-up-funding-drops-35-in-2022-fintech-edtech-among-worst-hit-sectors-report-355907-2022-12-10)
- Shutdowns: Udayy laid off all 100 staff and closed when schools reopened (2022); SuperLearn also shut — [Business Insider India](https://www.businessinsider.in/tech/news/edtech-startup-udayy-lays-off-all-100-employees-as-schools-and-colleges-physically-reopen/articleshow/91956974.cms); [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/another-edtech-startup-bites-the-dust-bengaluru-based-superlearn-shuts-shop). One source says 2,150 Indian edtechs shut down between 2020 and 2024 (weak source) — [Youth Inc](https://youthincmag.com/why-indian-ed-tech-startups-are-shutting-down-in-record-numbers)
- Exit values: Tynker went for about $2.2M against $200M paid — [Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/news/byjus-forced-to-sell-epic-and-tynker-in-distressed-deal-9350885)

### Inferences
- Churn drivers (synthesised): demand was a COVID-era substitute for screen time, the product was expensive for what parents saw as an optional subject, teachers changed after the demo class, the "child will build apps" promise went unmet, and loan-linked prepayments made refunds painful. A new entrant should avoid upfront annual EMI sales, publish a simple refund policy, and avoid outcome claims (ASCI risk).

## 6. AI coding assistants and the shift to "AI literacy" and computational thinking (2024–2026)

### Takeaway
The field has clearly moved from "learn syntax" to "AI literacy plus computational thinking". Code.org rebranded its flagship event as the Hour of AI (2025), and CBSE's 2026–27 mandate is called "Computational Thinking and AI", not "coding". Parents increasingly want critical thinking and safe, responsible use of AI more than programming lessons.

### Cited Findings
- Code.org: Hour of Code became Hour of AI to "expand foundational AI literacy alongside computer science" — [THE Journal](https://thejournal.com/articles/2025/10/02/codeorg-reinvents-hour-of-code-as-hour-of-ai.aspx). Code.org says 84% of students already use AI, but only 16% of high-school leaders say all their students learn about it in school — [Code.org](https://code.org/about)
- CBSE/NCERT CT and AI curriculum for Classes 3–8 (2026–27), with puzzles and games in early grades, AI concepts later, and a focus on ethics — [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/ai-story/ai-computational-thinking-curriculum-classes-3-8); [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/education/cbse-to-implement-computational-thinking-ai-curriculum-for-classes-38-from-202627-announces-teacher-training-theme-with-focus-on-coding-future-skills)
- Parent survey (Nov 2025, 300+ US/Canada parents): parents did not ask for coding lessons; they prioritised "digital defense" and critical thinking — [Marketing AI Institute](https://www.marketingaiinstitute.com/blog/parent-ai-education-gaps?hs_amp=true)
- GoStudent survey: 58% of parents believe AI skills are key to their children's future careers — [EdTech Innovation Hub](https://www.edtechinnovationhub.com/news/58-percent-of-parents-believe-ai-skills-are-key-to-their-childrens-future-careers)
- Allwork: 97% of parents fear AI will upend their children's careers within a decade (Oct 2025) — [Allwork](https://allwork.space/2025/10/97-of-parents-fear-ai-will-upend-their-childrens-career-within-a-decade/)
- CodeYoung survey of 364 parents (2026; the vendor sells coding courses): top AI skills wanted were fundamentals (60%), building projects (57%) and responsible use (54%) — [CodeYoung](https://www.codeyoung.com/blog/parents-kids-ai-survey)
- Free AI teacher tools are lowering prices: Khanmigo for Teachers is free in 49 countries in English — [Microsoft](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/education/blog/?p=6378)
- Coding vendors are reframing coding as a way to "understand, verify and customise AI-generated code" — [CodeWizardsHQ](https://www.codewizardshq.com/in-an-ai-world-should-my-child-still-learn-to-code/) (vendor, has a stake in the answer)

### Inferences
- A grade-wise app should be positioned as "CT and AI literacy, with coding as the tool": logic and decomposition from Classes 1–5, block coding plus AI concepts from Classes 3–8 (mapped to CBSE), and text coding with AI pair-programming and checking AI output from Classes 9–12.
- "Your child will be the next app founder" marketing is now both legally risky (ASCI) and out of step with what parents say they want.

### Gaps
- No India-specific parent survey on AI and coding attitudes (2024–26) was found.

## 7. Gaps and opportunities (regional language, low bandwidth, Android-first, syllabus alignment, teacher enablement, assessment, affordability)

### Takeaway
The best-documented unmet needs are: (a) phone-first and offline delivery, since few children have PCs while smartphone access at home is high; (b) regional-language instruction, since most students are not in English-medium schools; (c) making the government labs that already exist actually work and train their teachers; and (d) assessment and reporting that school leaders can use for the new CBSE CT/AI subject.

### Cited Findings
- ASER 2024 (rural, ages 14–16): about 90% have a smartphone at home, about 31% own one (36.2% of boys against 26.9% of girls), 82.2% can use one, 57% used it for study in the past week and 76% for social media — [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/india/education/in-rural-india-82-2-teens-aged-14-16-know-how-to-use-smartphone-but-only-57-use-it-for-studies/2467560); [India TV](https://www.indiatvnews.com/education/news/over-82-of-children-aged-14-16-know-how-to-use-smartphones-key-findings-of-aser-report-2025-01-28-973481); [Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/aser-report-says-over-56-percent-children-14-16-age-group-use-smartphone-for-educational-purpose/amp)
- CuriousJr claim: 88% of India's 270M K-12 children lack PC or laptop access, so it built a phone-first coding app — [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2022/01/gurugram-based-edtech-startup-helping-students-code-without-laptop/amp) **[older; founder claim]**
- Language: a study cited by Tinkerly says only 17% of Indian children study in English-medium schools against 49% in Hindi-medium schools; Tinkerly offered Hindi coding from INR 500 a month — [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2021/11/startup-bharat-jaipur-based-tinkerly-coding) **[older]**. CuriousJr added Telugu and Tamil and claims 70%+ prefer to learn in their mother tongue — [CXO Today](https://cxotoday.com/press-release/curiousjr-initiates-a-new-plan-for-coding-in-vernacular-languages/) **[older]**
- Lab use gap: about 35% of sanctioned ICT labs and about 88% of sanctioned smart classrooms were not working per a DoSEL document — [DoSEL](https://dsel.education.gov.in/scheme/ict-samagra)
- Teacher enablement: CBSE's teacher-training theme focuses on coding and future skills, and teacher training was set for the summer break before 2026–27 — [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/amp/education/cbse-ncert-introduce-ai-computational-thinking-curriculum-for-classes-3-to-8-teacher-training-to-begin-in-summer-break). Commentators say the curriculum has "lofty goals, little clarity" — [Vision IAS / Indian Express](https://visionias.in/current-affairs/upsc-daily-news-summary/article/2026-04-10/the-indian-express/polity-and-governance/cbses-ai-curriculum-lofty-goals-little-clarity)
- Affordability: B2C 1:1 classes cost about INR 700–2,833 per class (Codingal, BrightChamps) — [Codingal](https://www.codingal.com/en-us/llms-full.txt); [BrightChamps](https://brightchamps.com/en-in/courses/coding-classes-for-kids/beginner-coding-course-grade-1). Even Rs 2,000 per student per year is a "premium" B2B add-on — [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2025/08/edtech-unicorn-lead-group-narrows-losses-stabilises-core-business)

### Inferences
- Opportunity: an Android-first, offline-capable, multilingual app (Hindi plus 3–4 regional languages first) mapped grade by grade to the CBSE CT/AI books, NCERT and major state boards. It should include a teacher mode (lesson plans, a projected "teach this period" view, auto-graded worksheets) and a principal or parent dashboard. It should be priced around Rs 200–800 per student per year for B2B, with a free parent tier.
- A second wedge is pairing with ATL and ICT lab vendors (Tinkerly, STEMpedia and the like) to supply the software and teacher-training layer that unused labs lack.
- Screen-free or "unplugged" CT activities for Classes 1–3 match CBSE's puzzles-and-games approach and work in schools without devices.

### Gaps
- No quantitative data was found on the regional-language coding market or on Hindi coding app adoption after 2022.
- No ICSE or state-board coding or CT mandates were researched in this pass.
- No data was found on device specs (RAM and storage) typical of student households.

## 8. User and parent sentiment (reviews and complaints)

### Takeaway
Live-class coding services get high star ratings (Codingal about 4.8 on Trustpilot), but recurring complaints are about teacher continuity and demo-class bait-and-switch. The worst sentiment (WhiteHat Jr) centres on refunds, EMI and loan lock-in, and misleading ads.

### Cited Findings
- Codingal: about 580–684 Trustpilot reviews, about 4.8 TrustScore, and replies to 100% of negative reviews. Complaints: the "teacher assigned was different from the demo teacher", frequent teacher changes, and delays in changing teachers (admitted by the co-founder) — [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/codingal.com); [Trustpilot p7](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/www.codingal.com?page=7)
- WhiteHat Jr: refund delays, EMIs continuing after cancellation, no support response, and closure without notice (2023–2024) — [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/whitehatjr.com?page=4); [Voxya](https://voxya.com/consumer-complaints/refund-never-recieved-customer-support-not-replying/232577); [Voxya](https://voxya.com/consumer-complaints/bank-installment-issue-/188571)
- 2020 sector view: parents did not understand what kids' coding involves, there was backlash against screen-heavy coding for young children, and building trust was the main challenge — [afaqs](https://www.afaqs.com/news/mktg/coding-for-kids-how-this-edtech-sub-segment-became-a-pandemic-era-star) **[older]**

### Inferences
- Trust levers for a new entrant: a consistent teacher (or no live teacher dependence), monthly billing with no loan financing, transparent visible progress tied to the school syllabus, and no exaggerated outcome claims.
