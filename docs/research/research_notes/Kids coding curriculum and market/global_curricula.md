## England: National Curriculum Computing (2014) - KS1-KS4 expectations and status of the Curriculum and Assessment Review

### Takeaway
England has made computing (including programming) statutory from age 5 since September 2014; the 2025 Curriculum and Assessment Review (CAR) keeps computing as a subject, rebalances it toward digital literacy and AI, and replaces GCSE Computer Science with a broader Computing GCSE, with a revised curriculum to be published by spring 2027 and taught from September 2028.

### Cited Findings
- *(older, 2013/2014)* Programmes of study [K] — [gov.uk Computing PoS](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/national-curriculum-in-england-computing-programmes-of-study/national-curriculum-in-england-computing-programmes-of-study):
  - **KS1 (ages 5-7):** understand what algorithms are, how they are implemented as programs on digital devices, and that programs execute by following precise instructions; create and debug simple programs; use logical reasoning to predict behaviour of simple programs; use technology safely.
  - **KS2 (ages 7-11):** design, write and debug programs that accomplish specific goals, including controlling or simulating physical systems; solve problems by decomposing them; use sequence, selection and repetition; work with variables and various forms of input/output; understand networks including the internet.
  - **KS3 (ages 11-14):** use two or more programming languages, at least one of which is textual, to solve computational problems; use data structures (lists, tables, arrays); design and use modular programs with procedures/functions; Boolean logic; binary; hardware/software components.
  - **KS4 (ages 14-16):** all pupils should have the opportunity to study aspects of IT and computer science at sufficient depth to progress; develop capability in computer science, digital media and IT. GCSE Computer Science is optional.
- CAR final report "Building a world-class curriculum for all" and the government response were published 5 November 2025 — [Government response (PDF)](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/690b2a4a14b040dfe82922ea/Government_response_to_the_Curriculum_and_Assessment_Review.pdf); [HEPI](https://www.hepi.ac.uk/2025/11/06/the-curriculum-and-assessment-review/) [V]
- CAR recommends greater clarity in the Computing curriculum about what is taught at each key stage, building digital literacy and confidence with technology including AI — [Computing at School summary](https://www.computingatschool.org.uk/forum-news-blogs/2025/november/car-final-report-summary/) [V]
- Government accepted replacing GCSE Computer Science with a broader **GCSE in Computing** that "reflects the full breadth of the Computing curriculum" while still supporting progression to A-level Computer Science — [CAS](https://www.computingatschool.org.uk/forum-news-blogs/2025/november/car-final-report-summary/); [BCS](https://www.bcs.org/articles-opinion-and-research/reformed-school-curriculum-will-feature-ai-media-literacy-and-a-new-computing-gcse/) [V]
- Government response says pupils will learn "how computers can be trained using data" from a young age, plus AI literacy; government will explore a new Level 3 (age 16-18) qualification in data science and AI — [CAS](https://www.computingatschool.org.uk/forum-news-blogs/2025/november/car-final-report-summary/) [V]
- Government commitments: rebalance Computing to include digital literacy while keeping core CS; continue funding the National Centre for Computing Education — [CAS](https://www.computingatschool.org.uk/forum-news-blogs/2025/november/car-final-report-summary/) [V]
- Evidence base in the review: secondary computing teaching time has declined since 2011/12; GCSE CS take-up 20% of boys vs 6% of girls; students score 0.72 of a grade lower in GCSE CS than their other subjects (1.11 for girls); very few schools teach Computing to all KS4 students; specialist teacher shortage — [CAS](https://www.computingatschool.org.uk/forum-news-blogs/2025/november/car-final-report-summary/) [V]
- Timeline: government aims to publish the revised national curriculum by spring 2027, for first teaching from September 2028 — [search summary of Government response / Lords Library](https://lordslibrary.parliament.uk/national-curriculum-and-assessment-review/) [V, via search snippet; PDF not opened]
- Not all recommendations received a response on the day — [techUK](https://www.techuk.org/insights/policy-views/what-does-the-curriculum-and-assessment-review-mean-for-technology-and-computing-education) [V via snippet]

### Inferences
- As of late 2026, schools in England are still teaching the 2014 programmes of study; the revised Computing curriculum is in drafting (publication expected spring 2027). The age-5 start for algorithms/debugging is expected to remain.
- England is a clear "earlier than Grade 4" benchmark: block-based/physical programming in KS1-KS2, a mandated move to a text language by KS3 (age 11-14).

### Gaps
- Draft revised Computing programmes of study (if any consultation draft was published during 2026) were not found in this session.
- First teaching date of the new Computing GCSE (likely later than Sept 2028 for KS4) not confirmed.

## USA: CSTA standards (2017 and 2026 revision) and Code.org State of CS figures

### Takeaway
CSTA published the revised **2026 CSTA PK-12 Computer Science Standards** (first major revision since 2017), restructuring from the old 1A/1B/2/3A/3B levels into a PK/K band, grade-by-grade Grades 1-5, a middle-school band, foundational high school and specialty strands with AI woven throughout. Nationally, 60% of US public high schools offered foundational CS in 2024-25 (flat versus prior year) and 12 states require CS for graduation.

### Cited Findings
- *(older)* 2017 CSTA K-12 Standards levels: 1A (ages 5-7, K-2), 1B (ages 8-11, Gr 3-5), 2 (ages 11-14, Gr 6-8), 3A (Gr 9-10), 3B (Gr 11-12, specialty) [K] — [CSTA 2017 standards](https://csteachers.org/k12standards/)
- Revision announced with $1.6M funding; writing began September 2024; Draft 2.0 (2025) and Draft 3.0 feedback rounds — [CSTA funding announcement](https://csteachers.org/empowering-the-future-csta-announces-1-6-million-funding-for-groundbreaking-k-12-computer-science-standards-update/); [Draft 2.0](https://csteachers.org/Draft-2.0-Revised-PreK-12-CS-Standards) [V]
- The 2026 CSTA PK-12 CS Standards (copyright 2026, DOI 10.1145/3820482) use: a combined PK/K band; separate expectations for each of Grades 1-5; one middle-school band; foundational high-school band; Specialty I and II high-school standards in six areas (AI, cybersecurity, data science, game development, physical computing, software development); plus "X+CS" integration standards — [CSTA PK-12 Standards](https://csteachers.org/pk12standards/) [V]
- Five concepts: Algorithms & Design, Programming, Data & Analysis, Systems & Security, Computing & Society; twelve practices in four groups (Ethics & Social Responsibility, Inclusive Collaboration, Computational Thinking, Human-Centered Design). AI is distributed across the five concepts rather than a separate concept — [CSTA PK-12 Standards](https://csteachers.org/pk12standards/) [V]
- Publication reported July 2026 — [news.qq.com report](https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20260721A05JFN00) [V via search snippet; exact release date not confirmed on CSTA site]
- Code.org 2025 "State of AI + CS Education": 60% of US public high schools (14,805 of 24,405) offered foundational CS in 2024-25, unchanged from 2023-24; 6.1% of eligible students enrolled (down from 6.4%) — [Code.org 2025 report](https://advocacy.code.org/stateofcs/); [CSTA Washington review](https://www.cstawa.org/review-2025-of-ai-cs-education-report/) [V via search summaries]
- 12 states require CS for high school graduation (West Virginia the latest addition); 0 states require AI+CS for graduation; only 4 states (Colorado, Virginia, North Dakota, Ohio) explicitly emphasise AI in CS standards — [Code.org 2025 report](https://advocacy.code.org/stateofcs/); [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/new-report-ai--computer-science-are-the-foundation-for-us-k12-readiness-but-state-policies-lag-behind-302637515.html); [ASME](https://www.asme.org/topics-resources/content/requiring-cs-ai-may-not-meet-industry%E2%80%99s-true-needs) [V]
- A later Code.org/"CodeAI" page states 14 states require AI *or* CS coursework for graduation (different measure, later count) — [Code.org](https://code.org/en-US/codeorg-is-now-codeai) [V via snippet]
- Arkansas and Maryland report 100% of high schools offering foundational CS — [search summary of Code.org data](https://advocacy.code.org/stateofcs/) [V via snippet]

### Inferences
- In the US, model standards start at PreK/K, but actual exposure is uneven because standards are voluntary and adoption/implementation is state- and district-level; elementary CS is often delivered via Code.org CS Fundamentals (block-based) and text languages typically appear in middle/high school.
- The 2026 standards' grade-by-grade elementary expectations make "CS from Kindergarten" more explicit than before.

### Gaps
- Number of states that have adopted K-12 CS standards in the 2025 report (historically ~50 incl. DC) was not confirmed this session.
- A 2026 State of CS report (normally released ~Sept-Nov) was not found; 2025 figures are the latest confirmed.

## Other countries: Estonia, Finland, Singapore, Australia, China, South Korea, Japan, UAE, Israel, New Zealand

### Takeaway
Most benchmark countries introduce computational thinking or programming between ages 5 and 7 (England, Finland, Australia, NZ, Estonia, US model standards), while East Asian systems (Korea, Singapore's CFF, China's standalone IT subject) formally start in Grades 3-6. Since 2025 the main new trend is AI education layered on top of CS: UAE (KG-12, mandatory), China/Beijing (AI from primary), Singapore (AI for Fun), Estonia (AI Leap at upper secondary), and Korea's AI textbooks (rolled back to optional).

### Cited Findings
**Estonia**
- *(older)* ProgeTiiger (launched 2012) supports programming/robotics from pre-school and early grades; programme not a compulsory subject [K] — [Brookings 2021](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/building-skills-for-life-how-to-expand-and-improve-computer-science-education-around-the-world/)
- AI Leap (TI-Hüpe) launched September 2025, giving students and teachers free access to AI tools plus training; initially ~20,000 Grade 10-11 students and ~3,000 teachers; available in upper-secondary schools; aim is "meaningful" rather than more AI use; University of Tartu study of effects — [TI-Hüpe](https://tihupe.ee/en/); [Eurydice](https://eurydice.eacea.ec.europa.eu/news/estonia-ai-leap-initiative-enhance-learning-and-teaching); [TI-Hüpe research](https://tihupe.ee/en/research-and-reviews/) [V via search summary]
- Expansion to vocational schools projected to ~58,000 students and 5,000 teachers by 2027; teacher numbers vary across sources (3,000 vs 4,800) — [Eesti Elu](https://eestielu.ca/estonia-among-the-first-to-nationally-integrate-ai-into-schools/); [TFiR](https://tfir.io/estonias-ai-leap-2025-free-ai-education-for-students-and-teachers/) [V; secondary sources, conflicting figures]

**Finland**

## Patterns: start age for unplugged/block-based work vs. transition to text-based languages

### Takeaway
Internationally, exposure to algorithms/unplugged or block-based programming typically starts at **ages 5-7 (Grade 1 or earlier)** in England, Finland, Australia, NZ, Estonia and US model standards, with formal coding blocks in **Grades 3-6** in East Asia; the move to a **text-based language typically happens around ages 11-14 (Grades 6-8)**. "Coding starts around Grade 4" is therefore typical for *structured block-based coding* in Asian systems but late compared with countries that start computational thinking at age 5.

### Cited Findings
- England mandates algorithms/debugging at age 5 and a textual language by KS3 (age 11-14) — [gov.uk Computing PoS](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/national-curriculum-in-england-computing-programmes-of-study/national-curriculum-in-england-computing-programmes-of-study) [K]
- Australia v9: visual programs Years 3-6, general-purpose language Years 7-8 — [ACARA v9](https://v9.australiancurriculum.edu.au/f-10-curriculum/learning-areas/technologies/digital-technologies) [K]
- US 2026 CSTA standards begin at PK/K with grade-by-grade Grades 1-5 — [CSTA](https://csteachers.org/pk12standards/) [V]
- Singapore CFF targets upper primary (~age 10-12) — [IMDA](https://www.imda.gov.sg/How-We-Can-Help/code-for-fun) [V]
- Korea's mandatory hours sit in Grades 5-6 and middle school — [Asia Economy](https://cm.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2022122211013751722) [V]
- UAE starts AI/computing concepts at KG — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/uae/education/uae-schools-to-introduce-ai-curriculum-in-new-academic-year-1.500242831) [V]
- Brookings (2021) comparative review of CS education worldwide documents wide variation and that many countries embed CS in other subjects at primary level — [Brookings](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/building-skills-for-life-how-to-expand-and-improve-computer-science-education-around-the-world/) [K, older]

### Inferences
- Three-stage pattern: (1) ages 5-7 unplugged + tangible robots/ScratchJr; (2) ages 7-11 block-based (Scratch, Code.org, micro:bit MakeCode); (3) ages 11-14 text-based (Python most common), with exam-level CS at 14-16.
- Mandatory status varies: many countries embed programming in maths/technology (Finland, Japan) rather than as a separate subject; the US remains voluntary at national level.
- For India benchmarking: a Grade 4 start matches Singapore/China-style formal coding, but a Grade 1 computational-thinking strand would align with England/Australia/US/Finland.

### Gaps
- No single peer-reviewed cross-country dataset for 2025-2026 start ages was found this session; OECD reports were not checked.
- Actual classroom implementation (hours delivered vs. mandated) is not captured in curriculum documents.
