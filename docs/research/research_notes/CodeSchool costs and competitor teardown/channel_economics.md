# School Book Channel Economics in India (K-8 private-school booklist titles)

Scope: what share of MRP a small publisher of supplementary booklist titles (computer, GK, activity) keeps after channel margins, discounts, school commissions, returns, sampling/sales cost and the cash cycle. It does not repeat the CBSE "no forced single-vendor purchase" rules or the CT&AI book price survey (Rs 179-630), which an earlier report covered. Research date: 10 November 2026. Figures are dated where the source allows. Anything marked **[Estimate]** is my inference, not a sourced number.

Overall source caveat: no audited industry dataset breaks down school-book channel margins in India. The figures below come from trade-press opinion pieces, booksellers' associations, parents' complaints reported in news, a publishing blog, and listed-company earnings calls. Read them as ranges, not benchmarks.

---

## 1. Typical trade discounts (publisher to distributor to retailer) and total discount off MRP

### Takeaway
Discounts off MRP for school books are high and variable. The trade-press range for publisher discounts is **40-65% off MRP**. Local booksellers say they get **under 40%** on school textbooks and online sellers **up to 50%**. General book-trade sources put the distributor at 40-55% of MRP, with the retailer's 20-35% inside that. Publishers set MRP high enough to absorb these discounts, which a veteran publisher says inflates MRP and squeezes author royalties.

### Cited Findings
- Sesh Seshadri, a veteran publishing commentator, says school publishers' discount levels "range from 40% to 65%". He says this high-discount model drives MRP up and leaves authors little royalty, because royalties are now paid on net receipts rather than on published price. He proposes going back to a "10% discount to educational institutions" and cutting reseller discounts. (14 Oct 2022) — [All About Book Publishing](https://allaboutbookpublishing.com/9876/school-book-publishing-calling-for-an-imaginative-turnaround/)
- A Bhopal booksellers' association says local shops typically earn **less than 40% commission from publishers** on school textbooks, against **up to 50%** reportedly given to online sellers. It planned to ask the state School Education Minister to restrict online textbook sales. These are the association's claims. — [Free Press Journal (Bhopal)](https://www.freepressjournal.in/bhopal/booksellers-oppose-online-sale-of-school-textbooks)
- A general-trade (not school-specific) breakdown, all as % of MRP (17 Feb 2026):
  - **Distributor margin 40-55%**, up to 60% on slow-moving titles.
  - **Retailer margin 20-35%**, often inside the distributor margin.
  - **Publisher share 15-30%**, which must still cover printing, marketing and royalty.
  - Worked example: on a Rs 300 MRP book at a 50% distributor discount, the publisher nets Rs 150.
  - Source: [IIP Books blog](https://iipbooks.com/blogs/view/distributor-margins-in-india-how-book-distribution-really-works)
- Booksellers' "trade discount" ranges "anywhere between 10% and 65%" with no organised discount structure in India (older Scroll piece on GST, c. 2017). — [Scroll](https://scroll.in/article/845328/booksellers-and-publishers-are-feeling-the-heat-of-gst-will-they-pass-on-the-burden-to-the-reader)
- A Delhi distributor says a "discount war" among distributors has shrunk margins sharply as discounts rose. — [All About Book Publishing, "Book distribution works on trust"](https://allaboutbookpublishing.com/2081/book-distribution-works-on-trust/)
- A Kolkata school-book supplier advertises margins "upto 50%" for reselling to schools and retail shops. This is a seller's marketing claim. — [IndiaMART listing](https://m.indiamart.com/city/kolkata/textbook.html)
- A franchise listing offers distributors a 40% discount on list price for certain school books (single example). — [Franchise India listing](https://dealer.franchiseindia.com/manufacturer/E-Learning-2412)
- Benchmark on what is left after discounts: S Chand reported its "Highest Gross Margins of 68% in 5 years" for FY25. It credited better product mix, content licensing and stable paper prices. Gross margin here is on net revenue, i.e. after trade discounts, so direct material and print cost is about 32% of net revenue. (Q4 FY25 earnings call, May 2025) — [S Chand Q4 FY25 call transcript](https://files.tijorifinance.com/insight/india/7264/Conference%20Call/CC-Mar25.pdf)
- S Chand's FY26 guidance was 5-7% volume growth, with the rest from "product mix, price increase etc.". Annual list-price increases are a standard lever. (May 2025) — [S Chand Q4 FY25 call transcript](https://files.tijorifinance.com/insight/india/7264/Conference%20Call/CC-Mar25.pdf)

### Inferences
- **[Estimate]** For a small publisher selling booklist titles through a distributor or bookseller, plan on **45-55% off MRP** as the realistic base trade discount. That leaves **45-55% of MRP** as gross realisation before returns.
  - On Rs 449: about Rs 200-245.
  - On Rs 599: about Rs 270-330.
- **[Estimate]** Where the bookseller also has to fund a school commission (section 2), the discount the publisher concedes moves toward the 55-65% top of Seshadri's range. Realisation then falls to **35-45% of MRP**: about Rs 157-202 on Rs 449, or Rs 210-270 on Rs 599.
- Pricing to absorb: publishers set MRP high and keep discounting flexible, rather than setting a low MRP with thin discounts (Seshadri). A Rs 449/599 pack competes on MRP against titles priced the same way, so CodeSchool cannot assume it will realise more per rupee of MRP than incumbents do.

### Gaps
- No source gave a clean two-step split for school books specifically (publisher to distributor X%, distributor to retailer Y%). The two-tier figures above are from general trade.
- No source covered how Madhubun/Viva, Cordova, MBD or OUP India set discounts for K-8 supplementary titles. None of them publish this.

## 2. School "commissions"/"donations" for booklist adoption: prevalence, size, legal position, reputational risk

### Takeaway
News reports and enforcement actions show school-level margins on booklist sales are common. Reported figures:
- **15-40%** commission from publishers and traders (Andhra Pradesh, 2018).
- **40-50%+** retained margin alleged by parents (Telangana, June 2025).
- Books priced **5-10x cost** in the Jabalpur case (2024), where the alleged book commission totalled Rs 4.12 crore.

The regulatory trend has hardened against it:
- Delhi DoE orders (2025, April 2026).
- Karnataka, Haryana and Punjab bans on campus sales or tied vendors.
- Maharashtra's July 2026 assembly statement.
- MP criminal FIRs, which the High Court upheld in April 2026.
- NHRC notices to all states (April 2026).

Paying commissions is a material legal and reputational risk for a new brand.

### Cited Findings
- **Andhra Pradesh (Mar 2018):** publishers and traders reportedly offer "15 per cent to 40 per cent commission" to school managements on books, stationery, uniforms and shoes. Managements tie up with publications and vendors "taking big commissions". — [The Hans India](https://thehansindia.com/posts/index/Andhra-Pradesh/2018-03-31/Private-schools-turning-into-business-hubs/370804)
- **Telangana (9 Jun 2025):** parents allege schools receive publisher discounts but pass on none, keeping a margin of 40-50% "sometimes even more".
  - Book costs cited: up to Rs 10,000 for primary-class books; Rs 7,000 for nursery books.
  - Telangana Education Commission member PL Vishweshwer Rao said "Schools are not supposed to sell books" and called it "a definite violation of the rules".
  - Source: [Frontlist](https://www.frontlist.in/parents-slam-private-schools-over-overpriced-june)
- **Madhya Pradesh / Jabalpur (2024 to 2026):**
  - On 24 May 2024 the Collector directed FIRs against school managements, principals, booksellers and publishers.
  - Reports cite FIRs against 51 persons over "illegal school fee of Rs 81.30 crore and books commission of Rs 4.12 crore".
  - The state alleged unverifiable ISBNs and books priced about 5-10 times actual cost.
  - In April 2026 the MP High Court refused to quash the FIRs.
  - Sources: [Daily Pioneer (2024)](https://www.dailypioneer.com/2024/state-editions/jabalpur-distict-collector---s-action-being-implemented-in-the-state.html); [Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/mp-11-firs-registered-against-schools-shop-owners-for-illegally-raising-fees-textbook-prices); [LiveLaw (bail denied)](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/high-court/madhya-pradesh-high-court/madhya-pradesh-high-court-denies-bail-private-schools-books-sale-with-forged-isbn-numbers-case-263938); [SCC Online (Apr 2026, no relief to schools)](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2026/04/29/mp-hc-no-relief-to-private-schools-for-unauthorized-fee-hike-and-book-selling/); [LiveLaw (FIR not quashed)](https://www.livelaw.in/high-court/madhya-pradesh-high-court/madhya-pradesh-high-court-private-unaided-schools-fir-fake-isbns-school-fees-hike-531340)
- **NHRC (Apr 2026):** notices to all states after complaints that private schools pressure parents to buy private-publisher textbooks. It asked for enrolment data and details of textbooks procured for 2025-26, and reportedly directed a pan-India audit. — [Telangana Today](https://telanganatoday.com/nhrc-issues-notices-over-costly-textbooks-in-private-schools); [PrintWeek](https://www.printweek.in/article/nhrc-orders-audit-on-costly-school-textbooks/4j01mkmty0dd7xccg492abq94s); [Hindusthan Samachar](https://english.hindusthansamachar.in//Encyc/2026/4/23/NHRC-PRIYANK-KANUNGO-NOTICE-ST.php)
- **Delhi High Court (Aug 2025):** a PIL alleges private schools force costly books on parents, excluding EWS students. The court sought replies from the Delhi government, CBSE and NCERT. — [Madhyamam/IBTimes coverage via search](https://www.ibtimes.co.in/nhrc-issues-notice-over-costly-private-textbooks-asks-centre-states-enforce-ncert-norms-901441)
- **Delhi DoE:**
  - Mar 2025 order: schools must display at least five vendors on their websites and must not change uniform specifications for three years.
  - 1 Apr 2026 directive: private unaided recognised schools must not force purchase of books, notebooks, uniforms or bags from specific vendors.
  - Sources: [The Week (Mar 2025)](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/national/2025/03/26/des48-dl-doe-books-vendors.html); [Business Today (Apr 2026)](https://www.businesstoday.in/india/story/delhi-govt-orders-private-schools-to-stop-forcing-students-to-buy-books-uniforms-from-specific-vendors-523625-2026-04-02)
- **Karnataka:** the state barred private schools from selling textbooks, notebooks, uniforms and stationery on campus. The Karnataka Textbook Society supply is excluded. — [Udayavani](https://www.udayavani.com/english-news/karnataka-govt-bans-sale-of-uniform-other-items-in-private-schools)
- **Maharashtra (2 Jul 2026):** a minister of state told the Legislative Council the government will act strictly against private schools, including CBSE and ICSE schools, that compel purchases from designated shops. — [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/amp/education/maharashtra-to-crack-down-on-schools-forcing-parents-to-buy-educational-materials-from-specific-shops)
- **Haryana:** the Directorate of Secondary Education ordered recognised private schools not to compel purchase from specific shops. There is a 2025 Sirsa crackdown on costly books. — [Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/haryana/dont-force-students-to-buy-books-from-specific-shops-schools-told-495494/amp); [Tribune (Sirsa)](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/haryana/sirsa-pvt-schools-under-fire-for-forcing-costly-books-on-students-govt-cracks-down/amp)
- **Punjab:** the state barred schools from selling books and uniforms on premises. A parents' association called the ban an "eyewash", meaning poor enforcement. There was further action in 2025. — [Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/archive/punjab/state-bars-schools-from-selling-books-uniform-792612); [Tribune (eyewash)](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/archive/bathinda/parents-assn-chief-terms-ban-on-sale-of-books-on-school-premises-eyewash-793335); [Daily Pioneer 2025](https://dailypioneer.com/2025/state-editions/punjab-govt-acts-against-private-schools----arbitrary-practices-on-books-and-uniforms.html)
- **CBSE (context only):**
  - The April 2017 advisory said schools are community services, not commercial entities, and should not sell books or uniforms, including via "selected vendors".
  - Later circulars and a Delhi HC ruling shifted the position. CBSE then asked schools to sell only NCERT books on premises.
  - Sources: [CBSE circular 2018-19 "Commercialisation"](https://saras.cbse.gov.in/saras/Circulars/\2018-19Circular\Circular\Commercialisation.pdf); [Scroll](https://scroll.in/latest/862224/scroll_in)

### Inferences
- **[Estimate]** Where a commission exists, it typically sits at **15-40% of MRP** and is funded from the bookseller's margin. That is why publisher discounts reach 55-65%.
- **[Estimate]** A new entrant matching incumbents' "school margin" through a booklist pack would realise only about 35-45% of MRP. It would also take on criminal exposure (the MP precedent) and NHRC/state scrutiny.
- The defensible alternative is a transparent institutional discount or a documented school service fee, i.e. Seshadri's "10% to institutions". The reputational and legal trend since 2024 favours that over undisclosed commissions.

### Gaps
- No primary source quantified commissions as a % for CBSE/ICSE K-8 supplementary titles specifically. Reported figures cover all books and supplies, or are parent allegations.
- I did not retrieve the official texts of the Karnataka, Haryana or Maharashtra orders, so penalty terms are unconfirmed.
- No specific provision in state fee-regulation acts that covers commissions was confirmed in this pass.

## 3. Specimen/sample copies, sales-team cost, and sales cycle

### Takeaway
Free specimen copies to decision-makers are universal and are cited as a reason school books cost more. But no source quantifies copies per school or sampling and sales cost as a % of revenue. The selling season is concentrated: S Chand books **80-85% of annual revenue in Q4 (Jan-Mar)**, so sampling and adoption decisions must land before January.

### Cited Findings
- Publishers "leave as many sample books as possible as free copies with the decision makers", which "increases the cost of selling" (Seshadri, Oct 2022). — [All About Book Publishing](https://allaboutbookpublishing.com/9876/school-book-publishing-calling-for-an-imaginative-turnaround/)
- A Booklink article lists free samples to schools, teacher resources and workshops among the extras that add to private-publisher book cost. No figure is given. — [Booklink](https://booklink.in/archive/317-education-in-india-a-bullet-train-on-a-broad-gauge.html)
- Oswaal Books offers free specimen copies to private- and government-school teachers year-round "to review the material before deciding on bulk purchases". — [Oswaal Books](https://oswaalbooks.com/pages/specimen-books-for-teachers)
- Delhi Samagra Shiksha library procurement caps sample submissions at five per category, and samples are not returned. — [Delhi DoE notice 2025-26](https://edudel.nic.in/upload/upload_2025_26/1527_28_dt_31072025.PDF)
- S Chand: "our sale season primarily is Jan to March… 80-85% of our annual number we do in that Q4." (Head IR Atul Soni, May 2025) — [S Chand Q4 FY25 call transcript](https://files.tijorifinance.com/insight/india/7264/Conference%20Call/CC-Mar25.pdf)
- S Chand FY26 commentary says 70-80% of annual revenue is typically booked in Q4 as K-12 school deliveries peak. — [search summary of FY26 results; Business News Today](https://business-news-today.com/why-did-s-chand-stock-fall-despite-best-ever-results-and-rs-1036m-net-cash/)

### Inferences
- **[Estimate]** Sales cycle: specimen distribution and teacher meetings from about Oct to Dec, booklist finalisation about Dec to Feb, bulk supply to booksellers Jan to Mar, and parent purchase Mar to Apr. Cash arrives later (section 4). This matches the S Chand Q4 concentration; the October start is an assumption.
- **[Estimate, unsourced]** Planning assumptions:
  - Sampling: 3-10 specimen sets per school (one per class teacher plus coordinator and principal).
  - Field sales and sampling together: 10-20% of net revenue for a small publisher.
  - Treat both as placeholders to replace with CodeSchool's own pilot data.

### Gaps
- No source found for copies per school, specimen budgets, sales-rep cost or marketing as % of revenue for any Indian school publisher. S Chand's annual-report expense notes would give "advertisement and sales promotion", but the FY25 annual report PDF timed out on fetch.

## 4. Credit periods, payment cycles, returns, bad debts and working capital

### Takeaway
The channel runs on credit and sale-or-return. S Chand's year-end receivables were **140 days in FY25, rising to 160 days in FY26**; inventory was 223 days and net working capital 151 days in FY25. Management says credit discipline, not demand, is what limits its growth. Returns are material enough that S Chand caps them by policy and provisions for them. A small publisher should expect cash about 4-6 months after supply, and must finance print and inventory for most of a year.

### Cited Findings
- S Chand Q4 FY25 (May 2025) — [Q4 FY25 call transcript](https://files.tijorifinance.com/insight/india/7264/Conference%20Call/CC-Mar25.pdf):
  - Trade receivables were Rs 2,753m, or **140 days** (143 days in FY24), "the lowest Q4 receivable days in the company's history".
  - Inventory was 223 days (284 days in FY24).
  - Net working capital was 151 days (157 days in FY24).
- S Chand CFO Saurabh Mittal (May 2025) on credit — [same transcript](https://files.tijorifinance.com/insight/india/7264/Conference%20Call/CC-Mar25.pdf):
  - "since we do a lot of credit sales, we do not want to… work with channel partners who would eventually delay payments".
  - "we've had to say 'no' to a lot of channel partners who have not cleared previous accounts."
  - He said faster growth "means a lot of cash burn".
- On returns, the CFO said S Chand has "strict policies in terms of sales return, which we have pegged to a certain level, beyond which we do not take back". He also said returns had dropped sharply over five years by working with better customers. In 2018, "about 4,000 channel partners… was causing a lot of sales return… a lot of delay in payments". (May 2025) — [same transcript](https://files.tijorifinance.com/insight/india/7264/Conference%20Call/CC-Mar25.pdf)
- S Chand FY26: receivable days rose to 160 (from 140) and NWC days to 164, with management expecting normalisation in H1 FY27. — [search summary of FY26 call; financialreports.eu](https://financialreports.eu/filings/s-chand-and-company-limited/call-transcript/2026/47858706/)
- S Chand's FY25 annual report describes a network of over 4,000 distributors and channel partners. That conflicts with the CFO's framing that the 4,000 figure was the 2018 level. — [S Chand AR FY25 (NSE)](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/annual_reports/AR_28103_SCHAND_2024_2025_A_10964806_01092025230351.pdf)
- Navneet receivable days differ by source and definition. All are much lower than S Chand, as Navneet is mainly Maharashtra/Gujarat state-board guides with a cash-heavier dealer channel:
  - 42 days at 31 Dec 2024.
  - 56 days at H1 FY25.
  - About 73-90 days at Mar 2025.
  - PL research says 80-85% of publication revenue comes from Maharashtra and Gujarat.
  - Sources: [Trendlyne](https://trendlyne.com/latest-news/only-news/915/NAVNETEDUL/navneet-education-ltd); [Value Research](https://www.valueresearchonline.com/stocks/43488/navneet-education-ltd/key-ratios/); [PL India note May 2025](https://www.plindia.com/ResReport/NELI-21-5-25-PL.pdf)
- Distributors may return unsold copies, deduct damaged stock and delay payment. Offline bookstores have higher-than-normal return rates. No % is given. (Feb 2026) — [IIP Books blog](https://iipbooks.com/blogs/view/distributor-margins-in-india-how-book-distribution-really-works)
- Seshadri recommends ending supply on credit for a cash-and-carry model, under which "returns of unsold books would become very minimal". This implies returns are significant under the current credit model. (Oct 2022) — [All About Book Publishing](https://allaboutbookpublishing.com/9876/school-book-publishing-calling-for-an-imaginative-turnaround/)

### Inferences
- **[Estimate]** For a small publisher without S Chand's bargaining power, plan on:
  - Payment 120-180 days after Jan-Mar supply, so cash in May to Sept.
  - Returns of 5-15% of gross supply under sale-or-return. Supplementary booklist titles are adopted per school, so over-supply to a bookseller comes back after the April buying window.
  - Bad debts of 2-5% of sales.
- **[Estimate]** At about 12% annual cost of capital on 150-160 days of receivables, plus a print run financed from about Nov-Dec, financing cost is about 5-7% of revenue.
- Seasonality compounds the problem. With 80-85% of sales in one quarter, the whole year's print cost is paid upfront, and revenue arrives about two quarters after delivery.

### Gaps
- No public sales-return % for S Chand or any school publisher. S Chand's AR revenue note ("contract price less discounts/returns") was not retrieved because the PDF fetch timed out.
- No source gives standard credit terms (e.g. 90/120/180 days) in distributor contracts for small publishers.
- No bad-debt % found.

## 5. Direct-to-school and direct-to-parent models and their margins

### Takeaway
Direct routes exist but are now politically exposed or still pay big platform discounts.
- **School shop / campus kits:** these are being banned or restricted (Karnataka, Punjab, CBSE non-NCERT, Telangana). Where they still run, schools keep 40-50%.
- **Online marketplaces:** local booksellers say online sellers get discounts of up to 50%.
- **Publisher e-store:** no margin data was found.

### Cited Findings
- Schools selling bundles from campus earn "substantial profits", and schools now demand high discounts (Seshadri, Oct 2022). — [All About Book Publishing](https://allaboutbookpublishing.com/9876/school-book-publishing-calling-for-an-imaginative-turnaround/)
- Online sellers reportedly get up to 50% discount from publishers on school textbooks, versus under 40% for local shops (Bhopal booksellers' claim). — [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/bhopal/booksellers-oppose-online-sale-of-school-textbooks)
- Campus sales are banned in Karnataka and Punjab. Delhi requires at least five vendor options listed on school websites (2025, reaffirmed Apr 2026). — sources in section 2.
- A market-research summary says the shift online creates channel conflict with traditional distributors and retailers, which makes it hard for publishers to balance volume and profit. — [Ken Research](https://www.kenresearch.com/india-books-publishing-retail-distribution-and-stationery-market)

### Inferences
- **[Estimate]** Direct-to-parent through a publisher e-store, with the school publishing the booklist and parents buying from any vendor (the route the Delhi and Maharashtra orders favour): realisation of about 75-85% of MRP before shipping and payment fees.
  - Last-mile shipping on a 1-2 kg pack is likely Rs 60-120.
  - That is 13-27% of a Rs 449 pack, so the shipping figure is a placeholder to verify.
- **[Estimate]** Marketplace: at a 40-50% effective discount or fee, realisation is about 50-60% of MRP.
- **[Estimate]** A school-collected kit (school bills parents and pays the publisher) avoids distributor margin but brings back the commission and legality issue. It also puts collection timing in the school's hands.

### Gaps
- Amazon/Flipkart book-category fees, shipping costs and returns were not researched in this pass.
- No data found on publisher e-store share or conversion for school booklist titles.

## 7. Edtech school-licence economics (Rs 300-1,000/student digital licence) for comparison

### Takeaway
Integrated K-12 edtech providers (e.g. LEAD) charge schools a per-enrolled-student annual fee, usually on multi-year (about 3-year) contracts. The school collects from parents and pays the provider, so the provider carries the school's fee-collection risk. Sector sources point to widespread late fee collection. Receivables management contributed to Educomp's insolvency. No rupee-per-student prices or invoice terms were found.

### Cited Findings
- LEAD School: schools pay a per-enrolled-student annual fee, typically on three-year contracts. The bundle includes courseware, software, hardware and a school kit. This comes from secondary business-model summaries, not filings. — [Lapaas Voice](https://lapaasvoice.com/startup/lead-school/business-model/); [YourStory (Dec 2021)](https://yourstory.com/2021/12/lead-transform-affordable-private-schools-reimagine-classrooms-digitising-pedagogy)
- LEAD's own FAQ (Jul 2022) presents better parent willingness to pay fees on time as a benefit to partner schools. That implies the provider's revenue depends on school fee collection. — [LEAD School FAQ](https://leadschool.in/faqs/how-lead-helps-its-partner-schools-with-fee-collections)
- A payments vendor claims up to 80% of institutions face significant fee delays, with over half of annual cash flows arriving more than 60 days late. This is a vendor's marketing figure. — [Innoviti](https://innoviti.com/blogs/how-schools-and-educational-institutions-can-reduce-payment-failures-and-revenue-leakage/)
- Receivables management was among the major reasons for listed edtech firm Educomp Solutions entering insolvency resolution. — [Mynd Solution](https://www.myndsolution.com/best-practices/managing-fa-for-educational-institutions-and-edtech-in-india/)

### Inferences
- **[Estimate]** A school-billed digital licence avoids distributor and bookseller margins, so the provider may keep 80-100% of the price net of any sales commission. Collection is the risk instead:
  - The school invoices parents across instalments (quarterly or termly fees).
  - The provider is paid in tranches or after the session starts.
  - Expect 60-180 days of slippage and partial non-payment.
- **[Estimate]** A hybrid that sells the printed pack through parent-facing channels and bills the digital licence to the school splits the risks. Book margin is lost to the channel, and the licence carries the school's credit risk.

### Gaps
- No rupee-per-student licence prices, invoice timing or sales-commission rates were found for LEAD, Next Education, Extramarks or similar.
- GST treatment of a book-plus-licence bundle was not researched. Books and digital services are taxed differently, so it should be confirmed with a tax adviser.
