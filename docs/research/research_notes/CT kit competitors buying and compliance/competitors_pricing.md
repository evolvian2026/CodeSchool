# Competitors and pricing for CT, coding and AI curricula sold to Indian K-8 schools (CBSE CT&AI, 2026-27)

All prices were seen on the pages cited between 1 and 6 November 2026, unless a page gives its own date. Retail and booklist prices are a snapshot. Vendor statements are marked "(claim)".

## Q1. Textbook publishers: CBSE CT&AI-aligned books for 2026-27 (series, classes, MRP, add-ons, alignment claims)

### Takeaway
The big trade publishers that have confirmed CT&AI books are Oxford University Press India (a six-book Classes 3-8 series, Rs 305 for Class 8) and Goyal Brothers ("Think Smart", Rs 270 for Class 6). Arihant has a Class 6 textbook too. A long tail of small or self-published workbooks costs Rs 179-599. Uolo's Tekie.AI books are on a 2026-27 school booklist at Rs 579-629 per class. Printed CT&AI books cost about Rs 270-630 per student per year. That is the same band as CodeSchool's planned Rs 200-800 licence, so a book-only offer competes head-on with print.

### Inferences
- OUP is the most credible trade-publisher entrant with a full Classes 3-8 series priced near Rs 300 per book. Expect other incumbents (Kips, Orange, Eupheus, Madhubun) to follow for 2027-28 even where no title was found yet.
- The Tovira "Student Handbook" listings use the same title as CBSE's free handbooks. Their stated publication date (Dec 2025) is earlier than CBSE's March 2026 first edition. They may be reprints or unofficial compilations, so treat them with caution.
- Print CT&AI books (Rs 270-630) are roughly what parents already pay per child per year. A CodeSchool licence at Rs 200-400 undercuts a book, while Rs 600-800 matches a premium book-plus-platform such as Tekie.AI.

### Gaps

## Q2. School-facing edtechs and lab providers: what they sell, prices, claimed school counts

### Takeaway
Uolo Tekie is the closest public price benchmark: from Rs 799 per student per year for Grades 1-8, covering book, coding platform, teacher portal and training (claim). STEMpedia sells books plus PictoBlox credits at Rs 440-480. LEAD Group bundles coding into its school system. Lab and ATL vendors (Avishkaar, STEMROBO, Scaleopal) sell infrastructure at lakh-rupee scale. Most edtechs (Next Education, Extramarks, Codingal, Tinkerly, etc.) publish no school prices.

## Q3. Free competitors active in India

### Takeaway
The main free substitute is CBSE's own package: twelve handbooks, free tools named in the framework, and free teacher training. CSpathshala (CC BY lesson plans) and Raspberry Pi's Experience AI (an India launch in July 2026 via University of Southampton Delhi) add free curriculum. Free material covers CT puzzles and AI awareness, but it does not provide school-ready projects, rubrics or progress tracking.

### Cited Findings
**CBSE**
- Free Student and Teacher Handbooks for Classes 3-8 — [EdTechReview](https://www.edtechreview.in/trends-insights/insights/cbse-ai-computational-thinking-curriculum-classes-3-8/)
- Tools named in the framework: Scratch (where equipment exists), Teachable Machine, and Machine Learning for Kids for Class 8 no-code AI — [EdTechReview](https://www.edtechreview.in/trends-insights/insights/cbse-ai-computational-thinking-curriculum-classes-3-8/)

**Teacher training (free or near-free)**
- District Level Deliberations: one-day, about 40 teachers each, 6 CPD hours; more than 450 scheduled as of August 2026 — [EdTechReview](https://www.edtechreview.in/trends-insights/insights/cbse-ai-computational-thinking-curriculum-classes-3-8/)
- The 3030 Eklavya series with IIT Gandhinagar's Centre for Creative Learning (CCL) started 15 August 2026: eight live Sunday sessions in Hindi. It is free, with an optional certificate at Rs 200 plus taxes — [EdTechReview](https://www.edtechreview.in/trends-insights/insights/cbse-ai-computational-thinking-curriculum-classes-3-8/)
- A CBSE regional workshop charges a Rs 700 online registration fee — [CBSE notification (PDF)](https://www.cbse.gov.in/cbsenew/documents/Notification_CT_AI_2026_27_09042026.pdf)

**CSpathshala (ACM India)**
- "200+ lesson plans" including slides and student workbooks, free under CC BY. A draft Grades 1-8 curriculum has been developed — [CSpathshala](https://cspathshala.org/)

**Experience AI (Raspberry Pi Foundation, with Google DeepMind)**
- India launch on 13 July 2026 through the University of Southampton Delhi, with Tatras Data training teachers. Materials are lesson plans, presentations, videos, activities and assessments, for public and private schools. It is described as the Foundation's first partnership of this kind in India — [University of Southampton Delhi](https://www.delhi.southampton.ac.uk/news/press-release-experience-ai-educational-programme-comes-to-classrooms-in-india-through-raspberry-pi-foundation-and-university-of-southampton-delhi-partnership/)
- Earlier Raspberry Pi work in India: teacher training with Mo School and Pratham in Odisha, and Code Clubs in four states (2023) — [Raspberry Pi blog, Odisha](https://www.raspberrypi.org/blog/tag/odisha/)

### Inferences
- Free materials mean CodeSchool cannot charge for content alone. Paid value has to come from the delivery layer: ready lesson scripts, project kits, rubrics, auto-tracked progress, and reports a principal can show inspectors.

### Gaps
- India-specific 2026 activity was not verified for Code.org/CodeAI, MIT Day of AI, Intel AI for Youth, Microsoft, Google, or Bebras India (CSpathshala runs Bebras India; 2026 participation figures not found).

## Q4. Competitor gaps that match CBSE's missing pieces, and who claims to fill them

### Takeaway
CBSE has not published marks weightage, a report-card format, or project rubrics (marked "coming soon" in April 2026). Few vendors claim to fill these gaps. PiyushAI claims rubrics and teacher manuals. Thinking Juggernaut claims activities and online assessment. Tekie claims auto-grading and a teacher portal. No vendor was found claiming coding activities mapped to NCERT Maths chapters, device-light ML labs, or a student progress journal.

### Cited Findings
- **Missing from CBSE:** no published marks weightage and no report-card format. Rubrics for the full project list were "coming soon" at the April 2026 webinar — [EdTechReview](https://www.edtechreview.in/trends-insights/insights/cbse-ai-computational-thinking-curriculum-classes-3-8/)
- **CBSE assessment model:** continuous and formative. Classes 3-5 use CT puzzles in written tests and a teacher observation journal. Classes 6-8 add practicals, projects and reflections — [EdTechReview](https://www.edtechreview.in/trends-insights/insights/cbse-ai-computational-thinking-curriculum-classes-3-8/)
- **Infrastructure:** under UDISE+ 2025-26, 36.5% of schools have functional desktops (25.5% of government schools, 63.2% of private unaided schools) and 67.4% have internet — [EdTechReview](https://www.edtechreview.in/trends-insights/insights/cbse-ai-computational-thinking-curriculum-classes-3-8/)
- **Vendor framing (Scaleopal):** one-day trainings give no session plans for the 40 AI Literacy hours, there is no structured guidance for interdisciplinary projects, and schools must configure free AI tools themselves. Classes 6-8 need a lab of at least 15 workstations (claim) — [Scaleopal Labs](https://labs.scaleopal.com/insights/cbse-ct-ai-curriculum-classes-3-8-school-setup)
- **Who claims to fill gaps:**
  - PiyushAI: chapter-aligned workbooks for NCERT Maths, Science and TWAU, teacher manuals with assessment rubrics, an AI handbook and teacher certification (claims) — [PiyushAI](https://piyushai.com/computational-thinking-and-artificial-intelligence-for-cbse/)
  - Thinking Juggernaut: AI Kit with 20+ activities, online assessment and certificate, and teacher training (claim) — [Thinking Juggernaut](https://thinkingjuggernaut.in/blog/cbse-computational-thinking-ai-curriculum-classes-2026-27)
  - Uolo Tekie: hands-on projects, auto-grading and a teacher portal (claim) — [Uolo](https://theuolo.com/tekie.html)
  - STEMpedia: 25 lab activities per book, but it needs PictoBlox and devices — [STEMpedia](https://thestempedia.com/shop/skillful-minds-computer-book-class-6/)
- **Critique:** R. Ramanujam (Azim Premji University) argues that teachers are ill-prepared — [EdTechReview](https://www.edtechreview.in/trends-insights/insights/cbse-ai-computational-thinking-curriculum-classes-3-8/)

### Inferences
- **Open white space:**
  - Rubric-scored project kits for the two 20-hour projects per year in Classes 6-8.
  - A CBSE-format assessment and progress journal that fills the missing report-card format.
  - Unplugged or single-device ML labs for the roughly 63% of schools without functional desktops. In the private unaided segment that share is about 37%.
- PiyushAI is the only vendor found claiming "NCERT chapter-aligned plus rubrics", but it lists no prices and its digital portal is not yet live. That is a weak incumbent CodeSchool could beat on execution.

### Gaps
- Product samples were not inspected, so it is unverified whether any vendor's "rubrics" or "projects" actually map to CBSE's project list.

## Q5. Price benchmarks for comparable school spend

### Takeaway
Per-student benchmarks:
- Printed CT&AI or computer books: Rs 179-630.
- Book-plus-platform programmes: Rs 579-799+ (Tekie).
- ATL-style labs: about Rs 6.5-7 lakh in equipment per school, roughly Rs 10,000+ per student for a 60-student batch.

CodeSchool's Rs 200-800 per student per year sits inside the book and book-plus-platform band and far below lab spend.

### Inferences
- Schools already pass about Rs 300-630 per child per year to parents through booklists for computer or CT books. A CodeSchool licence of Rs 300-600 billed through the booklist, or bundled with a slim workbook, fits existing buying habits.

### Gaps
- No published per-student annual fees were found for private-school robotics or coding programmes (for example Avishkaar, Tinkerly, Robotix, Makers' Muse). These are typically quoted per school.
- No 2026-dated GeM ATL package award prices were retrieved.
