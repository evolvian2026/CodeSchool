# How Indian private (CBSE) schools buy books and digital academic products, and the rules that constrain edtech sales (current to Nov 2026)

Scope note: Research done with web search and one page fetch (about 16 tool calls). The official CBSE bye-laws PDF and the circular PDFs were not fetched in full, so clause numbers and dates for some rules come from press or secondary sources and are marked as such. Status labels: **[BINDING]** statute, bye-law or notified rule; **[GUIDANCE]** circular, advisory or "strongly advised"; **[PRACTICE]** reported market behaviour.

## Buying process and calendar (who decides, when, how publishers and edtechs sell, margins and commissions, sahodaya and chains)

### Takeaway
Independent sources on how decisions are made are thin. What the sources do show is a market where publishers send sales reps to visit principals and teachers with specimen copies, and where schools reportedly earn large commissions (about 30-60%) on non-NCERT books sold on campus or through a nominated shop. A per-student CT/AI product packaged like a "book" would land in this commission-driven channel, and that channel is under heavy regulatory pressure.

### Cited Findings
- [PRACTICE] Publishers' sales representatives hand specimen copies to teachers and principals at schools, at their homes or at coaching institutes. — [Ashirwad Publication](https://www.ashirwadpublication.com/)
- [PRACTICE] Specimen copies are tied to the subject a teacher teaches and often need proof, such as a principal's authorisation. Requests go through online forms with verification. — [Oswal Publishers](https://oswalpublishers.com/specimen-copy/); [MTG Learning Media](https://mtg.in/specimen_copy/); [Vidyabhartee](https://vidyabhartee.com/pages/request-specimen-copy)
- [PRACTICE] Some publishers now give free digital specimen copies to verified educators across India. — [Future Topper](https://www.futuretopper.in/teacher-specimen-copy)
- [PRACTICE] Publishers also reach teachers through teacher events and workshops, and through exhibitions at educator conferences. — [Oswaal Books teachers' event form](https://oswaalbooks.com/pages/teachers-event-form); [Nature Nurture publisher guide](https://www.naturenurture.org/school-book-publishers)
- [PRACTICE] Commission figures: booksellers reportedly get only 15-20% commission on NCERT books, while other publishers pay 30-40%. — [Journey Line, "Game of commission in books"](https://www.journeyline.in/newsdet.aspx?q=254871)
- [PRACTICE] An official in Kashmir put the commission at about 40-60% on books sold on school premises or at designated bookshops (2018 report). — [Kashmir Vision, 31 Oct 2018](https://kashmirvision.in/2018/10/31/text-books-prescribed-by-private-schools-cost-a-fortune/)
- [PRACTICE] A Tribune letter claims some schools earn up to 80% profit on some books. This is opinion and anecdote. — [The Tribune letters, 22 Apr 2014](https://www.tribuneindia.com/2014/20140422/letters.htm)
- [PRACTICE] Price-gap example: a 256-page NCERT book costs Rs 65 while a 167-page private publisher book costs Rs 305. Schools are accused of changing books every year to keep the commissions coming. — [NewsBlaze India](https://newsblaze.in/india/education-ripoff-game-of-book-commissions_4757)
- [PRACTICE] In Sirsa (Haryana), a government crackdown followed complaints that schools were making deals with publishers and earning commissions. — [The Tribune, Sirsa](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/haryana/sirsa-pvt-schools-under-fire-for-forcing-costly-books-on-students-govt-cracks-down/amp)
- Market context: about half of India's K-12 students are in private schools. More than 73% of government-school students get free textbooks, while most private-school students buy their own. — [EY-Parthenon / Association of Publishers in India](https://www.ey.com/content/dam/ey-unified-site/ey-com/en-in/insights/strategy-transactions/documents/ey-value-proposition-of-the-indian-publishing.pdf)
- [PRACTICE] B2B2C edtech model, using LEAD as an example: schools pay for the integrated platform and parents pay extra for premium add-ons. Typical Indian per-student annual licences are cited at Rs 500 to Rs 5,000+, which is a vendor-blog figure. — [upGrowth GTM guide](https://upgrowth.in/edtech-gtm-b2c-b2b-b2b2c-customer-type/); [CoSchool comparison](https://www.coschool.ai/feeds/blog/edtech-platform-schools-india)
- Next Education says it has 18,000+ partner schools and uses "institutional pricing or partnership model". It publishes no prices. — [CoSchool comparison](https://www.coschool.ai/feeds/blog/edtech-platform-schools-india)

### Inferences
- A Rs 200-800 per-student product sits in the same price band as one to three private-publisher textbooks. If it goes on the book list, the school or bookseller will probably expect a margin similar to the 30-40% reported for private publishers, so net realisation could fall to roughly Rs 120-560 per student. This is an estimate, not a sourced figure.
- April sessions and recurring "book changes every year" suggest decisions are made in the winter before the session. That fits the commonly cited December-February window, but no source found confirms it (see Gaps).
- The owner or management likely controls commission-linked decisions, and teachers or coordinators influence the shortlist through specimens. The sources show reps targeting both groups, but none breaks down decision rights.

### Gaps
- No independent source found giving the December-February book-list finalisation calendar, or showing how decision rights split between owner, principal, coordinator and subject heads.
- No source found on Sahodaya school complexes as a buying or influence channel, or on how school chains run central procurement.
- No Federation of Educational Publishers in India (FEPI) interview, and no systematic survey of distributor and bookseller margins was found. All commission figures are anecdotal or regional, and several are from before 2020.
- No LEAD, Next Education or similar margin data, and nothing confirming how per-student fees are collected through schools.

## CBSE rules on textbooks, compelled purchases, commercial activity, and digital products

### Takeaway
After the 2024 revision, CBSE requires NCERT textbooks for Classes 9-12 [BINDING], but for Classes 1-8 it only "strongly advises" NCERT or SCERT books [GUIDANCE]. Private-publisher books and digital content therefore remain legally usable in Classes 1-8, provided they are vetted, aligned with NCF-SE, and published on the school website with a declaration signed by the principal and manager. Separately, CBSE has prohibited coercing parents to buy books, uniforms or stationery from the school or from particular vendors since 2016-17, and courts have accepted on-campus sales only when there is no coercion. No 2025-2026 CBSE circular on books or edtech purchasing was found.

### Cited Findings
- [BINDING, original 2018 text] The 2018 Affiliation Bye-Laws (clause 2.4.7, per secondary sources) say schools "may prescribe NCERT text books in the classes and subjects in which these have been published by the NCERT", and ask for "extreme care" in choosing private publishers' books. — [CBSE Affiliation Bye-Laws page](https://www.cbse.gov.in/cbsenew/aff-bye-laws.html); [SchoolServ, bye-laws 2018](https://www.schoolserv.in/cbse-affiliation-bye-laws-2018/)
- [BINDING, 2024 amendment] The revised rule reads: "Schools shall mandatorily follow the NCERT textbooks prescribed in CBSE curriculum" for Classes 9-12. For Classes 1-8, schools are "strongly advised" to follow NCERT or SCERT books. Where neither exists, the CBSE books uploaded on its website should be followed. — [Careers360, 14 Aug 2024](https://news.careers360.com/cbse-mandates-ncert-textbooks-for-classes-9-12-in-schools-revises-guidelines/amp); [Career Power](https://www.careerpower.in/school/news/cbse-revised-guidelines-to-mandates-ncert-textbooks-for-class-ix-to-xii)
- [BINDING, 2024 amendment] "Extreme care should be taken while using supplementary material, digital content as well as books of private publishers". Supplementary material must be aligned with NCF-SE. Schools must post prescribed book lists online with a declaration signed by the manager and principal, and CBSE will act against books with objectionable content. — [Careers360, 14 Aug 2024](https://news.careers360.com/cbse-mandates-ncert-textbooks-for-classes-9-12-in-schools-revises-guidelines/amp); [FELA summary](https://www.thefela.org/news/details/new-cbse-guidelines-ncert-textbooks-what-you-need-know)
- The website-upload condition reportedly comes from an earlier bye-law amendment made after a 2018 Madras High Court matter: schools may prescribe non-NCERT books only if the list is on their website, signed by the principal and manager. This is a secondary source. — [Go4Reviews, Jan 2020](https://go4reviews.in/2020/01/are-ncert-books-really-mandatory-for-cbse-schools/)
- [GUIDANCE/CIRCULAR] CBSE circular No. CBSE/AFF/Circular/10/2017 of 19 Apr 2017 tells schools to "desist from the unhealthy practice of coercing parents to buy text books, note books, stationary, uniforms, shoes, school bags etc from within" the premises or from selected vendors. — [CBSE circular PDF, 19 Apr 2017](https://www.cbse.gov.in/cbsenew/Examination_Circular/2017/30_CIRCULAR.pdf); [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/cbse-warn-pvt-schools-coercing-2000851)
- [GUIDANCE] An April 2016 CBSE circular told schools not to force children to buy textbooks other than NCERT/CBSE books. CBSE allowed small school outlets to supply only NCERT books and stationery, and parents remain free to buy anywhere. — [The Tribune, Ludhiana](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/archive/ludhiana/parents-not-bound-to-buy-books-from-one-shop-only-563819/); [The Tribune, Haryana](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/haryana/schools-warned-against-prescribing-non-ncert-books-495320)
- [GUIDANCE] A CBSE advisory warns that action will be taken against schools running commercial activities such as selling books, uniforms and stationery to students. A school can prescribe books but cannot require purchase from a particular vendor. — [Nagpur Today](https://www.nagpurtoday.in/cbse-advises-schools-against-commercial-activity-of-selling-books-stationary-and-uniform-issues-circular/) (page date is unclear; the content appears to be older); [Hindustan Times via PressReader, 11 Apr 2018](https://www.pressreader.com/india/hindustan-times-st-jaipur/20180411/281603831044714)
- [BINDING, case law] In 2018 the Delhi High Court held that sales of NCERT and non-NCERT books in school shops are not commercialisation if there is no coercion. Publishers welcomed the ruling. — [Careers360, publishers welcome HC decision](https://news.careers360.com/publishers-welcome-high-court-decision-non-ncert-books/amp)
- Booksellers have publicly opposed CBSE notices as biased. — [The Tribune, Jalandhar](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/archive/jalandhar/cbse-notice-biased-say-book-sellers-410295)
- [PRACTICE vs RULE] Reports show schools continuing to sell books and uniforms on premises despite the orders. — [The Tribune, "Defying orders"](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/archive/features/defying-orders-private-schools-sell-books-uniforms-on-premises-387901)

### Inferences
- For Classes 1-8, a CT/coding/AI workbook or digital licence is permissible as "supplementary material / digital content". It must be NCF-SE aligned, free of objectionable content, and listed on the school's signed website book list. Including it is a school choice, not a CBSE requirement.
- Any model that forces parents to buy the product from the school or one vendor is exposed under CBSE's 2017 anti-coercion circular and under state rules (next section). Licence codes sold only through the school are effectively single-vendor, which is a real compliance grey zone.
- CBSE has no edtech-specific procurement guidance (none found), so a school's purchase of a digital product falls under general rules: fee regulation, anti-coercion and the content-vetting duty.

### Gaps
- The full text and exact clause and date of the 2024 amendment notification were not retrieved from cbse.gov.in. Careers360 calls it a "proposed amended rule", so whether it is final should be checked against the current bye-laws PDF.
- No CBSE circulars from 2023-2026 on non-NCERT books or book-selling were found. None found on edtech or digital-product purchasing.
- The specific bye-law clauses on commercial activity and fee transparency (for example, which clause bars profit-making or requires fee disclosure) were not confirmed.

## State fee regulation and extra charges (computer, smart-class, bundled digital fees)

### Takeaway
State fee laws increasingly restrict schools to approved fee heads, set up parent-inclusive fee committees, and require refunds of unapproved charges. Delhi's 2025 Act is the strictest and newest. A "smart class" or "digital product" charge that is not an approved fee head is at risk. In 2026 several states (Delhi, Maharashtra, Rajasthan, J&K) explicitly barred forcing parents to buy books or uniforms from specific vendors.

### Cited Findings
- [BINDING] Delhi: the Delhi School Education (Transparency in Fixation and Regulation of Fees) Act, 2025 was passed on 8 Aug 2025 and published in the Gazette on 14 Aug 2025. — [PRS India, Act 4 of 2025](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_states/delhi/2025/Act4of2025DL.pdf)
- [BINDING] The Act came into force by notification dated 10.12.2025, and its Rules were notified the same day. — [Delhi DoE order, 24 Dec 2025](https://edudel.nic.in/upload/upload_2025_26/9744_52_dt_24122025.PDF); [TeamLease RegTech](https://teamleaseregtech.com/updates/article/50600/govt-of-delhi-issued-the-delhi-school-education-transparency-in-fixati/)
- [BINDING] Under the Delhi Act, schools may charge only defined fee heads. Unapproved fees, arbitrary "miscellaneous" collections and capitation fees are prohibited, and every school must form a School Level Fee Regulation Committee (SLFRC). — [Delhi DoE order](https://edudel.nic.in/upload/upload_2025_26/9744_52_dt_24122025.PDF); [Advocate Gandhi overview](https://advocategandhi.com/comprehensive-overview-of-the-delhi-school-education-fees-regulation-and-management-of-transparency-rules-2025/)
- [BINDING] Parents effectively hold a veto over fee hikes. Excess fees must be refunded within 20 working days. There are three tiers of committee: school, district, and a Revision Committee as final authority. — [News on AIR, 9 Aug 2025](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/delhi-assembly-passes-bill-granting-parents-veto-on-school-fee-hikes)
- [STATUS 2026] On 2 Feb 2026 Delhi told the Supreme Court it would implement the Act from the 2026-27 session. Private school groups challenged the Act in the Delhi High Court. A later directive required SLFRCs by 15 July, and under Delhi HC interim orders schools must charge 2025-26 fees until new structures are approved. — [NewKerala](https://www.newkerala.com/news/a/delhi-govt-directs-private-unaided-schools-set-up-113.htm); [Delhi DoE acts and rules page](https://www.edudel.nic.in/edu/actsandrules.html) (lists a Removal of Difficulties Order, 2026)
- [BINDING/DIRECTIVE] Delhi DoE (1 Apr 2026): private unaided schools must not compel parents to buy books or uniforms from specific vendors, and must upload the year's list of books and writing material to their websites. — [Careers360, Delhi directive](https://news.careers360.com/delhi-government-directive-private-schools-books-uniforms-specific-vendors-ban-doe-guidelines-dsear-rte-rules-parent-complaints/amp)
- [DIRECTIVE] An earlier Delhi DoE order said private schools cannot force parents to buy costly books, or change uniforms within three years. — [Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/private-schools-cant-force-parents-buy-costly-books-change-uniform-in-next-3-years-doe/amp)
- [DIRECTIVE] Maharashtra (15 Apr 2026): the state education directorate ordered schools to pass a resolution, from 2026-27, that they will not force purchase of uniforms or school material from specific shops. — [Sanatan Prabhat](https://sanatanprabhat.org/english/169614.html) (a partisan outlet; corroboration recommended)
- [BINDING] Rajasthan: the Rajasthan Schools (Regulation of Fee) Act, 2016 governs fees through school-level committees. The Supreme Court upheld it, and in a related matter asked about 36,000 Rajasthan private schools to charge 15% less (COVID-era). — [India Code, Act 14 of 2016](https://indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/18865/1/14_of_2016.pdf); [Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/sc-asks-36000-private-schools-of-rajasthan-charge-15-less-fees-from-students/amp)
- [GUIDANCE/DIRECTIVE] Rajasthan education department guidelines: no fee hike for three academic years (reported "till 2027"). A parent-teacher committee fixes fees and its members' details are posted on the school portal. Charging above the approved fee is illegal and excess must be refunded. Parents may buy uniforms and books in the open market. Textbook selection must follow board norms (RBSE, CBSE, CISCE, CAIE). — [India.com](https://www.india.com/education/no-fee-hike-in-private-schools-for-three-years-rajasthan-education-dept-issues-10-new-guidelines-6933779/); [EdInbox](https://edinbox.com/news/1003-breaking-no-fee-hike-for-rajasthan-private-schools-till-2027/)
- [DIRECTIVE] J&K: the Fee Fixation and Regulation Committee (FFRC) told private schools not to compel parents to buy private-publisher textbooks, noting that affiliating bodies prescribe NCERT books. The Director of School Education Jammu barred forcing purchases from particular shops, citing action under Section 27(2) of the Act. — [Greater Kashmir](https://www.greaterkashmir.com/education/dont-compel-parents-to-purchase-textbooks-from-private-publishers-ffrc-directs-private-schools-11736751); [Careers360, J&K](https://news.careers360.com/dont-force-students-buy-books-uniforms-from-particular-shop-j-k-admin-private-schools/amp)
- [ENFORCEMENT] Punjab and Haryana HC-linked case (Ludhiana): parents sought a Rs 6.3 crore refund of excess fees charged under heads that included "smart class" charges. The article is undated. — [The Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/archive/education/parents-meet-dc-seek-refund-of-rs-6-3-crore-as-per-hc-orders-354377)
- [PRACTICE] Consumer commissions reportedly order pro-rata refunds of annual "smart-class fee" (parent guide, secondary). — [RTI wiki guide](https://righttoinformation.wiki/private-school-fee-refund-tc-blocked-india)
- [DIRECTIVE] Kota (Rajasthan) district collector: schools that print their names on books, worksheets or notebooks lose affiliation (2018). — [Hindustan Times via PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/india/hindustan-times-st-jaipur/20180411/281603831044714)

### Inferences
- Bundling a CT/AI licence into school fees ("computer fee" or "smart class fee") is legally possible only as an approved fee head passed by the school's fee committee. In Delhi from 2026-27 that means SLFRC approval with parent members, and interim HC orders freeze fees at 2025-26 levels. A new mid-cycle fee head is very hard to add.
- A school-branded workbook or licence that parents must buy from the school or a named vendor is exactly what Delhi, Maharashtra, Rajasthan and J&K 2026 directives target.
- The school's own budget (an existing tuition or computer-fee head, with the school paying the vendor) avoids parent-facing compliance problems but squeezes the school's margin.

### Gaps
- The Gujarat Self-Financed Schools (Regulation of Fees) Act 2017 and its FRC, the Maharashtra Educational Institutions (Regulation of Fee) Act 2011, Tamil Nadu's fee committee, Karnataka's fee rules and the UP Self-Financed Independent Schools (Fee Regulation) Act 2018 were not researched in this pass. No citable findings, so their treatment of computer or smart-class fees is unknown here.
- No Rajasthan 2025 school-fee bill was found. Only a 2025 coaching-centres bill (passed 3 Sept 2025) and a private medical college fee bill turned up. The "2025 act" in the brief may be a misidentification, or something not indexed. — [Lokmat Times](https://www.lokmattimes.com/national/rajasthan-govt-passes-coaching-institute-controlled-and-regulation-bill-2025)
- No 2024-2026 order found that specifically strikes down a "computer fee", "smart class fee" or bundled digital-product fee.

## Taxes (GST on books, education services, and digital content or SaaS to schools)

### Takeaway
Printed books are nil-rated, and from 22 Sept 2025 exercise books, notebooks, and reportedly maps and atlases, are nil as well. Services *to* schools up to higher secondary are exempt only for a narrow list under Entry 66(b) of Notification 12/2017-CT(Rate). Online or digital content and SaaS licences supplied to schools generally attract 18% GST. The online-journals carve-out is narrow and probably does not cover a CT/AI platform.

### Cited Findings
- Printed books remain nil-rated. — [Disytax bookstore GST guide 2026](https://disytax.com/gst-for-book-store/)
- From 22 Sept 2025 (56th GST Council; Notifications 9/2025 and 10/2025-CT(Rate) dated 17 Sept 2025), exercise books, graph books, laboratory notebooks, notebooks, pencils, sharpeners, crayons and erasers moved to nil. — [Business Today, 5 Sept 2025](https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/tax/story/gst-20-school-supplies-gets-cheaper-jeeneet-private-coaching-to-draw-18-rate-492781-2025-09-05); [StudyCafe, exemption list](https://studycafe.in/?p=393400); [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/education/from-pencils-to-notebooks-here-are-key-stationery-items-now-free-of-gst-details)
- Printed maps, atlases and globes are reported as nil-rated under GST 2.0. This is contradicted by one guide claiming 18%, which is likely unreliable. — [StudyCafe](https://studycafe.in/?p=393400); contradicted by [Disytax](https://disytax.com/gst-for-book-store/)
- Entry 66 (Notification 12/2017-CT(Rate)) exempts services by an educational institution to its students, and only listed services *to* institutions offering pre-school and up-to-higher-secondary education (transport, catering, security, cleaning, admission and exam-related services). — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/gst-educational-services-entities-12aa.html); [Logitax](https://logitax.in/resources/gst_on_various_services_by_or_to_educational_institutions)
- In 2023 a Karnataka ruling held catering to a school (Std 1 to 2nd PUC) exempt under Entry 66. — [TaxManagementIndia](https://www.taxmanagementindia.com/visitor/Detail_Case_Laws.asp?ID=433416)
- Entry 66(b)(v) covers only "online educational journals or periodicals". The UP AAR (Manupatra) held it does not cover online databases, online books, newspapers or directories. — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/gst-exemption-online-database-online-books-newspapers-directories-non-educational-journals.html); [Busy.in case law](https://busy.in/caselaw/manupatra-information-solutions-private-limited-authority-for-advance-ruling-uttar-pradesh)
- One commentary says the online-journals exemption is restricted to higher-education institutions, not schools. This is unverified and conflicts with broader readings. — [InCorpX 2026](https://www.incorpx.io/blog/gst-on-education-coaching-services-india-2026)
- Karnataka AAAR (Informatics Publishing) found access to online content taxable. — [GST Council AAR PDF](https://gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-02/kar_aaar_14-d_2019-20_informaticspublishingltd.pdf)
- Odisha AAR held that Coursera user licences supplied to a state skill authority are a licensing service, not an education service, taxable at 18%. — [LiveLaw Biz](https://www.livelawbiz.com/amp/gst/aar/supply-of-coursera-user-licenses-to-odisha-skill-development-authority-is-licensing-service-not-education-service-odisha-aar-534168)
- Kerala AAR (Oct 2022): online education to individual students by anyone other than a formal school or institution is taxable. — [Taxo.online](https://taxo.online/?p=167568)
- Secondary commentary says digital material (PDFs, videos, e-books) is treated as an online content service at 18%. E-books were cut from 18% to 5% from 27 July 2018; the current rate was not verified. — [TaxTMI blog](https://www.taxtmi.com/tmi_blog_details?id=670666); [StudyCafe](https://studycafe.in/gst-applicable-on-online-educational-journals-or-periodicals-to-educational-institution-117407.html)
- GST 2.0 confirmed 18% for private coaching. — [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/tax/story/gst-20-school-supplies-gets-cheaper-jeeneet-private-coaching-to-draw-18-rate-492781-2025-09-05)

### Inferences
- A per-student CT/AI digital licence (SaaS or app) sold to a school or to parents should be planned at 18% GST. A Rs 500 sticker price would include about Rs 76 GST if quoted inclusive.
- A printed CT workbook, as a book, would be nil-rated. That favours a hybrid "printed book + bundled access code" format, but bundling (composite or mixed supply) may push the whole bundle to the higher rate if it is a mixed supply. Specialist advice is needed.
- Fees a school collects from its own students are exempt, but the school cannot claim input tax credit on the 18% charged by the edtech vendor. That cost lands with the school or the parent.

### Gaps
- Official CBIC notification text was not fetched. No CBIC FAQ quote was retrieved.
- The current GST rate on e-books after GST 2.0 was not confirmed.
- No ruling found specifically on a coding or AI curriculum platform licensed to a K-12 school.

## Government and other channels (GeM, Samagra Shiksha ICT, state tenders, ATL, parent B2C)

### Takeaway
Government channels fund hardware-heavy ICT labs and smart classrooms in government and aided schools, with purchases routed through GeM. They are not a channel to private CBSE schools. Through the private-school route, parent payment (B2C via the school) is the most common funding source, but it is the most heavily regulated against coercion.

### Cited Findings
- [BINDING scheme norms] ICT@Samagra Shiksha covers government and aided schools with Classes VI-XII. An ICT lab gets a non-recurring grant of up to Rs 6.40 lakh and a recurring grant of up to Rs 2.40 lakh per school per year for 5 years. Smart classrooms get Rs 2.40 lakh non-recurring for two classrooms and Rs 0.38 lakh recurring. 120,614 ICT labs and 82,120 smart classrooms have been sanctioned to date. — [DoSEL, ICT@Samagra Shiksha](https://dsel.education.gov.in/en/scheme/ict-samagra)
- [BINDING directive] The Ministry has directed that all purchases from central funds under Samagra Shiksha go through GeM. Capital cost reportedly includes a minimum 5-year AMC (secondary source). — [ELMO India procurement guide](https://elmoindia.in/smart-classroom-procurement-government-schools-nep-2020-gem-budgeting/); [iDream Education](https://www.idreameducation.org/samagra-shiksha-ict-scheme-india/)
- ICT and smart classrooms are also being extended to Kasturba Gandhi Balika Vidyalayas. — [Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/government-equip-kasturba-gandhi-balika-vidyalayas-ict-labs-smart-classrooms/amp)
- Tamil Nadu leads the southern states in ICT labs and smart classrooms. — [NewKerala](https://www.newkerala.com/news/a/tn-tops-southern-states-ict-labs-smart-classrooms-271.htm)
- Samagra Shiksha framework (Dec 2022) document. — [Samagra Shiksha framework PDF](https://educationforallinindia.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/samagra-shiksha-framework-dec-2022.pdf)

### Inferences
- Government ICT money goes to government and aided schools as hardware-plus-content bundles with multi-year AMCs. A Rs 200-800 software-only licence vendor would need to partner with ICT integrators who bid on state tenders. This is irrelevant to private CBSE schools.
- The parent channel through the school (book list or kit) is how most private K-8 supplementary products are funded today. Under the 2026 rules it must be optional and available from the open market or more than one vendor.

### Gaps
- Not found: a GeM category for educational software or e-content, typical GeM price bands, Atal Tinkering Lab (ATL) vendor empanelment rules under the 2024-26 regime, and state-specific CT or coding tenders (BOOT model).
- No data found on what share of private-school supplementary products are parent-funded versus school-funded.

## What this implies: most viable and compliant channel for a Rs 200-800 per-student annual licence

### Takeaway
The evidence points to a school-adopted, parent-optional model packaged as supplementary material. Concretely, a printed NCF-SE-aligned CT/AI workbook (nil GST) with a bundled access code, placed on the school's signed, website-published book list, and purchasable from open-market booksellers or online (not only from the school). The alternative is a school-budget licence paid from an already-approved fee head. Adding a new "computer/AI fee" head is the riskiest route in fee-regulated states, especially Delhi from 2026-27.

### Inferences
- **Book list (parent pays, open-market availability): most viable.** It fits existing buying habits and the publisher sales motion (specimens, reps, workshops). Compliance holds if purchase is not compelled from a single vendor. Expect to budget a 30-40% channel margin. Risks: commission practices draw regulatory and reputational scrutiny, and CBSE urges NCERT for Classes 1-8.
- **School's own budget: cleanest compliance, but harder to sell.** No parent-facing fee issue arises, but the school absorbs 18% GST without credit and gets no commission. It suits the low end (Rs 200-300) or premium schools.
- **Separate school fee head: highest regulatory risk.** It needs fee-committee approval and is exposed to refund orders, as with smart-class fees in the Ludhiana case.
- **Direct B2C to parents via school referral:** compliant if optional, but conversion is likely low without the school's prescription. No data was found.
- **Government (GeM/Samagra):** not relevant to private CBSE schools, and hardware-led.

### Gaps
- No empirical data on conversion, willingness to pay, or school preferences between these channels.
- The legality of "access code sold only via school" under each state's rules has not been specifically adjudicated (none found).
