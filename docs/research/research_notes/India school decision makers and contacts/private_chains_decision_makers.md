# Decision-makers in Indian private CBSE schools and chains, and organisation-level contact points of major chains

Research date: 10 November 2026. Scope: India, private CBSE schools and chains. Organisational contacts only: no names or personal contact details of individuals were collected. Where an official page lists a named person's email, I have recorded only the role or department and left out the name and address. "UNVERIFIED" means the detail comes from a third-party directory or aggregator, or from background knowledge I could not confirm with a fetch in this session.

Method note: the CBSE domains (cbse.gov.in, cbseaff.nic.in) returned 403 errors or DNS failures to the fetch tool, and davcmc.net.in returned a 503. Bye-law clause details therefore rest on search snippets and on the school disclosure pages that cite them, not on the primary PDF. Coverage of the 30+ chains is uneven. Chains with no confirmed data are listed under Gaps rather than guessed.

## Q1. Who decides, and how, inside a private standalone CBSE school when a booklist title or curriculum programme is added

### Takeaway
In a standalone private CBSE school, subject teachers, HODs and the academic coordinator usually propose booklist titles, often after reviewing publishers' specimen copies. The principal finalises and issues the booklist. The trust or society (its chairman, secretary, manager or director), acting through the School Managing Committee (SMC), holds overall control, the budget and commercial approvals. A paid add-on programme such as computational thinking or AI involves money and vendor terms, so it is likely to need management sign-off as well as the principal's academic approval. The cycle runs from specimen review, to the finalised list, to a disclosure before the April session. I found no single authoritative published description of the internal process. The picture below is assembled from bye-law references, booklists, job descriptions and press reports.

### Cited Findings
- **Legal frame.** CBSE says the School Managing Committee rules sit in Chapter 8 of the revamped Affiliation Bye-Laws 2018 and tie in with the RTE Act's Section 21(1) requirement. The SMC's duties and powers operate "subject to overall control of the Society/Trust/Company". For a recognised aided school, CBSE's SMC notice caps the committee at 15 members. — [CBSE: School Management Committee page](https://www.cbse.gov.in/cbsenew/sch_mgmt.html) (via search snippet; the fetch was blocked, HTTP 403); [CBSE: Role of School Management Committee (PDF)](https://www.cbse.gov.in/cbsenew/board/Role%20of%20School%20Management%20Committee.pdf) (blocked, HTTP 403)
- **The Manager's role.** CBSE also publishes a separate "Role of Manager" document. The Manager is the society or trust's representative who runs the school. — [CBSE: Role of Manager (PDF)](https://www.cbse.gov.in/cbsenew/board//Role%20of%20Manager.pdf) (title from search; content not fetched)
- **SMC composition (UNVERIFIED).** From background knowledge of the 2018 bye-laws, not confirmed in this session: the SMC of an unaided school includes trust or society nominees, the principal as ex-officio member secretary, teacher representatives, parent representatives, educationists and members from other schools, and a CBSE or state nominee. The trust or society appoints the chairman. Check the composition and the member cap for unaided schools against Chapter 8 of the PDF at [CBSE: Affiliation Bye-Laws](https://www.cbse.gov.in/cbsenew/aff-bye-laws.html).
- **Books disclosure clause.** Schools publish a "Declaration for List of Books as per section 2.4.7(B) of Affiliation Bye-Laws", which shows that the bye-laws contain a book-list provision. My understanding is that it requires the school to declare the books it prescribes and not to compel purchase from a particular vendor, but I could not read the clause text, so this reading is UNVERIFIED. — [Birla High School Mukundapur: Book List 2025-26](https://bhs-mukundapur.birlahighschool.com/book-list-2025-26)
- **NCERT pressure.** CBSE and some state authorities have told affiliated schools to prescribe NCERT books, and the Madras High Court directed CBSE to make sure private affiliated schools use NCERT books. Compliance is weak: private schools still list books from private publishers. In Bhopal, one principal said NCERT books were not challenging enough, which is why private-publisher books were chosen. — [Free Press Journal: CBSE schools overlook NCERT-only orders](https://www.freepressjournal.in/bhopal/private-cbse-schools-overlook-orders-to-prescribe-only-ncert-books-lists-books-by-private-publishers)
- **Delhi rule.** Private unaided schools in Delhi must publish their prescribed books and materials, and cannot force parents to buy books or uniforms from specific vendors. — [EducationWorld: Delhi government on books and uniforms](https://www.educationworld.in/private-schools-cant-force-parents-to-buy-books-uniforms-from-specific-vendors-delhi-government)
- **Booklist format.** School booklists are typically issued under the principal's name, with columns for subject, title, publisher and ISBN. Examples: [EPS Delhi Book List 2026-27](https://epsdelhi.com/pdf/BOOK%20LIST%202026-27.pdf), [DPS Bhopal Book List 2025-26](https://dpsbhopal.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Book-List-25-26-I-to-XII-DPS-Bhopal.pdf), [DPS Rau Book List 2025-26](https://dpsrau.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/DPS-Rau-Book-List-2025-26-I-to-XII.pdf). The DPS lists were uploaded in February 2025 for a session starting in April 2025, which suggests the lists are final by about January or February.
- **Selection sequence.** One headmaster described the order as follows: schools receive specimen copies from publishers, select books for each subject, then issue the booklist so students can buy them. (This is a West Bengal school, quoted in a report on state-council electives.) — [Millennium Post: booklist delay](https://www.millenniumpost.in/bengal/booklist-delay-leaves-semester-iv-students-uncertain-about-textbooks-632681)
- **Specimen copies.** Publishers send free specimen copies to teachers and schools so they can review material before bulk adoption. — [Oswal Publishers: specimen copy](https://oswalpublishers.com/specimen-copy/); [ThePrint: specimen books for teachers](https://theprint.in/ani-press-releases/going-beyond-the-average-essential-specimen-books-for-teachers/2056868/)
- **Management's commercial interest.** Press reports allege that publishers and traders offer school managements commissions of 15-40% on books and supplies. These are reported allegations, not established findings, but they indicate that booklist economics are a management-level matter. — [Tribune India: publisher-school nexus](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/archive/chandigarh/how-nexus-between-private-publishers-schools-thrives-753952); [Himachalscape: parents demand public display of book names and publishers](https://himachalscape.com/nexus-between-pvt-school-mgmt-middleman-parents-demand-public-display-of-book-names-publishers)
- **Principals and price rises.** In a Bengaluru-area booklist dispute (Baldwin schools), principals were blamed for an unapproved price hike, which shows principals as the operational owners of the list. — [Deccan Herald: Baldwin booklist row](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/india%2Fkarnataka%2Fbengaluru%2Fbook%20list%20row%20baldwin%20principals%20blamed%20for%20unapproved%20price%20hike%203519269)
- **Academic coordinator.** CBSE-school job postings describe the academic coordinator as coordinating CBSE curriculum delivery across grades, acting as the main contact on CBSE academic matters, aligning SOPs with CBSE requirements, reporting to the Principal or Director, and assisting the Principal in academic decision-making. — [EduXHire: Academic Coordinator (CBSE School)](https://eduxhire.com/?p=157379); [Qureos: Academic Coordinator / Section Head](https://app.qureos.com/jobs/academic-coordinator-section-head-1769200688887)
- **Sales cycle.** A consultancy playbook (vendor-sourced) puts the average school B2B sales cycle at 4-9 months. It describes schools as cautious buyers with long decision hierarchies, constrained by the academic calendar. — [Raysolute: edtech go-to-market for schools](https://www.raysolute.com/edtech-india-go-to-market-schools.html)
- **Session timing.** The CBSE academic year runs from April to March for most schools. — [search summary of session timing; see bookmandee checklist](https://bookmandee.com/?p=12717). NCERT supply problems (only about half of the planned 2026-27 books printed by 7 May 2026) push schools towards private-publisher alternatives. — [Lokmat Times: NCERT textbook gap](https://www.lokmattimes.com/national/ncert-races-to-bridge-textbook-gap-only-half-of-planned-books-printed-so-far-for-new-academic-session/amp/)

### Inferences
- **Proposers:** the computer science HOD or ICT coordinator and the maths HOD (computational thinking straddles both), plus the academic or CBSE coordinator, who checks NEP/NCF and CBSE alignment. **Approvers:** the principal (academic) and the trust or society's chairman, secretary, manager or director (commercial). Where the item is non-standard, such as a paid programme, the SMC or management ratifies it. Purchase or admin staff handle the vendor, bookseller and pricing logistics after approval.
- **Practical timing for an April 2027 launch:** specimens and demos in October-November 2026; HOD and coordinator evaluation in November-December; principal and management sign-off in December-January; publication of the booklist and the 2.4.7(b)-type declaration in January-February. This window is inferred from upload dates and the specimen-copy sequence. No source states a formal October-January rule.
- The NCERT-only directives and parent-cost scrutiny favour a programme that is positioned as a supplementary or skill subject (computational thinking and AI are not covered by NCERT core texts for Classes 3-8) and that is transparently priced.

### Gaps
- I could not read the primary text of Chapter 8 (SMC) or clause 2.4.7 of the Affiliation Bye-Laws 2018, because the CBSE sites blocked the fetch. Verify the SMC composition and member cap for unaided schools, and the book clause, against the PDF.
- I found no authoritative Indian source, industry article or edtech sales guide describing a formal booklist committee or a sign-off matrix. EducationWorld, ET Education, Elets and Entrackr did not surface relevant pieces in these searches.
- No source documents a fixed October-January decision calendar.

## Q2. How chains centralise curriculum and booklist choices

### Takeaway
The evidence is strongest for three models. DAV CMC centrally prescribes its own published textbooks across DAV Public Schools nationwide through its Centre for Academic Excellence. Podar runs a "centrally designed curriculum" with a central Chief Academics Officer function and a central Purchase Department. Orchids (K12 Techno Services) uses in-house books and kits with group-wide copyright. For these chains the buyer is head office (central academic plus procurement), not the individual principal. A vendor playbook says chain deals take 6-12 months but then cover 10-100 schools at once.

### Cited Findings
- **DAV.** The DAV Centre for Academic Excellence (DAV CAE), "a wing of DAVCMC", issues notices prescribing books "uniformly, in DAV Public Schools PAN India" for each session (the 2025-26 specimen notice is an example). — [DAV CMC: specimen copy notice (PDF)](https://davcmc.net.in/HighlightNewsDoc/a3ce64fe-96cf-402f-bb75-b835b2b5f55d_Specimen%20Copy%20of%20Punjabi_Books__Atlas%20(1).pdf)
- **DAV in practice.** DAV school syllabi name DAV CAE and DAV CMC Publication Division titles, for example "Secondary Mathematics Class-VII, Publication Division, DAV College Managing Committee, New Delhi". — [DAV Bistupur Class VII syllabus 2026-27](https://davbistupur.org/File/6704/FAV_J04817_2026-27_DAV%20BISTUPUR_SYLLABUS_CLASS-STD-VII.pdf); [DAV Bistupur Class VI syllabus](https://davbistupur.org/File/6704/MNZ_DAV%20Bistupur_Syllabus%20-%20Std-VI.pdf)
- **Podar.** The official site refers to a "centrally designed curriculum". It lists a Podar Innovation Center with a Chief Academics Officer for the international schools, a Chief Academics Officer for the preschools, a Purchase Department headed by a Chief Procurement Officer, a Business Opportunities desk, and a Franchise or Business Development desk. — [Podar Education Network: about](https://www.podareducation.org/about-podar-education-network) (individuals' names and emails deliberately not recorded)
- **Orchids.** Its network uses group learning material ("videos, worksheets, content, books"), subject books with QR codes per lesson, subject kits, an in-house robotics and coding curriculum, and an "MIT-based computer curriculum". The site carries a "K12 Techno Services" copyright. — [Orchids: Digital Enhanced Learning](https://www.orchidsinternationalschool.com/digital-enhanced-learning-del); [Orchids: Academics](https://orchidsinternational.prod-gke.letseduvate.com/primary-and-secondary); [PR Newswire: Orchids MIT-based computer curriculum](https://www.prnewswire.com/in/news-releases/orchids-the-international-school-introduces-mit-based-computer-curriculum-for-students-682666931.html); [Orchids: Coding](https://www.orchidsinternationalschool.com/activities/coding)
- **Orchids' boards.** Teaching follows CBSE up to Grade VIII, with lesson plans and assessments "as per the norms of international boards". — [Orchids: Primary and Secondary](https://orchidsinternationalschool.com/primary-and-secondary)
- **Chain deal dynamics (vendor-sourced).** Direct business development with a chain head office reaches 10-100 schools at a time; the examples given are DPS Society, Ryan Group and Amity. Chain deals take 6-12 months but scale rapidly. — [Raysolute: edtech go-to-market for schools](https://www.raysolute.com/edtech-india-go-to-market-schools.html)
- **Generic chain uniformity.** Chains "often share resources and teaching methods" with a "uniform approach" across schools (generic claim from a vendor blog, dated 14 April 2025). — [Tynker: biggest school chains in India](https://www.tynker.com/blog/biggest-school-chains-india/)

### Inferences
- **Fully central** (sell to head office): DAV CMC (an in-house publisher, so it is a potential licensing or co-publishing partner, or a competitor), Podar, Orchids/K12 (in-house content; they may treat an external programme as a competitor or a white-label opportunity), and likely VIBGYOR, Lighthouse Learning (EuroSchool, Billabong) and Jaipuria. These have corporate structures, but their central control is UNVERIFIED.
- **Federated or school-level:** the DPS Society (each DPS is run by a member school society or franchisee, and booklists vary between DPS Bhopal and DPS Rau), Bharatiya Vidya Bhavan kendras, Chinmaya Vidyalayas (local trusts under the CCMT Education Cell), Vidya Bharati (state-level units), and Catholic diocesan schools. For these, the central body can endorse or circulate, but individual principals and managements adopt.

### Gaps
- No source confirms central versus school-level booklist control for Ryan, VIBGYOR, Lighthouse Learning, Jaipuria, GD Goenka, Narayana, Sri Chaitanya, Amity, Manav Rachna, Zee Learn, Edify, Indus or Pathways.
- No chain publishes an "approved vendor list" or pilot policy that I could find.

- **Endorse-and-cascade routes:** the DPS Society, Chinmaya CCMT Education Cell, Bharatiya Vidya Bhavan and Vidya Bharati. Approval or circulation by the central body helps, but individual schools adopt.
- DAV, Orchids and Podar have in-house content or innovation centres, so pitch to them as a partner, licensing or white-label arrangement rather than as a booklist title.
- For every chain, the right head-office function to approach is the central academic or curriculum team (titles such as Chief Academic Officer, Head of Academics or Director of Curriculum) for the content decision, and the procurement or purchase department for commercial terms. Use only general or department inboxes and the web forms the organisations publish.
