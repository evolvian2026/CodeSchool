# Government and non-private school systems in India: decision-makers, official contact points and procurement routes (as of Nov 2026)

Scope note: organisational contacts only (designations, offices, official sites, office emails and phones). Individual officer names were deliberately left out, even where sources gave them. Officeholders and many published numbers change often, and several sources below are school-level mirrors or older pages, so every email and phone here should be re-checked on the body's own "Contact Us" or "Officers" page before outreach. "UNVERIFIED" marks anything not confirmed from a primary or current page in this research pass. I made about 18 search and fetch calls, so coverage of some bodies (Navy, Air Force, Railway schools, Tamil Nadu, Delhi, Punjab, UP, Maharashtra, Himachal) is thin and is flagged in Gaps.

## 1. Kendriya Vidyalaya Sangathan (KVS): HQ structure, regional offices, ZIETs, adoption and partnership routes, and its role in CBSE CT&AI

### Takeaway
KVS runs from a New Delhi HQ with a Commissioner and Joint Commissioners for each function (Academics and Training are the relevant ones), 25 regional offices and 5 ZIETs. The official website has moved from kvsangathan.nic.in to kvs.gov.in. KVS is formally a named partner in the Ministry of Education's rollout of AI and Computational Thinking from Class 3 (2026-27), so the JC (Academics) office at HQ is the entry point for anything curricular or system-wide. The JC (Training) office and the ZIETs are the entry points for teacher training.

### Inferences
- Decision path at KVS: system-wide adoption of supplementary material is decided at HQ (JC Academics, under the Commissioner). Training content and partnerships run through JC Training, the ZIETs and the RO training cells. Individual KVs buy small items locally from Vidyalaya funds, which is why tender notices appear on KV and RO sites.
- Because KVS took part in framing CT&AI, it is likely to push NCERT/CBSE material first. A private workbook or kit would most plausibly enter as a supplementary resource, a pilot under an MoU or a ZIET training partnership, rather than as a replacement textbook. This is an inference, not documented.
- Tenders: the HQ tender section was empty when fetched. In practice, central autonomous bodies must use GeM for goods and services that are listed there (General Financial Rules), so vendor registration on GeM is a prerequisite. The GFR rule comes from background knowledge, not from a source fetched here.

- I found no documented KVS MoU with an edtech or CT vendor in this pass. KVS's MoU policy and GeM usage for academic material are not documented here.

## 4. State government schools: who decides (SPD, SCERT, DIET, directorates), how CT/coding/AI gets added (AWP&B, PAB, ICT), procurement, and state examples with private partners

### Takeaway
In state schools, money for CT, coding and AI flows mostly through Samagra Shiksha. The State Project Director's office prepares the Annual Work Plan & Budget, and the MoE Project Approval Board (PAB) approves ICT labs, smart classrooms and innovation components. PAB minutes are published per state on dsel.education.gov.in. Content and teacher training sit with SCERTs and DIETs, and curriculum and textbooks with SCERT and the state textbook boards. Recent state AI and coding programmes have mostly come through MoUs with big tech, IITs or foundations (Telangana–Amazon, Assam's six MoUs, AP–IIT Madras/Bodhan AI, Bihar with 11 IT firms) or through in-house state agencies (Kerala KITE). Vendor tenders have been less common.

### Cited Findings
- PAB minutes and addenda for 2025-26 are published per state on dsel.education.gov.in/sites/default/files/pab/ (for example Chhattisgarh, Kerala, HP, Punjab, Gujarat, Rajasthan, MP, UP and Haryana) — [PAB Chhattisgarh 2025-26](https://dsel.education.gov.in/sites/default/files/pab/CG2526.pdf); [Kerala 2025-26](https://dsel.education.gov.in/sites/default/files/pab/Kerala_2526.pdf); [HP 2025-26](https://dsel.education.gov.in/sites/default/files/pab/hp2526.pdf)

### Inferences

This is a synthesis of how Samagra Shiksha works, not taken from a single source.
- Partners in the examples were mostly picked through no-cost or CSR-funded MoUs (Amazon, Google, Microsoft, IIT Madras, foundations) rather than open tenders. A paid vendor of workbooks and kits would normally have to go through GeM or a state e-procurement tender, or be empanelled by the state, after budget approval.
- Kerala builds its own content and kits through KITE, so it is a weak prospect for outside workbooks and kits and a stronger prospect for co-development or training partnerships.

## 5. CSR route: company funding of education programmes in government schools (Schedule VII) and NGO/foundation partnerships with states

### Takeaway
Education, including digital, AI and coding skilling, is routinely booked as CSR under Schedule VII item (ii), "promoting education... and employment-enhancing vocation skills". Big-tech and foundation MoUs with states and central systems (Amazon Future Engineer through Learning Links Foundation in EMRS, Amazon–Telangana, Assam's six MoUs) are the main way private CS/AI programmes reach government schools.

### Cited Findings
- Companies classify AI and digital skilling programmes as "Sch VII (ii) – Enhancing vocation skills" or "Promoting education" — [Cisco India CSR cash grant projects](https://www.cisco.com/c/dam/global/en_in/about/docs/india-cash-grant-projects.pdf)
- Corporate CSR action plans list government-school workshops, mentoring and computer literacy under Schedule VII (ii) — [IndusInd Insurance CSR proposals FY2025-26](https://www.indusindinsurance.com/PublicDisclosure/csr-proposals-fy2025-26.pdf); [Hero MotoCorp CSR projects](https://heromotocorp.com/content/dam/hero-aem-website/in/en-in/company-section/investors/csr-reports/CSR_Projects.pdf)
- Item (ix) covers contributions to government-funded incubators, public universities and IITs. This is relevant where an IIT-linked partner (for example IIT Madras–Bodhan AI) is the delivery body — [IndiaCSR on Schedule VII](https://indiacsr.in/what-is-relation-between-corporate-social-responsibility-csr-and-schedule-vii/)
- Foundation-intermediated delivery precedent: Amazon Future Engineer, implemented by Learning Links Foundation in EMRS under a NESTS MoU (2026-28) — [Devdiscourse](https://www.devdiscourse.com/article/law-order/3930890-nests-expands-digital-education-initiative-across-emrs-network)
- Foundation and CSR precedents with states: LEHS–Wadhwani AI, Educate Girls and EkStep–AXL (Assam), and One Future Foundation (Odisha district) — [Sentinel Assam](https://www.sentinelassam.com/topheadlines/ai-push-in-assam-schools-ssa-signs-six-mous-for-technology-enabled-learning); [Ommcom News](https://ommcomnews.com/odisha-news/ai-hubs-to-come-up-in-bhadrak-govt-schools/)

### Inferences
- A practical route for a for-profit provider is to become the content and training supplier to a CSR-funded implementing NGO. That NGO holds the MoU with the SPD, NESTS or NVS, and the company invoices the NGO. Under the CSR Rules, the implementing agency must be registered with MCA (form CSR-1). That rule comes from background knowledge and was not re-sourced here.
