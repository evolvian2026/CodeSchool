# US K-12 Standards and Frameworks for Computational Thinking, Computer Science and AI (grade-by-grade fact base, as of Nov 2026)

Scope note: all US documents below are voluntary, non-federal models. States choose whether to adopt or adapt them. Document status and dates are given for each. Primary sources were read directly where possible: the full 2026 CSTA PK-12 Standards PDF, the 2017 CSTA K-12 Standards PDF, and the AI4K12 Big Idea 3 and Big Idea 5 chart PDFs.

## 1. K-12 Computer Science Framework (2016): five core concepts, seven practices, CT definition, grade-band progression

### Takeaway
The K-12 CS Framework (October 2016) is a conceptual framework, not a set of standards. It defines 5 core concepts (17 subconcepts) and 7 core practices. Practices 3-6 make up "computational thinking". It sets out "by end of grade 2 / 5 / 8 / 12" statements, and it does not mention AI or machine learning. It was the base for the CSTA 2017 standards, and its CT practices carry into the 2026 CSTA standards.

### Cited Findings
- **Date and authors:** The framework was released in October 2016. ACM, Code.org, CSTA, the Cyber Innovation Center and the National Math and Science Initiative led it, in partnership with states and districts. — [ACM bulletin, Oct 2016](https://www.acm.org/articles/bulletins/2016/october/k-12-framework); [CSTA 2017 Standards PDF](https://csteachers.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/csta-k-12-computer-science-standards-revised.pdf)
- **Five core concepts and their subconcepts:**
  - Computing Systems: Devices; Hardware & Software; Troubleshooting
  - Networks & the Internet: Network Communication & Organization; Cybersecurity
  - Data & Analysis: Collection; Storage; Visualization & Transformation; Inference & Models
  - Algorithms & Programming: Algorithms; Variables; Control; Modularity; Program Development
  - Impacts of Computing: Culture; Social Interactions; Safety, Law & Ethics
  — [k12cs.org statements by grade band](https://k12cs.org/framework-statements-by-grade-band/)
- **Seven core practices:**
  1. Fostering an Inclusive Computing Culture
  2. Collaborating Around Computing
  3. Recognizing and Defining Computational Problems
  4. Developing and Using Abstractions
  5. Creating Computational Artifacts
  6. Testing and Refining Computational Artifacts
  7. Communicating About Computing
  — [k12cs.org](https://k12cs.org/framework-statements-by-grade-band/)
- **CT definition:** "Computational thinking refers to the thought processes involved in expressing solutions as computational steps or algorithms that can be carried out by a computer." The framework credits Cuny, Snyder & Wing (2010) and Aho (2011). CT is "delineated by" practices 3-6, while practices 1, 2 and 7 are general CS practices that complement CT. The framework also stresses that using a computer in class does not by itself constitute CT. — [k12cs.org Computational Thinking](https://k12cs.org/computational-thinking/)
- **Grade-band structure:** Statements are written "by the end of Grade 2, 5, 8, 12", with labels such as "K–2.Algorithms and Programming.Algorithms". — [k12cs.org](https://k12cs.org/framework-statements-by-grade-band/)
- **Algorithms & Programming by end of Grade 2:**
  - "People follow and create processes as part of daily life... Many of these processes can be expressed as algorithms that computers can follow."
  - Control: "Computers follow precise sequences of instructions that automate tasks. Program execution can also be nonsequential by repeating patterns of instructions and using events to initiate instructions."
  - Variables: "Information in the real world can be represented in computer programs."
  — [k12cs.org](https://k12cs.org/framework-statements-by-grade-band/)
- **Algorithms & Programming by end of Grade 5:**
  - Variables: "Programming languages provide variables, which are used to store and modify data."
  - Control: "Control structures, including loops, event handlers, and conditionals, are used to specify the flow of execution. Conditionals selectively execute or skip instructions under different conditions."
  - Algorithms: "Different algorithms can achieve the same result."
  - Program Development: "People develop programs using an iterative process involving design, implementation, and review."
  — [k12cs.org](https://k12cs.org/framework-statements-by-grade-band/)
- **Algorithms & Programming by end of Grade 8:**
  - Modularity: "Programs use procedures to organize code, hide implementation details, and make code easier to reuse. Defining parameters for procedures can generalize behavior and increase reusability."
  - Variables: "A meaningful identifier is assigned to each variable..."
  - Algorithms: "Algorithms that are readable are easier to follow, test, and debug."
  — [k12cs.org](https://k12cs.org/framework-statements-by-grade-band/)
- **Data & Analysis:**
  - End of grade 2: "Data can be used to make inferences or predictions about the world"; "People use computers to transform data into new forms, such as graphs and charts."
  - End of grade 5: "Many factors influence the accuracy of inferences and predictions, such as the amount and relevance of data collected."
  - End of grade 8: "Computer models can be used to simulate events, examine theories and inferences, or make predictions."
  — [k12cs.org](https://k12cs.org/framework-statements-by-grade-band/)
- **No AI content:** The grade-band statements never mention artificial intelligence or machine learning. The closest content is the Inference & Models statements and a Grade 5 Culture example about autonomous cars. — [k12cs.org](https://k12cs.org/framework-statements-by-grade-band/)

### Inferences
- The framework's sequencing is: sequence, loops and events by end of grade 2; conditionals and variables by end of grade 5; procedures with parameters by end of grade 8. This matches CSTA 2017 and is the baseline for comparing CBSE Classes 3-8.
- The framework's "CT = practices 3-6" framing (define problems, abstraction, create, test) is process-oriented. A CBSE comparison should check whether CBSE frames CT as a process (practices) or as content topics (decomposition, pattern recognition and so on).

### Gaps
- I did not open the full 2016 framework PDF, so page numbers and some statements that were truncated on k12cs.org (for example the grade 8 Control and Program Development statements) are not verified verbatim.

## 2. CSTA K-12 Standards (2017) and the new 2026 CSTA PK-12 Standards: structure, changes, AI integration, example standards by grade

### Takeaway
The 2017 standards set 5 levels (1A K-2, 1B 3-5, 2 6-8, 3A 9-10, 3B 11-12), had about 120 standards and contained almost no AI (only two grade 11-12 standards). The 2026 CSTA PK-12 Standards are final, published summer 2026 and launched at the July 2026 CSTA conference. They replace those levels with:
- a PK/K band
- separate grade-level standards for each of grades 1-5
- a single middle school (6-8) band
- a foundational high school band
- high school Specialty I and II tiers in 6 areas, including Artificial Intelligence (AIN)

Machine-learning standards run every year from PK/K to high school. Grade 5 students "Train a machine learning model to make a classification or prediction" (E5-ALG-ML-02).

### Cited Findings

**2017 CSTA K-12 CS Standards (superseded by 2026)**
- **Release:** Created by educators and released at the CSTA Annual Conference in July 2017 under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. The K-12 CS Framework informed them. — [CSTA 2017 Standards PDF](https://csteachers.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/csta-k-12-computer-science-standards-revised.pdf)
- **Levels:** 1A = Grades K-2 (ages 5-7); 1B = Grades 3-5 (ages 8-11); 2 = Grades 6-8 (ages 11-14); 3A = Grades 9-10 (ages 14-16); 3B = Grades 11-12 (ages 16-18). The ID format is Level-Concept-Number, for example 1B-AP-10. Concept codes are CS, NI, DA, AP and IC. — [CSTA 2017 PDF](https://csteachers.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/csta-k-12-computer-science-standards-revised.pdf)
- **Level 1A examples (K-2):**
  - 1A-AP-08: "Model daily processes by creating and following algorithms (sets of step-by-step instructions) to complete tasks."
  - 1A-AP-09: "Model the way programs store and manipulate data by using numbers or other symbols to represent information."
  - 1A-AP-10: "Develop programs with sequences and simple loops, to express ideas or address a problem."
  - 1A-AP-14: "Debug (identify and fix) errors in an algorithm or program that includes sequences and simple loops."
  - 1A-DA-07: "Identify and describe patterns in data visualizations, such as charts or graphs, to make predictions."
  - 1A-NI-04: passwords
  — [CSTA 2017 PDF](https://csteachers.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/csta-k-12-computer-science-standards-revised.pdf)
- **Level 1B examples (3-5):**
  - 1B-AP-08: compare and refine multiple algorithms
  - 1B-AP-09: "Create programs that use variables to store and modify data."
  - 1B-AP-10: "Create programs that include sequences, events, loops, and conditionals."
  - 1B-AP-11: decompose problems
  - 1B-AP-12: modify or remix existing programs
  - 1B-NI-04: model packets
  - 1B-DA-07: "Use data to highlight or propose cause-and-effect relationships, predict outcomes, or communicate an idea."
  — [CSTA 2017 PDF](https://csteachers.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/csta-k-12-computer-science-standards-revised.pdf)
- **Level 2 examples (6-8):**
  - 2-AP-10: "Use flowcharts and/or pseudocode to address complex problems as algorithms."
  - 2-AP-11: "Create clearly named variables that represent different data types and perform operations on their values."
  - 2-AP-12: "Design and iteratively develop programs that combine control structures, including nested loops and compound conditionals."
  - 2-AP-14: "Create procedures with parameters to organize code and make it easier to reuse."
  - 2-DA-09: "Refine computational models based on the data they have generated."
  - 2-IC-21: "Discuss issues of bias and accessibility in the design of existing technologies."
  — [CSTA 2017 PDF](https://csteachers.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/csta-k-12-computer-science-standards-revised.pdf)
- **AI in 2017:** The only explicit AI standards were at Level 3B (grades 11-12):
  - 3B-AP-08: "Describe how artificial intelligence drives many software and physical systems."
  - 3B-AP-09: "Implement an artificial intelligence algorithm to play a game against a human opponent or solve a problem."

  I found no AI or machine-learning standards for K-10. — [CSTA 2017 PDF](https://csteachers.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/csta-k-12-computer-science-standards-revised.pdf) (my text search of the full PDF)

**2026 CSTA PK-12 CS Standards (final, current)**
- **Status and publication:**
  - Published 2026 by CSTA. DOI 10.1145/3820482, ISBN 979-8-4007-2780-1, licensed CC BY-NC-SA 4.0.
  - The writing phase began September 2024. Draft 1.0 came out around winter-spring 2025, Draft 2.0 around fall 2025, Draft 3.0 around spring 2026, and the standards were "Published" in summer 2026.
  - About 25 writers from 18 states took part, plus an advisory board of about 70 and about 450 asynchronous reviewers.
  — [2026 CSTA PK-12 Standards PDF](https://csteachers.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/2026-CSTA-PK%E2%80%9312-Computer-Science-Standards.pdf); [CSTA PK-12 Standards page](https://csteachers.org/pk12standards/)
- **Launch:** Officially launched at the 2026 CSTA Annual Conference in New Orleans. — [CSTA Washington](https://www.cstawa.org/introducing-the-2026-csta-pk-12-computer-science-standards-what-washington-educators-need-to-know/) (via search summary); the PDF upload path is dated 2026/07.
- **Definition of CS:** "Computer science is the study and human-centered practice of using data, algorithms, and computing systems to solve problems, make discoveries, and express ideas."
  - CS "is" the application of CT "to develop both rules-based and data-driven solutions".
  - CS is "not" "using technology tools like word processors, slide decks, or generative AI tools".
  - CS is not "being replaced by AI. CS is what powers AI."
  — [2026 Standards PDF](https://csteachers.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/2026-CSTA-PK%E2%80%9312-Computer-Science-Standards.pdf)
- **Structure:**
  - Foundational standards begin with "a combined pre-kindergarten and kindergarten (PK/K) band, followed by grade-level expectations for grades 1-5. Middle school is organized as a single grade band, leading into a final band that represents foundational high school learning."
  - Three components: concepts, practices and dispositions.
  - Dispositions: creativity, critical thinking, curiosity, persistence, reflectiveness, resourcefulness, sense of belonging.
  — [2026 Standards PDF](https://csteachers.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/2026-CSTA-PK%E2%80%9312-Computer-Science-Standards.pdf)
- **Five concepts and subconcept codes:**
  - ALG Algorithms & Design: PS Algorithmic Problem Solving, ML Machine Learning, IM Impacts of Algorithms & Design
  - PRO Programming: PD Program Development, VD Variables & Data Storage, RD Reading & Documenting, TR Testing & Refining
  - DAT Data & Analysis: DC Data Collection & Preparation, DI Data Investigation, IM Impacts of Data Science
  - SYS Systems & Security: HW Hardware & Software, SE Security, NT Networks, IM Impacts of Computing Systems
  - SOC Computing & Society: HI History of Computing, ET Emerging Technologies, HU Humans & Computing, CE Career Exploration
  — [2026 Standards PDF](https://csteachers.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/2026-CSTA-PK%E2%80%9312-Computer-Science-Standards.pdf)
- **ID format:** Grade band - Concept - Subconcept - Number, for example E3-ALG-ML-02.
  - Elementary IDs begin with "E" plus K (meaning PK/K) or the grade number 1-5.
  - Middle school IDs use "MS" and high school IDs use "HS".
  - Numbers run continuously within each grade or band.
  — [2026 Standards PDF](https://csteachers.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/2026-CSTA-PK%E2%80%9312-Computer-Science-Standards.pdf)
- **Number of foundational standards** (counted from Appendix B): PK/K 16, Grade 1 16, Grade 2 16, Grades 3, 4 and 5 19 each, Middle School 44, High School 45, about 194 in total. Specialty AIN has 14 Specialty I and 10 Specialty II standards. — [2026 Standards PDF, Appendix B](https://csteachers.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/2026-CSTA-PK%E2%80%9312-Computer-Science-Standards.pdf)
- **Twelve practices in four categories:**
  - Ethics & Social Responsibility (ESR 1-2): positive social impact; respect rights and dignity, including privacy and attribution
  - Inclusive Collaboration (IC 3-5): communicate; manage projects; act responsibly in collaborations
  - Computational Thinking (CT 6-9):
    - 6 Define computational problems, including "rule-based approaches, data-driven approaches (e.g., machine learning)" and decomposition
    - 7 Develop and use abstractions
    - 8 Create computing technologies "through traditional programming, model training, or a combination"
    - 9 Test and refine, including "accuracy evaluation for machine learning models"
  - Human-Centered Design (HCD 10-12)

  The 2026 CT definition reads: "a way of thinking about problems and formulating problems and solutions so that an information-processing agent (e.g., a computer) can help to solve them." The CT practices are "largely based on" the 2016 framework practices, plus NGSS and "Computational Thinking 2.0 (Tedre et al., 2021)". — [2026 Standards PDF](https://csteachers.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/2026-CSTA-PK%E2%80%9312-Computer-Science-Standards.pdf)
- **What changed from 2017:**
  - Algorithms & Design becomes a concept separate from Programming.
  - Programming shifts from "writing code toward a more balanced approach that also emphasizes reading, evaluating, modifying, and debugging programs".
  - Computing Systems and Networks & the Internet merge into Systems & Security.
  - New or expanded priorities: Ethics & Impacts; AI & Emerging Technologies; Human-Centered Design; Inclusive Collaboration; History of Computing; Career Exploration.
  - Practices drop from 7 to 12 practices in 4 categories, and dispositions are added.
  - Sources drawn on include the AI4K12 Guidelines (2022), the K-12 Data Science Learning Progressions (DS4E, 2025), CYBER.ORG cybersecurity standards (2021), CS2023, and the CSTA & AI4K12 "AI Learning Priorities for All K-12 Students" (2025).
  — [2026 Standards PDF](https://csteachers.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/2026-CSTA-PK%E2%80%9312-Computer-Science-Standards.pdf)
- **How AI is woven in:** "the writing team chose to integrate AI learning throughout the Standards rather than define AI as a separate concept."
  - Algorithms & Design: "Rule-based vs. data-driven approaches, machine learning, and societal impacts (e.g., bias)"
  - Programming: "Reading and evaluating AI-generated code"
  - Data & Analysis: "Data fluency and societal impacts"
  - Systems & Security: "Security implications and environmental impacts"
  - Computing & Society: "History of computing and AI, emerging technologies, the relationship between humans and AI..."

  Standards are tagged "AI-related" when they explicitly reference AI, align with an AI Learning Priorities outcome, or are foundational to AI literacy. — [2026 Standards PDF](https://csteachers.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/2026-CSTA-PK%E2%80%9312-Computer-Science-Standards.pdf)
- **Instructional time assumed:** "In elementary school (grades PK-5), students engage in approximately 20-40 hours of CS learning per year. In middle school (grades 6-8) and high school (grades 9-12), students experience the equivalent of a yearlong course at each grade band." — [2026 Standards PDF](https://csteachers.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/2026-CSTA-PK%E2%80%9312-Computer-Science-Standards.pdf)
- **Block vs text programming:** "Programming is not limited to text-based code. Especially in early grades, students may use block-based or tangible programming systems."
  - EK-PRO-PD-04 boundary: "Unplugged activities are appropriate... Students are not required to use computers."
  - E1-PRO-PD-04 boundary: "Students should translate algorithms with sequential steps into block-based code."
  - E3-PRO-PD-04 boundary: "Students are not expected to use text-based languages."
  - E3-PRO-TR-08 boundary: students are "not expected to debug syntax errors in text-based languages."
  - E5-PRO-VD-06 boundary: "Students should use variables in block-based or beginner text-based environments."
  - HS-ALG-ML-08 boundary: "a block-based platform or a simple Python library."
  - Physical Computing specialty: "block-based, hybrid, or text-based environments."
  — [2026 Standards PDF](https://csteachers.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/2026-CSTA-PK%E2%80%9312-Computer-Science-Standards.pdf)
- **High school tiers:**
  - Specialty I ("first focused learning experience") and Specialty II ("preparing students for postsecondary coursework, industry certifications...").
  - Six areas: Artificial Intelligence (AIN), Cybersecurity (CYB), Data Science (DSC), Game Development (GMD), Physical Computing (PHY), Software Development (SWD).
  - X+CS standards integrate foundational CS into other subjects.
  - AIN examples:
    - S1-AIN-DD-02 "Modify AI system training data to improve fairness and accuracy in outputs"
    - S1-AIN-DS-06 "Examine how data flows through a neural network structure"
    - S1-AIN-HR-09 "Analyze the potential biases and limitations of AI systems"
    - S1-AIN-HR-10 "Analyze the environmental impacts of widespread AI adoption"
    - S1-AIN-PP-11 "Integrate a prebuilt AI agent into an application"
    - S2-AIN-DD-01 unsupervised learning
    - S2-AIN-PP-08 "Develop an AI agent to accomplish a defined task"
    - S2-AIN-PP-10 "Debate aspects of AI regulatory frameworks and legislation across countries"
  — [2026 Standards PDF](https://csteachers.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/2026-CSTA-PK%E2%80%9312-Computer-Science-Standards.pdf)

### Inferences
- In the 2026 standards, AI starts at PK/K as pattern and attribute-based classification and becomes explicit in Grade 1. Children examine how a model is trained in Grade 2 and see the effect of adding training data in Grade 3. Training-data properties and the term "bias" come in Grade 4, and students first train a model themselves in Grade 5. This is a year-by-year ML spine across grades 3-5, directly comparable to CBSE Classes 3-5.
- Compared with 2017, control structures are pushed slightly later and spread out: loops in grade 2 and conditionals in grade 3 (2017 had loops at K-2 and conditionals at 3-5). Procedures without parameters sit in MS. Parameters and return values move to HS (2017 had parameters at 6-8).
- The 2026 standards never require text-based programming at any foundational grade. Beginner text-based work is first explicitly "appropriate" at Grade 5, and text-based languages are allowed but not required in MS and HS.
- Using an AI tool (MS-ALG-PS-05) and evaluating AI-generated code (MS-PRO-RD-18) first appear in middle school. Generative-AI tool use is not an elementary expectation.

## 3. AI4K12 "Five Big Ideas in AI" and grade-band progression charts

### Takeaway
AI4K12 is a joint AAAI and CSTA initiative started in May 2018. It defines five Big Ideas and publishes grade-band (K-2, 3-5, 6-8, 9-12) progression charts. As of November 2026 the official site still describes all five charts as drafts open for public comment. Grades 3-5 train a classification model and check its accuracy on new inputs. Grades 6-8 train and evaluate classifiers on tabular data, model unsupervised clustering and compare decision trees with neural networks. On the ethics side, grades 3-5 evaluate how AI affects different groups differently and grades 6-8 evaluate how stakeholder values shape AI design.

### Cited Findings
- **Origin and sponsors:** AI4K12 is "jointly sponsored by" AAAI and CSTA. AAAI announced it on 15 May 2018, and NSF funding was awarded on 5 March 2019. — [ai4k12.org](https://ai4k12.org/)
- **Founding paper:** Touretzky, Gardner-McCune, Martin & Seehorn, "Envisioning AI for K-12: What Should Every Child Know about AI?", AAAI-19 Proceedings vol. 33, pp. 9795-9799 (Blue Sky track). An AAAI/CSTA working group formed in May 2018 drafted national K-12 AI guidelines organised around five big ideas. — [AAAI OJS](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/5053) (via search summary)
- **Follow-up paper:** Touretzky, Gardner-McCune & Seehorn, "Machine Learning and the Five Big Ideas in AI", *International Journal of AI in Education* (2022). It uses Big Idea 3 as its in-depth example. — [DOI 10.1007/s40593-022-00314-1](https://doi.org/10.1007/S40593-022-00314-1)
- **The five Big Ideas:** Big Idea 1 Perception; 2 Representation & Reasoning; 3 Learning; 4 Natural Interaction; 5 Societal Impact. — [ai4k12.org gradeband charts](https://ai4k12.org/gradeband-progression-charts/)
  - Big Idea 3 concept statement: "Computers can learn from data." — [BI3 chart PDF](https://ai4k12.org/wp-content/uploads/AI4K12-Big-Idea-3-Progression-Chart-Working-Draft-of-Big-Idea-3-v.08.07.2026-b-1.pdf)
  - Big Idea 5: "AI can impact society in both positive and negative ways." — [AI4K12 Big Idea 5 page](https://ai4k12.org/big-idea-5-societal-impact/)
- **Chart status:**
  - The site says "All five of the grade band progression charts are currently available for public comment and feedback."
  - Big Idea 1 draft released 28 May 2020. Big Idea 3 V.0.1 released 19 November 2020. Big Idea 2 released for feedback June 2021. Big Idea 4 "Working Draft v.2.27.2022". Big Idea 5 V.0.1 released 22 December 2022.
  - The Big Idea 3 PDF URL contains "v.08.07.2026", but the document itself is marked "V.0.1 - Released November 19, 2020. Subject to change based on public feedback". The version is unclear.
  — [ai4k12.org](https://ai4k12.org/); [gradeband charts page](https://ai4k12.org/gradeband-progression-charts/); [BI3 PDF](https://ai4k12.org/wp-content/uploads/AI4K12-Big-Idea-3-Progression-Chart-Working-Draft-of-Big-Idea-3-v.08.07.2026-b-1.pdf); [BI5 PDF](https://ai4k12.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/AI4K12-Big-Idea-5-Progression-Chart-Working-Draft_v.0.1_12.22.2022.pdf); [AI4K12 list, BI2 release](https://lists.aaai.org/ai4k12/msg/16947749/)
- **Chart format:** Each cell gives an LO ("Learning Objective: What students should be able to do") and an EU ("Enduring Understanding: What students should know"), with optional unpacked descriptions and activities. — [BI3 PDF](https://ai4k12.org/wp-content/uploads/AI4K12-Big-Idea-3-Progression-Chart-Working-Draft-of-Big-Idea-3-v.08.07.2026-b-1.pdf)
- **Big Idea 3 (Learning) by band.** Source for all items: [BI3 chart PDF](https://ai4k12.org/wp-content/uploads/AI4K12-Big-Idea-3-Progression-Chart-Working-Draft-of-Big-Idea-3-v.08.07.2026-b-1.pdf); the 3-5 code is corroborated by [Day of AI / AIforCA](https://sites.google.com/view/aiforca/you-can-teach-ai/3-5/day-of-ai-2024-3-5).
  - 3-A-i, humans vs machines:
    - K-2 "Describe and provide examples of how people learn and how computers learn."
    - 3-5 "Differentiate between how people learn and how computers learn."
    - 6-8 "Contrast the unique characteristics of human learning with the ways machine learning systems operate."
    - 9-12 "Define supervised, unsupervised, and reinforcement learning algorithms..."
  - 3-A-ii, finding patterns:
    - K-2 "Identify patterns in labeled data and determine the features that predict labels" (e.g., "poisonous fish" cartoon images)
    - 3-5 "Model how supervised learning identifies patterns in labeled data" (students draw a decision tree)
    - 6-8 "Model how unsupervised learning finds patterns in unlabeled data" (clusters)
    - 9-12 adjusting model parameters (regression)
  - 3-A-iii, training a model:
    - K-2 "Demonstrate how to train a computer to recognize something" (Teachable Machine with instructor help)
    - 3-5 "Train a classification model using machine learning, and then examine the accuracy of the model on new inputs" (Teachable Machine or Machine Learning for Kids)
    - 6-8 "Train and evaluate a classification or prediction model using machine learning on a tabular dataset"
    - 9-12 supervised or unsupervised training on real-world data
  - 3-A-iv, training vs using a model: K-2 N/A; then "Demonstrate how training data are labeled when using a machine learning tool"; "Explain the difference between training and using a reasoning model"; at 9-12 the full pipeline, including cross-validation and test sets.
  - 3-A-v and 3-A-vi:
    - 3-5 "Analyze a game where one constructs a decision tree..."
    - 6-8 "Compare how a decision tree learning algorithm works vs. how a neural network learning algorithm works"
    - Reinforcement learning ("learn from experience (i.e., trial and error)") appears in the upper bands.
  - 3-B neural networks: K-2 and 3-5 N/A. 6-8 "Illustrate how a neural network of 1 to 3 neurons is a function that computes an output" and adjust weights (perceptron rule). 9-12 backpropagation and architectures (CNN, RNN, GAN).
  - 3-C-i datasets:
    - K-2 "Create a labeled dataset with explicit features to illustrate how computers can learn to classify things like foods, movies, or toys"
    - 3-5 "Create a labeled dataset with explicit features of several types and use a machine learning tool to train a classifier on this data"
    - 6-8 decision-tree feature encodings
    - 9-12 compare real datasets
  - 3-C-ii large datasets, e.g., ImageNet: starts above K-2.
- **Big Idea 5 (Societal Impact).** Released 22 December 2022. Topics: Ethical AI, AI & Culture, AI & the Economy, AI for Social Good. Source for all items: [BI5 chart PDF](https://ai4k12.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/AI4K12-Big-Idea-5-Progression-Chart-Working-Draft_v.0.1_12.22.2022.pdf).
  - 5-A-i:
    - K-2 "Evaluate the ways a decision impacts people differently."
    - 3-5 "Evaluate how an AI technology can have different effects on different groups of people."
    - 6-8 "Evaluate the ways various stakeholders' goals and values influence the design of AI systems."
    - 9-12 "Explain how use of AI systems has led to disparate impacts on different groups."
  - 5-A-ii, ethical design criteria: begins K-2 with "Discuss the characteristics of systems that are fair and unfair..." Middle bands evaluate how an AI system meets design criteria. 9-12 "Analyze an AI system to determine whether it satisfies ethical design criteria."
  - 5-B-i, AI in daily life:
    - K-2 "Identify devices in daily life that use AI technologies"
    - 3-5 "Describe how AI-powered services are used in daily life"
    - 6-8 examine an aspect of daily life predicted to change
    - 9-12 debates as AI is woven into culture
  - 5-B-ii, 5-A-iii and 5-C-ii have only three objectives each, so the column alignment is uncertain. In order:
    - 5-B-ii: "Analyze deepfake images or videos and identify the flaws..." (likely 3-5), then "Critique uses of AI technology that can be used to surveil people or violate their privacy" (likely 6-8), then AI regulation (9-12).
    - 5-A-iii: "Create a model card for a classification or prediction model", then stakeholder values, then "Design an AI System using an ethical design process" (9-12).
    - 5-C-ii: "Describe some jobs that no longer exist due to advances in technology" (K-2), then "Describe how a job will change due to the introduction of AI or robotic technologies", then predicting new or lost jobs, then (9-12) "Investigate the skills needed for AI-enabled careers".
  - 5-D AI for Social Good: from the middle bands, "Describe and use some of the AI extensions or plugins available in a programming framework" and "Create a novel application using... AI extensions". Also "Design a solution to a societal problem that makes use of AI technology."
- **Earlier wording (2019 ISTE presentation excerpt):**
  - Grades 3-5: "Explore how behavior is influenced by bias and how it affects decision making"; "Describe ways that AI systems can be designed for inclusivity."
  - Grades 6-8: "Explain potential sources of bias in AI decision making."
  — [exploreaiethics.com summary](https://www.exploreaiethics.com/curriculum/k-12-guidelines-for-artificial-intelligence-what-students-should-know/) (via search snippet; superseded by the 2022 chart)
- **Influence on CSTA 2026:** The 2026 CSTA standards list the "AI4K12 Guidelines (2022)" among their sources. — [2026 Standards PDF](https://csteachers.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/2026-CSTA-PK%E2%80%9312-Computer-Science-Standards.pdf)

### Inferences
- AI4K12 is more technical than CSTA 2026 at the same grades:
  - AI4K12 3-5 includes decision trees and training a classifier, while CSTA delays independent training to Grade 5.
  - AI4K12 6-8 includes neural networks of 1-3 neurons and unsupervised clustering, which do not appear in CSTA MS foundational standards (CSTA puts neural networks in the HS AIN specialty, S1-AIN-DS-06).
- CSTA 2026 is the "adoptable standards" layer. AI4K12 is a deeper, still-draft guideline layer that curriculum developers often cite.

## 4. ISTE Standards for Students (Computational Thinker), ISTE CT Competencies for educators, ISTE/ASCD AI guidance

### Takeaway

### Cited Findings

- **The four indicators:**
  - 1.5.a Problem Definitions: "Students formulate problem definitions suited for technology-assisted methods such as data analysis, abstract models and algorithmic thinking in exploring and finding solutions."

## 5. Other relevant frameworks: NGSS, AILit (EC/OECD), Digital Promise, TeachAI, CSTA/AI4K12 AI Learning Priorities, state policy data

### Takeaway
- NGSS embeds "Using Mathematics and Computational Thinking" as Science & Engineering Practice 5 across K-12.
- The EC/OECD "Empowering Learners for the Age of AI" AI Literacy Framework (AILit) went from a May 2025 draft to a final version in June 2026. It has 4 domains and 19 competences and will inform PISA 2029.

- The CSTA & AI4K12 "AI Learning Priorities for All K-12 Students" (2025) directly fed the AI content of the 2026 CSTA standards.

### Cited Findings
- **NGSS Practice 5 (NRC Framework 2012, NGSS 2013):**
  - Appendix F's "practices matrix" lists what students should reach by the end of each band (K-2, 3-5, 6-8, 9-12).

  - 3-5: "Mathematical and computational thinking in 3–5 builds on K–2 experiences and progresses to extending quantitative measurements to a variety of physical properties and using computation and mathematics to analyze data and compare alternative design solutions."

  - Title: "Empowering Learners for the Age of AI: An AI Literacy Framework for Primary and Secondary Education".

  — [ailiteracyframework.org blog](https://ailiteracyframework.org/blog/empowering-learners-for-the-age-of-ai-literacy-framework/) (via search summary); [EdTech Innovation Hub](https://edtechinnovationhub.com/news/european-commission-and-oecd-set-19-ai-literacy-competences-for-schools)

  - "AI Literacy: A Framework to Understand, Evaluate, and Use Emerging Technology", June 2024, by Mills, Ruiz, Lee, Coenraad, Fusco, Roschelle and Weisgrau.
  - Three modes: Understand, Evaluate, Use.
  - Ties AI literacy to computational thinking, data literacy, digital citizenship and media literacy.
  - Types of use: Interact, Create, Problem Solve.

  — [Digital Promise, June 2024](https://digitalpromise.org/2024/06/18/ai-literacy-a); [Exec summary PDF](https://digitalpromise.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/24cLSR0009-Exec-Summary-AI-Literacy-Framework-Paper_FINAL.pdf) (via search summary)

  - The full report needs a free CSTA account.
  - The 2026 standards tag a standard "AI-related" if it "closely aligns with a learning outcome from the AI Priorities for All K-12 Students project (CSTA & AI4K12, 2025)."
  — [CSTA AI Priorities](https://csteachers.org/ai-priorities/); [CSTA AI Learning Priorities](https://csteachers.org/ai-learning-priorities/); [NSF PAR record](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10603950); [2026 Standards PDF](https://csteachers.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/2026-CSTA-PK%E2%80%9312-Computer-Science-Standards.pdf)

### Inferences

  - G4: training-data properties and the term bias (E4-ALG-ML-02); human vs machine learning (E4-SOC-HU-18)

### Inferences
- For the CBSE Classes 3-8 comparison, the US 2026 baseline is: by Class 3 equivalent (age 8-9), conditionals in block code and observing ML retraining; by Class 5 (age 10-11), variables in programs and training a classifier; by Classes 6-8, procedures, flowcharts and pseudocode, rule-based vs data-driven reasoning, bias mitigation, model cards, and critical use of generative-AI tools and AI-generated code. Text-based programming is optional throughout.
- Actual US classroom implementation will lag the 2026 standards for years. Only a handful of states have AI in their standards as of late 2025, and adoption of the 2026 standards is just starting. A fair comparison is between CBSE's mandated syllabus and the US voluntary model standards, not with what typical US students experience.
- The "46 states" figure is CSTA's own count from its 2026 graphic. Other sources give 42-44 depending on date and definition.
