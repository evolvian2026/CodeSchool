# US Federal and State Policy, Mandates, AI Guidance and Funding for K-12 CS, CT and AI Education (current to Nov 6, 2026)

Context: The US has no national curriculum. K-12 content is set by the 50 states, and the federal government mostly acts through executive orders, competitions, grant priorities and guidance on how existing grant money may be spent. "Mandate" below means a state statute or regulation. "Guidance" means voluntary, non-binding documents.

## 1. Code.org / CSTA / ECEP "State of CS Education" reports: standards, offer and graduation mandates, K-8, PD funding, certification, the "10 policies"

### Takeaway
The latest edition is the 2025 report, renamed the "2025 State of AI + Computer Science Education" (data finalized Dec 1, 2025; covered Dec 10-16, 2025). It found high school CS access flat at 60% and participation slightly down at 6.1%. 12 states require a CS course to graduate (West Virginia was added in April 2025). No state yet required a combined AI+CS course, though Alabama enacted one in 2026 (class of 2032). In 2024, 32 states required every high school to offer CS. I found no 2026 edition published as of early November 2026. The report comes out in the fall, so one may be imminent.

### Cited Findings
**Report editions and framing**
- The latest edition is the "2025 State of AI & Computer Science Education," and it is the first to include state-by-state analysis of AI education policy. Its data was finalized Dec 1, 2025. — [Code.org advocacy, State of CS](https://advocacy.code.org/stateofcs/)
- The 2025 report adds four "AI + CS" policies. (1) **AI+CS standards:** AI is a named strand or is integrated across each grade band. (2) **AI+CS guidance for schools:** official state guidance emphasizing CS's role in AI literacy. (3) **AI+CS PD funding:** dedicated state funding. (4) **AI+CS graduation requirement:** every student must earn credit for a course covering foundational CS and AI, and the state publishes a list of qualifying courses. — [Code.org advocacy, State of CS](https://advocacy.code.org/stateofcs/)
- Code.org rebranded as "CodeAI" on June 2, 2026, shifting its focus to AI education. — [GeekWire, 2026](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/solidifying-its-shift-to-ai-education-code-org-rebrands-as-codeai/); [Code.org/CodeAI](https://code.org/en-US/codeai)

**2024 report numbers (2023-24 school year)**

### Inferences
- The best single "mandate" metric for comparison with CBSE is about 12 of 50 states (24%) requiring a CS course for high school graduation. That is a one-course high school requirement, not a K-12 sequenced curriculum like CBSE's CT&AI from Class 3.
- "Standards adopted" in nearly all states does not mean instruction is mandated. Most standards are voluntary frameworks for districts.
- The AI layer of US policy is very new: 4 states had AI in their standards as of late 2025.

## 2. Federal policy: EO 14277 (April 2025), Task Force, Presidential AI Challenge, pledges, ED guidance and the AI supplemental priority, CS for All (2016), 2026 developments

### Takeaway
Federal action is voluntary and incentive-based. President Trump signed EO 14277, "Advancing Artificial Intelligence Education for American Youth," on Apr 23, 2025. It created a White House Task Force on AI Education (chaired by the OSTP Director), the Presidential AI Challenge, and a public-private "Pledge to America's Youth" (60+ signers by June 2025). The Department of Education followed with a July 22, 2025 Dear Colleague letter saying existing formula and discretionary grant funds may be used for AI. It finalized a Secretary's Supplemental Priority on AI on Apr 13, 2026 (91 FR 18774, effective May 13, 2026). The first Challenge drew over 20,000 students (awards June 9, 2026). It was relaunched Oct 13, 2026 as the "Presidential Super Intelligence Challenge" following a September 2026 EO. None of these mandate curriculum.

**Earlier federal action: CS for All (2016)**
- On Jan 30, 2016, President Obama announced "Computer Science for All."
  - It proposed $4 billion over 3 years for states (in the FY2017 budget request) and $100 million in competitive grants for districts.
  - NSF was to spend more than $120 million over 5 years, and CNCS $17 million, on CS teachers.
  - The $4 billion was a budget request that Congress did not enact as proposed.
  - [Obama White House archive](https://obamawhitehouse.archives.gov/blog/2016/01/30/computer-science-all); [CRA](https://cra.org/govaffairs/blog/2016/01/president-announces-huge-new-computer-science-for-all-initiative); [EdSurge](https://edsurge.com/news/2016-01-30-obama-announces-computer-science-for-all-initiative); [GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2016/president-obama-announces-over-4-billion-to-go-towards-computer-science-for-all-initiative/)

### Inferences
- The federal levers are an EO, a student competition, corporate pledges, permission to spend existing Title or discretionary money, and a grant scoring preference. None sets content or requires instruction. This is structurally the opposite of CBSE's centrally issued curriculum.
- The 2026 rebranding from "AI" to "super intelligence" is a terminology change at the White House level. Its effect on state policy is unknown.

## 3. State AI guidance and AI-literacy laws

### Takeaway
As of August 21, 2026, about 35 states plus Puerto Rico had official K-12 AI guidance. California (Sept 2023) and Oregon (2023) were the first, and Illinois (June 2026) the most recent; nearly all of it is voluntary. Binding laws are fewer but growing: California AB 2876 (2024) requires the curriculum commission to consider AI literacy, Ohio HB 96 (2025) requires district AI policies by July 1, 2026, and in 2026 13 AI-in-classroom bills were enacted (FutureEd). The 2026 laws include Alabama HB 329 (AI+CS graduation course, class of 2032), Utah HB 218 (required grade 7 or 8 digital skills course with AI literacy), Maryland SB 720, Idaho SB 1227, Oklahoma SB 1734 and Virginia HB 1186.

### Cited Findings
**State guidance (voluntary)**
- 35 states plus Puerto Rico had official AI guidance or policy as of Aug 21, 2026. New Jersey, New York and Tennessee are listed as having no official state guidance.
  - Earliest: California (Sept 2023) and Oregon (2023).
  - Others: Alabama (June 2024), Wisconsin (June 2024), Colorado (Aug 2024), Louisiana (Fall 2024), Georgia (Jan 2025), New Mexico (May 2025), Missouri (2025-26), Vermont (Jan 23, 2026) and Illinois (June 2026).
  - [AI for Education state tracker](https://www.aiforeducation.io/ai-resources/state-ai-guidance)
- Other counts: Ballotpedia reported 31 states as of Jan 2026. Code.org's 2025 report counted 34 states with AI guidance. Playlab claims 40 plus PR (unreliable dating). — [Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/AI_guidance_issued_by_state_departments_of_education); [Playlab](https://learn.playlab.ai/resources/aistatepolicies); count summary via search aggregation
- Only 17 states' guidance frames CS as fundamental to AI literacy. — [K-12 Dive, Dec 2025](https://www.k12dive.com/news/most-states-still-dont-tie-ai-literacy-and-computer-science-together/807525/)

**Laws (binding)**
- **California AB 2876 (signed Sept/Oct 2024):**
  - Amends Ed Code §33548 so the Instructional Quality Commission must "consider" adding AI literacy to the math, science and history-social science frameworks at their next revision after Jan 1, 2025, and to instructional-materials criteria.
  - It defines AI literacy as the knowledge, skills and attitudes about how AI works, including its principles, applications, limitations, implications and ethics.
  - The verb is "consider," so it is softer than press reports that it "mandates" AI literacy in every grade.
  - [LegiScan bill text](https://legiscan.com/CA/text/AB2876/id/2977688); [Asm. Berman press release, Oct 3, 2024](https://berman.asmdc.org/press-releases/20241003-california-teach-ai-literacy-every-grade); [CalChamber](https://advocacy.calchamber.com/2024/10/02/governor-signs-calchamber-sponsored-bill-to-teach-ai-literacy-in-school/)
- California SB 1288 (2024) requires state guidance and a model AI policy, which CDE labels non-mandatory. Single source. — [TrueMadeAI tracker](https://www.truemadeai.com/resources/state-k12-ai-laws-and-guidance)
- **Ohio HB 96 (FY2026-27 budget, 2025):**
  - Every district, community school and STEM school must adopt an AI-use policy by July 1, 2026. They may adopt the state model policy, which the Department of Education and Workforce released in early January 2026.
  - The law does not require schools to use AI.
  - [Ohio DEW model policy](https://education.ohio.gov/Topics/AI-in-Ohio-s-Education/Model-Policy); [Ohio DEW, Jan 6, 2026](https://education.ohio.gov/Media/Ed-Connection/Jan-6-2026/Department-releases-model-policy-for-artificial-in); [Axios Columbus, July 8, 2025](https://www.axios.com/local/columbus/2025/07/08/ohio-schools-require-ai-policy-artificial-intelligence)
- **2026 session (FutureEd tracker, updated Oct 6, 2026):** 87 bills in 28 states on AI in classroom instruction, 13 enacted. The tracker details six:
  - **Alabama HB 329:** Students must complete an approved CS course that includes AI instruction to graduate, with the full requirement starting for the class of 2032. This appears to be the first AI+CS graduation requirement.
  - **Utah HB 218:** A required grade 7 or 8 digital skills course that includes AI literacy, cybersecurity and digital privacy.
  - **Maryland SB 720 (AI Ready Schools Act):** State guidance, district AI policies and district AI coordinators. It creates a statewide AI Education Collaborative, adds AI literacy to college, career and workforce readiness, and funds paid train-the-trainer PD.
  - **Idaho SB 1227:** The state will develop a generative AI framework that includes AI literacy standards and PD, and districts must adopt aligned policies.
  - **Oklahoma SB 1734 (Responsible Technology in Schools Act):** Districts must adopt AI policies before 2027-28. AI use must be educator-directed and human-in-the-loop, AI cannot be the primary basis for high-stakes decisions, and parents can opt their children out.
  - **Virginia HB 1186:** An AI Innovation in Education Pilot Program, state guidance, and district policies.
  - [FutureEd 2026 Legislative Tracker](https://www.future-ed.org/legislative-tracker-2026-state-ai-in-education-bills/)
- Illinois and Tennessee: secondary sources conflict on Illinois bill numbers and status (HB 316 vs. SB 1920), and on Tennessee's 2024 law requiring district AI policies. Both are unverified. — [aiteacherhacks blog](https://aiteacherhacks.com/ai-literacy-laws-for-schools/) (low-reliability aggregator)

### Inferences
- Most US state AI activity is governance (use policies, privacy, academic integrity) rather than curriculum. Only Alabama (a graduation course) and Utah (a middle-school course) clearly mandate AI instruction, both from 2026.
- I found no state law requiring AI instruction in elementary grades. CBSE's CT&AI from Class 3 is ahead of every US state mandate I found.

### Gaps
- FutureEd's other 7 enacted 2026 bills are not detailed.
- I have not verified Illinois's AI literacy law, Tennessee's 2024 policy law (Public Chapter re SB 1711), or the details of Louisiana and Ohio AI literacy courses.
- I have not verified TeachAI or CRPE tracker counts. The AI for Education tracker is the primary count used.

## 4. States with strong elementary and middle CS policy

### Takeaway
I could confirm only a few K-8 mandates from accessible sources. Indiana's 2018 law (HB 281) required high schools to offer CS by 2021-22 and only asked boards to "make efforts" in K-8; later expansion to elementary schools is reported. Florida (middle schools) and Iowa (elementary) have also been reported as requiring CS offerings. The 2024 report said 17 states had extended course requirements to middle or elementary schools but did not name them in the coverage I accessed. Utah's 2026 grade 7 or 8 AI-literacy course requirement is the clearest recent middle-school mandate.

### Inferences
- K-8 CS mandates in the US are mostly "offer" requirements (schools must provide CS). They are not "every student, every grade" sequences, and standards are typically voluntary.

## 5. Teacher certification and endorsement policies for CS

### Takeaway
Most states have some CS teacher certification or endorsement. An older NASBE figure from 2023 data says 43 states have policies requiring a CS certification, but I could not find a current (2025-26) count. Code.org counts a state as having the policy if it has a credential named "computer science" (or similar) that enables someone to teach CS courses.

### Cited Findings
- Policies require teachers to hold a CS certification in 43 states (2023 data). — [NASBE, "Navigating Systemic Access to CS Learning"](https://www.nasbe.org/navigating-systemic-access-to-computer-science-learning/) (via search summary)
- Code.org's definition: an endorsement, certification or licensure named "computer science" (or similar, e.g., computer programming) that enables teaching CS courses. Pathways vary widely, and common routes include "test-out" endorsements via Praxis or Pearson exams (e.g., Missouri, South Carolina, Texas). — [Code.org Teacher Pathway Recommendations (2024)](https://code.org/files/TeacherPathwayRecommendations.pdf); [CSTA recommendations](https://csteachers.org/resources-library/recommendations-for-states-developing-computer-science-teacher-pathways)
- New York will require credentials for all CS teachers (reported Feb 2024). — [Slashdot summary](https://news.slashdot.org/story/24/02/24/1633203/new-york-will-start-requiring-credentials-for-all-cs-teachers) (secondary)
- New Mexico HB 297 (2025) would codify CS endorsement pathways and reinstate two that expired Jan 1, 2025. — [NM LESC analysis](https://nmlegis.gov/Sessions/25%20Regular/LESCAnalysis/HB0297.PDF)
- Nevada offers two add-on CS endorsements, and Rhode Island has a RIDE CS endorsement (2025). — [Nevada STEM Hub](https://www.stemhub.nv.gov/csfornv/educators); [CS4RI](https://www.cs4ri.org/cs-endorsement-2025)

### Inferences
- Certification is widespread but often optional or add-on. Many CS teachers are cross-trained from math or business.

### Gaps
- I found no verified 2025 or 2026 count of states with CS certification, preservice programs or dedicated PD funding. These should be checked against the Code.org 2025 report tables.

## 6. Private funding and industry roles

### Takeaway
Industry and philanthropy carry much of US K-12 AI and CS delivery: curricula, PD and tools. The September 2025 White House commitments include Google ($1B for US education and job training, $150M in grants), Microsoft (free Copilot for K-12 for a year, $1.25M in Challenge prizes), Amazon (4M learners by 2028, up to $30M in credits), NVIDIA ($25M over 5 years for K-12), OpenAI (certify 10M workers by 2030), Anthropic ($1M over 3 years for K-12) and Code.org (AI+CS for 9M K-8 students a year). Most of these are company-reported, self-defined pledges, not audited spending.

### Cited Findings
All of the following are from [WhiteHouse.gov, "Major Organizations Commit to Supporting AI Education," Sept 9, 2025](https://www.whitehouse.gov/releases/2025/09/major-organizations-commit-to-supporting-ai-education):
- **Google:** $1B for US education and job training, including $150M in grants over 3 years ($3M to Code.org, $2M to Flourish Fund). Free Gemini for Education for US high schools.
- **Code.org:** 25M learners in an "Hour of AI." AI pathways in 25 states. AI Foundations course reaching 400K high schoolers a year. AI+CS for 9M K-8 students a year.
- **Microsoft:** Free Copilot for K-12 students and teachers for a school year. $1.25M in Presidential AI Challenge prizes. Free AI certifications for community-college faculty.
- **Amazon:** Reach 4M US learners by 2028. AI curricula for 10K educators. Up to $30M in cloud and AI credits.
- **NVIDIA:** $25M over 5 years for K-12 AI skills.
- **OpenAI:** Certify 10M workers by 2030.
- **Anthropic:** $1M over 3 years for K-12 AI and cybersecurity education. An educator AI Fluency curriculum under a Creative Commons license.
- **Meta:** Over $20M in educator and student AI resources.
- **Others:**
  - IBM: skill 2M learners by 2028.
  - Pearson: up to 250K free teacher certification vouchers.
  - Zoom: $5M over 3 years.
  - xAI: $10M.
  - Salesforce: $20M in AI-literacy grants.
  - Cisco: 1M students over 4 years.
  - Mastercard: 1M students over 5 years.
  - Booz Allen: 30K educators by 2028.
  - ISACA: free AI certification for up to 10K teachers.
  - Dell: technology partner for the Presidential AI Challenge.

### Inferences
- The US model relies on non-profit intermediaries (Code.org/CodeAI, CSTA, ECEP, AI for Education, TeachAI) and corporate pledges to supply curricula and PD. CBSE's model is centrally designed curriculum and training.

### Gaps
- Two Microsoft commitments are not in the White House release: the July 2025 "Microsoft Elevate" $4B pledge and Microsoft's National Academy for AI Instruction with the AFT ($23M with OpenAI and Anthropic). Both are known from background knowledge but were not verified in this session.
- I found no independent audit of whether the 2025 pledges were fulfilled as of Nov 2026.
