# US K-12 CS / CT / AI Education: Access, Equity, Participation, Assessment and Outcomes (as of Nov 2026)

Scope note: United States only. Research done 6 Nov 2026 with about 20 search/fetch calls. Several primary PDFs (Code.org State of CS full report, the Arkansas 2026 working paper) could not be fetched directly (404 / TLS certificate error), so some figures come from press releases or search-result summaries of primary documents. Each finding notes where that is the case. Code.org, CSTA and CSforALL are advocacy organisations (Code.org is partly funded by tech companies). Treat their framing, though not their school-level counts, as advocacy-sourced.

## 1. Share of US public high schools offering foundational CS, student enrollment, and equity gaps (incl. K-8)

### Takeaway
About 60% of US public high schools offered foundational CS in 2024-25. That figure has not moved since the 2024 report, which also said 60%. Only about 6% of high-school students actually enrol in a foundational CS course in a given year. Access is strongly tied to school size: 91% of large schools offer it against 44% of small ones. Girls are about one-third of enrolees even where the course is offered. Participation grew only in states with a CS graduation requirement. National K-8 access figures are not reported in comparable form.

### Cited Findings
- **2025 State of AI + CS Education (Code.org, CSTA and CSforALL; released 10 Dec 2025; data for 2024-25):** 60% of US public high schools offer foundational CS. National CS participation was "largely flat year over year". — [PR Newswire, Code.org/CSforALL release, 10 Dec 2025](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/new-report-ai--computer-science-are-the-foundation-for-us-k12-readiness-but-state-policies-lag-behind-302637515.html)
- **Enrollment, 2024-25:** 6.1% of eligible students were enrolled in foundational CS. — [EdWeek, Dec 2025](https://www.edweek.org/teaching-learning/how-to-get-more-students-into-computer-science-classes-in-the-age-of-ai/2025/12) (via search summary of the 2025 report)
- **States with a CS graduation requirement (2025 report):** CS access rose 18% and participation rose 5.1%. — [PR Newswire, 10 Dec 2025](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/new-report-ai--computer-science-are-the-foundation-for-us-k12-readiness-but-state-policies-lag-behind-302637515.html)
- **State graduation requirements:** 12 states require CS to graduate (2025 report). 0 states require both AI and CS. — [PR Newswire, 10 Dec 2025](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/new-report-ai--computer-science-are-the-foundation-for-us-k12-readiness-but-state-policies-lag-behind-302637515.html)
- **AI in state CS policy (2025 report):**
  - 4 of 50 states explicitly emphasise AI in their CS standards (CO, VA, ND, OH).
  - 5 states fund AI+CS professional development (AR, IN, LA, NJ, OH).
  - 17 of the 34 states with AI guidance say CS is fundamental.
  - Source: [PR Newswire, 10 Dec 2025](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/new-report-ai--computer-science-are-the-foundation-for-us-k12-readiness-but-state-policies-lag-behind-302637515.html); also [K-12 Dive](https://www.k12dive.com/news/most-states-still-dont-tie-ai-literacy-and-computer-science-together/807525/)
- **Gap by school size (2025 data):** 91% of large public high schools offered foundational CS, against 77% of medium and 44% of small schools. — [aiadoption.org analysis of State of CS data](https://aiadoption.org/data/us-high-schools-cs-by-school-size/) (secondary aggregator; the primary is the Code.org data portal)
- **States at full coverage:** Arkansas and Maryland report that 100% of their high schools offer a foundational CS course (2025 report). — [EdWeek, Dec 2025](https://www.edweek.org/teaching-learning/how-to-get-more-students-into-computer-science-classes-in-the-age-of-ai/2025/12) (via search summary)
- **2024 State of CS report (released 22 Oct 2024; data for 2023-24):**
  - 60% of public high schools offered foundational CS, and 6.4% of high-school students were enrolled.
  - Young women were 33% of CS enrolees, even where their school offered the course.
  - Black, Hispanic/Latino and Native American/Alaska Native students were less likely to attend a high school offering foundational CS. The release gives no percentages.
  - 11 states had a CS graduation requirement: AL, AR, IN, LA, NE, NV, NC, ND, RI, SC, TN.
  - More than $88M was allocated for CS in 2024 state budgets.
  - Source: [PR Newswire, Code.org/CSTA/ECEP, 22 Oct 2024](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/2024-state-of-computer-science-education-highlights-growing-investment-from-policymakers-continued-gaps-in-access-302282502.html)
- **English learners, students with disabilities, economically disadvantaged students:** the 2023 report found all three groups underrepresented in foundational CS relative to their share of the population. Code.org's data portal breaks down 9-12 foundational CS enrollment by gender, race/ethnicity, EL status, FRL eligibility and special-education status for 42 states. — [Code.org State of AI + CS Data portal](https://advocacy.code.org/report-data/); [Code.org Medium, State of CS 2023](https://codeorg.medium.com/state-of-cs-2023-u-s-sees-biggest-growth-since-2018-a332768312cc)
- **Small schools in 2023:** small schools were twice as likely as others to lack foundational CS (2023 report). — [GovTech, "Over 10,000 High Schools Don't Teach Compsci"](https://www.govtech.com/education/k-12/code-org-over-10-000-high-schools-dont-teach-compsci)
- **Historical baseline:** the 2020 State of CS report found less than half of US high schools taught CS. — [Code.org Medium, 2020 State of CS](https://codeorg.medium.com/2020-state-of-cs-report-shows-less-than-half-of-u-s-schools-teach-cs-2c733e3c5d54)

### Inferences
- High-school access plateaued at about 60% between the 2024 and 2025 reports. The near-term growth lever looks like graduation mandates rather than voluntary course offerings.
- Even with 60% of schools offering CS, only about 6% of students take it in a given year. "Offered" is a weak proxy for participation. This matters for India, where CBSE CT&AI is a compulsory curriculum for Classes 3-8, not an elective.
- The school-size gap (44% of small schools) is a reasonable proxy for a rural gap, because small high schools are disproportionately rural. This is an inference, not a reported rural figure.

### Gaps
- **Rural/urban/suburban, FRL, EL and race splits:** I found no 2025 report figures for access or participation. The numbers sit in the Code.org data portal / full PDF, which I could not fetch (advocacy.code.org/state-of-cs returned 404).
- **Female share in the 2025 report:** not found. 33% is the 2024 figure.
- **K-8 access:** I found no national share of elementary or middle schools offering CS. The Google/Gallup K-12 CS studies, which reported principal- and parent-level figures, were not located in this pass.

## 2. AP Computer Science Principles and AP CS A: exam takers, demographics, pass rates; College Board AI course news

### Takeaway
In May 2025, about 176,000 students took AP CSP (61.7% scored 3+) and about 94,000 took AP CSA (67.0% scored 3+), per College Board's global score distributions. College Board has not launched a standalone AP AI course as of Nov 2026. Instead it is building AI and machine learning, plus a new "AI Create/Design Project", into AP CSP from 2027-28. AP Cybersecurity is reported for 2026-27.

### Cited Findings
- **AP CSP, May 2025 (global):**
  - 175,935 students; 61.7% scored 3 or higher; mean score 2.86.
  - Score distribution: 5 = 10.7%, 4 = 19.9%, 3 = 31.1%, 2 = 21.4%, 1 = 16.9%.
  - Source: [College Board, AP CSP Student Score Distributions 2025](https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap25-computer-science-principles-score-distributions.pdf)
  - Third-party trackers give slightly different numbers (175,174 students; 63.0% scoring 3+). Use the College Board figures. — [Total Registration](https://www.totalregistration.net/AP-Exam-Registration-Service/Compare-Score-Distributions.php?id=54&year=2019)
- **AP CSA, May 2025 (global):**
  - 93,906 students; 62,900 scored 3+ (67.0%); mean score 3.18.
  - Score distribution: 5 = 25.5%, 4 = 21.8%, 3 = 19.8%, 2 = 10.9%, 1 = 22.1%.
  - Source: [College Board, AP CSA Student Score Distributions 2025](https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap25-computer-science-a-score-distributions.pdf)
- **AP CSP redesign (reported 28 Sep 2026):** from the 2027-28 school year, AP CSP will embed generative AI and machine learning, algorithm development, data ethics, human decision-making and AI's societal impacts. A new "AI Create Project" will have students build an app that uses AI. AP Seminar is also being revised, removing generative-AI access for scored essay drafting. — [GovTech, "AI Spurs College Board Redesign of 2 AP Courses," 28 Sep 2026](https://www.govtech.com/education/k-12/ai-spurs-college-board-redesign-of-2-ap-courses)
- **Curriculum vendor guidance:** a vendor notes the CSP changes (an AI Design Project, updates to the Create task, exam changes) take effect in 2027-28, not 2026-27. — [CodeHS help, "AP CSP 27-28 course updates"](https://help.codehs.com/en/articles/15482938-ap-csp-27-28-course-updates) (vendor source)
- **AP Cybersecurity:** reported as launching in 2026-27 in partnership with Cisco. A second outlet ties the new career-oriented APs (cybersecurity, business) to the US Chamber of Commerce and IBM. — [Blavity](https://blavity.com/college-board-adds-new-ap-courses); [Fortune, 18 Oct 2025](https://fortune.com/2025/10/18/gen-z-segregation-high-school-wealthy-privilege-advanced-placement-business-chamber-college-board/) (secondary; not verified against a College Board release)
- **Standalone AP AI course:** a consumer blog claimed an "AP Artificial Intelligence" course for 2026-27, then said no such course had been officially announced. I found no College Board confirmation. — [joingerald.com](https://joingerald.com/learn/financial-wellness/new-ap-courses-2026) (unreliable)

### Inferences
- About 270,000 AP CS exams were taken in 2025, against roughly 3.5 to 4 million students per high-school grade cohort. AP CS reaches a small minority of US students. This scale estimate is an inference, and the cohort size is not sourced here.
- AP CSP is the larger, more accessible entry exam: about 1.9 times as many takers as CSA, with a lower mean score. From 2027-28 it becomes the main national vehicle for AI-literacy content at high-school level.

### Gaps
- **Demographics:** I found no 2025 or 2026 shares of AP CS takers by gender, race or ethnicity. The College Board "AP Program Summary / demographic" reports were not retrieved.
- **Multi-year trend:** I did not collect a year-by-year series of takers (2017-2025). May 2026 score distributions were not retrieved.
- **"AP Career Kickstart":** no reliable information found.

## 3. NAEP Technology and Engineering Literacy (TEL) and assessment of CS/AI; state CS assessments

### Takeaway
NAEP TEL, a national-only grade-8 assessment, was given in 2014 and 2018. In August 2023 the National Assessment Governing Board voted to sunset it, cancelling the planned 2028 administration. There is currently no national US assessment of CS, CT or AI literacy. Some TEL content may move into the updated NAEP Science framework.

### Cited Findings
- **Sunset decision:** NAGB voted unanimously in August 2023 to sunset TEL and removed the 2028 administration. The stated reason was that multi-year framework and item-update cycles could not keep pace with technological change. Some TEL content may be included in the forthcoming NAEP Science Framework. — [NAGB news release, 2023](https://www.nagb.gov/news-and-events/news-releases/2023/governing-board-decides-to-sunset-the-naep-tel-assessment.html)
- **Administrations:** TEL was given in 2014 and 2018, grade 8 only, with national results only. — [NCES TEL page](https://nces.ed.gov/nationsreportcard/tel/); [NAEP TEL reporting results](https://www.nationsreportcard.gov/tel/about/reporting-results/)
- **First TEL release (2014 administration):**
  - 43% of 8th-graders performed at or above Proficient.
  - 18% of Black students did so.
  - Source: [NAGB TEL release deck](https://www.nagb.gov/content/dam/nagb/en/documents/newsroom/naep-releases/naep-tel-webcast-may-17/NAGB-TEL-Release-May-17.pdf)
  - The year is my inference from "first-ever release"; the document is dated May 17, which matches the 2016 release of the 2014 results.
- **2018 results:** published in NCES Digest Table 224.70, broken down by student and school characteristics. — [NCES Digest 2019, Table 224.70](https://nces.ed.gov/programs/digest/d19/tables/dt19_224.70.asp?current=yes) (table exists; I did not extract the values)

### Inferences
- The US has no national, comparable measure of student CS or AI competence. US "outcome" data at scale comes from AP exams (self-selected) and from administrative course-taking records. That is a structural contrast with any plan to assess CBSE CT&AI learning at scale.

### Gaps
- **2018 TEL figures:** I did not extract the 2018 Proficient share. Training memory suggests about 46%, but this was not verified; check NCES Table 224.70.
- **New NAEP plan:** I found no evidence of a NAGB plan to assess CS or AI directly.
- **State-level CS assessments:** not researched within budget. I found no source naming states with statewide CS end-of-course tests.

## 4. Outcome evidence: causal effects of CS/CT education; AI literacy outcomes

### Takeaway
The strongest US causal evidence comes from Maryland. A University of Maryland study by Liu, Conrad and Blazar (EdWorkingPaper 24-904, Jan 2024) found that taking a high-school CS course raises the probability of a CS bachelor's degree by 5 percentage points and raises earnings at age 24 by about 8%. A May 2026 Arkansas working paper reportedly finds that expanding access raised CS course-taking but produced no detectable effect on college, major or earnings up to five years out. That finding is provisional, because I could not read the full text. Meta-analyses of unplugged CT activities report large pooled effects on CT (g of about 0.6 to 1.0), but these are built on small, mostly non-US studies with publication-bias concerns. I found no rigorous evidence on AI-literacy instruction outcomes.

### Cited Findings
- **Maryland study (Liu, Conrad and Blazar; EdWorkingPaper 24-904, Jan 2024):**
  - Taking a high-school CS course raises the likelihood of earning a bachelor's in CS by 5 percentage points.
  - Attending a high school that offers CS raises the probability of employment at age 24 by 2.6 percentage points.
  - Earnings effects were estimated for students exposed to high-quality CS courses.
  - Source: [EdWorkingPapers ai24-904](https://edworkingpapers.com/ai24-904); [PDF](https://edworkingpapers.com/sites/default/files/ai24-904.pdf); [Maryland Longitudinal Data System Center brief, Feb 2024](https://mldscenter.maryland.gov/egov/Publications/ResearchSeries/2024/February/MLDSResearchSeriesComputerScience4All.pdf); [Brookings summary](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/high-school-computer-science-impacts-colleg-majors-and-increases-earnings)
- **Earnings claims as advocates cite them:**
  - One high-school CS course raises earnings at age 24 by "at least 8%". — [2024 State of CS release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/2024-state-of-computer-science-education-highlights-growing-investment-from-policymakers-continued-gaps-in-access-302282502.html)
  - The gain is 12% for Black students and 10% for young women. — [2025 State of AI+CS release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/new-report-ai--computer-science-are-the-foundation-for-us-k12-readiness-but-state-policies-lag-behind-302637515.html)
  - Both are advocacy restatements of the Maryland study; check the subgroup numbers against the paper.
- **Arkansas study ("The Impact of High School Computer Science Education: Evidence from Arkansas," May 2026, hosted by Arkansas DESE):**
  - Uses the 2015 statewide mandate that every public high school offer CS, with course availability as an instrument for CS-taking.
  - Search-result fragments say greater access raised CS course-taking but showed no detectable change in college attendance, CS major or earnings at 3 and 5 years. A fragment also reports no significant effect on employment.
  - Source: [Arkansas DESE PDF](https://dese.ade.arkansas.gov/Files/The_Impact_of_High_School_Cpmputer_Science_Education_2026_124639_20260707181726.pdf) (fetch failed on a TLS error; findings are provisional and the authors were not identified)
- **Arkansas background:** Arkansas has let CS count toward math or science requirements since 2015-16. It had the largest share of high schools teaching CS (89%) in a 2019 national report. — [Brookings case study](https://www.brookings.edu/research/how-arkansas-implemented-its-computer-science-education-program)
- **Unplugged CT meta-analysis (Chen et al., 2023, *International Journal of STEM Education*):**
  - 13 studies and 16 effect sizes.
  - Pooled Hedges' g = 1.028 (95% CI 0.641 to 1.415) on CT.
  - The authors flag a file-drawer (publication-bias) risk.
  - Source: [Springer, IJ STEM Ed, 2023](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40594-023-00434-7)
- **K-9 unplugged vs plugged meta-analysis (2025):** g = 0.631 (95% CI 0.463 to 0.799) for unplugged programming on CT. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/392341798_Comparing_the_effects_of_unplugged_activities_and_plugged_activities_on_the_development_of_students'computational_thinking_a_meta-analysis)
- **PBL meta-analysis (2025, 17 studies, n = 1,376):** plugged activities g = 0.818 against unplugged g = 0.649. — [EDUMATSAINS](https://ejournal.uki.ac.id/index.php/edumatsains/article/view/6958) (lower-tier journal)
- **Umbrella review of 33 CT syntheses:** effects vary by intervention type. Project-based, text-based programming and game-based learning show larger effects. — [PMC umbrella review](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12733880/)

### Inferences
- The two state studies point in different directions:
  - **Maryland** (taking a course, high-quality courses) shows positive effects on major choice and earnings.
  - **Arkansas** (mandated access everywhere) reportedly shows null downstream effects.
  - This suggests that universal offering without quality, which is the Arkansas mandate model, may not reproduce the Maryland effects. That is directly relevant to a mandated curriculum like CBSE CT&AI. The inference depends on confirming the Arkansas findings.
- The CT meta-analytic effects measure near-transfer CT tests, often designed by the researchers. They should not be read as evidence of effects on mathematics or later outcomes.

### Gaps
- **Code.org Baltimore study:** not located in this pass.
- **RCTs of elementary coding on CT or maths:** not retrieved. Candidate sources are ScratchJr/KIBO studies and the IES/WWC review; none was verified.
- **AI-literacy instruction outcomes:** I found no causal or quasi-experimental US evidence.
- **Arkansas paper:** authors, funding and exact estimates are unverified.

## 5. AI use in US schools: students, teachers, training, policy

### Takeaway
About half of US secondary students now use AI for school:
- 54% of teens per Pew (fall 2025).
- 54% of middle and high schoolers per RAND (2025).

About half to six in ten teachers use AI:
- 53% of ELA, math and science teachers (RAND).
- 60% of teachers, with 30% weekly (Gallup-Walton).

Policy and training lag behind. 45% of principals report an AI policy, about half of districts had trained teachers by fall 2024, and only 19% of students say their teachers explained how to use AI.

### Cited Findings
- **Pew, "How Teens Use and View AI" (published 24 Feb 2026; survey of US teens aged 13-17, 25 Sep to 9 Oct 2025):**
  - 54% of teens have used chatbots for schoolwork help.
  - 10% do all or most of their schoolwork with chatbot help, 21% some, 23% a little.
  - About 6 in 10 say students at their school use chatbots to cheat.
  - 12% have used chatbots for emotional support or advice.
  - Source: [Pew Research Center, 24 Feb 2026](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2026/02/24/how-teens-use-and-view-ai/); [report PDF](https://www.pewresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/20/2026/02/PI_2026.02.24_Teens-and-AI_REPORT.pdf)
- **Earlier Pew measure (ChatGPT only):** 26% of teens used ChatGPT for schoolwork in fall 2024, published Jan 2025, up from 13% in 2023. This is a different question from the 2026 "any chatbot" measure. — Reported in secondary summaries ([noxi.ai](https://noxi.ai/blog/ai-in-education-statistics)); not verified against Pew directly.
- **RAND, "AI Use in Schools Is Quickly Increasing but Guidance Lags Behind" (RR-A4180-1, 2025 survey panels):**
  - 54% of middle and high school students reported using AI for school.
  - 53% of ELA, math and science teachers used AI for instructional tasks.
  - 35% of district leaders provide AI training to students (spring 2025).
  - 45% of principals report school or district AI policies or guidance.
  - 34% of teachers report policies on AI and academic integrity.
  - 19% of students say teachers gave guidance on using AI for schoolwork (per the EdTech Current summary).
  - Source: [RAND RR-A4180-1](https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RRA4180-1.html); [PDF](https://www.rand.org/content/dam/rand/pubs/research_reports/RRA4100/RRA4180-1/RAND_RRA4180-1.pdf); [EdTech Current](https://edtechcurrent.com/articles/ai-guidance-gap-schools)
- **RAND American School District Panel (teacher training):**
  - About half of districts had trained teachers on generative AI by fall 2024.
  - Leaders projected that by the start of 2025-26 almost all low-poverty districts would have trained teachers, against only about 6 in 10 high-poverty districts. This is a projection, not an outcome.
  - Source: [RAND RR-A956-31](https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RRA956-31.html)
- **Gallup and Walton Family Foundation (survey of 2,232 US public-school teachers; published 24 Jun 2025):**
  - 60% of teachers used AI tools for work in 2024-25; 3 in 10 (32%) use them at least weekly.
  - Weekly users estimate they save 5.9 hours per week, extrapolated to "six weeks a year". This is a self-estimate.
  - Source: [Gallup, "Three in 10 Teachers Use AI Weekly, Saving Six Weeks a Year"](https://news.gallup.com/poll/691967/three-teachers-weekly-saving-six-weeks-year.aspx); [Walton Family Foundation report PDF](https://static.waltonfamilyfoundation.org/df/fb/eba12807470a9402d7433cc47dba/teaching-for-tomorrow-unlocking-six-weeks-a-year-with-ai-report.pdf) (foundation-funded)

### Inferences
- Student AI use is about half of secondary students, whichever survey is used. Yet roughly 1 in 5 students reports any teacher guidance, and fewer than half of principals report a policy. In the US, AI use is ahead of AI instruction. The CS-standards integration of AI is also thin: only 4 states emphasise AI in their CS standards (see section 1).
- The RAND poverty gap in teacher AI training is a new equity dimension that parallels the CS access gaps.

### Gaps
- **RAND 2025-26 wave:** any newer wave (fall 2025 or spring 2026) was not retrieved.
- **Common Sense Media:** 2024-2026 teen AI figures not retrieved.
- **District-policy share from NCES School Pulse Panel:** not retrieved.

## 6. Teacher supply for CS

### Takeaway
US CS teacher supply is thin. In 2020 only 82 of more than 150,000 newly certified teachers received CS certification. Most CS teachers come from other subjects: in 2020 only about 30% held a computer/technical sciences degree. Half of CS teachers have no CS colleague at their school (2025). I found no current national count of CS-certified teachers.

### Cited Findings
- **New certifications:** of more than 150,000 newly certified teachers in 2020, 82 received CS certification (US Department of Education data). — [AIR blog](https://www.air.org/resource/blog-post/three-ways-meet-growing-demand-k-12-computer-science-educators)
- **Certification routes, as of 2020:**
  - 19 states had an independent CS certification.
  - 42 allowed CS as an add-on to an existing license.
  - 24 had alternative pathways.
  - Source: [EdWeek, Oct 2023](https://www.edweek.org/teaching-learning/states-are-calling-for-more-computer-science-classes-now-they-need-the-teachers/2023/10)
- **CSTA/Kapor Center CS Teacher Landscape survey (about 3,700 PreK-12 CS teachers, summer 2020):**
  - About 30% graduated with a computer and technical sciences degree; 6% had a CS minor.
  - Among teachers holding at least one credential, 46% held one in computer and technical sciences and 23% in CTE.
  - Source: [CSTA, CS Teacher Landscape](https://csteachers.org/the-cs-teacher-landscape-results-from-a-nationwide-teacher-survey/); [Kapor Center](https://www.kaporcenter.org/the-computer-science-teacher-landscape-results-of-a-nationwide-teacher-survey/)
- **CSTA landscape follow-up (2025):**
  - Respondents teach at elementary (33%), middle (36%) and high school (51%) levels; multiple levels were allowed.
  - 74% have 0 or 1 CS colleagues at their school, and 50% have none.
  - Source: [CS Teacher Landscape site](https://landscape.csteachers.org/)
- **General teacher shortage context:**
  - 74% of public schools had difficulty filling at least one vacancy with a fully certified teacher before 2024-25, down from 79% the year before. — [NCES press release, 17 Oct 2024](https://nces.ed.gov/whatsnew/press_releases/10_17_2024.asp)
  - At least 406,964 positions (about 1 in 8) were unfilled or filled by teachers not fully certified. — [Learning Policy Institute, 2025 update](https://learningpolicyinstitute.org/blog/2025-update-latest-national-scan-shows-teacher-shortages-persist)

### Inferences
- About 70% of 2020 CS teachers lacked a CS or technical degree, and most CS teachers work alone at their school. The US relies on retraining teachers from other subjects through PD, as India will for CBSE CT&AI. The 82 new certifications in 2020 show the pre-service pipeline is negligible.

### Gaps
- **Current numbers:** I found no current national number of CS-certified teachers and no CS-specific vacancy rate.
- **Newer survey data:** I did not find 2025 figures for the share of CS teachers without a CS background. The 2020 figure is the latest verified.

## 7. Device and connectivity access in US schools

### Takeaway
US schools are close to universal 1:1 device coverage. About 88% of public schools reported a 1:1 program issuing every student a device in 2024-25 (NCES School Pulse Panel). In 2022-23, nearly half of schools also provided home internet to students who needed it.

### Cited Findings
- **1:1 devices, 2024-25:** about 88% of public schools have a 1:1 program giving every student a school-issued device (NCES School Pulse Panel, Feb 2025 release). — [Repost of NCES release, Feb 2025](https://educationresearchreport.blogspot.com/2025/02/more-than-half-of-public-school-leaders.html) (secondary repost; verify on the NCES SPP dashboard)
- **Home internet, 2022-23:** nearly half of public schools provided home internet access to students who needed it (NCES SPP, Aug 2022). — [THE Journal, 27 Sep 2022](https://thejournal.com/articles/2022/09/27/nearly-half-of-schools-providing-home-internet-access-to-students-who-need-it-this-school-year.aspx)

### Inferences
- Near-universal 1:1 devices mean US CS and AI instruction can assume "plugged" delivery. This contrasts with an Indian context where unplugged CT, as covered by the meta-analyses in section 4, may be a necessity rather than a pedagogical choice.

### Gaps
- **Original NCES release:** not fetched; the 88% figure relies on a repost.
- **Newer connectivity data:** I found no 2024-26 figures on hotspot or home-connectivity provision. Device types (Chromebook share) were not sourced.
