# How US K-8 Classrooms Teach Computational Thinking, Coding and AI: Curricula, Tools, Lesson Models and Teacher Support

Research date: 6 November 2026. US focus. Where a program comes from the UK or is global, that is noted. A note on names: Code.org now calls itself "CodeAI". Its site footer says "Code.org" is a former trademark of CodeAI. Both names appear below.

## Code.org / CodeAI: CS Fundamentals (K-5), CS Discoveries / AI Discoveries (6-8), AI Foundations (9-12), AI resources, Hour of AI, reach, and professional learning

### Takeaway
Code.org is the backbone of US K-8 computer science. CS Fundamentals has six grade-banded courses (A-F, K-5), each with 13-17 lessons of about 45 minutes. Lessons are teacher-led, mostly online block-based coding (Play Lab, Sprite Lab), mixed with unplugged activities. CS Fundamentals Unplugged is a separate device-free option. Code.org has moved hard into AI: there is a full-year middle-school "AI Discoveries" course (a reworked CS Discoveries), a high-school "AI Foundations" course, and many short AI units. In 2025 Hour of Code was relaunched as "Hour of AI", which Code.org and CSforALL report reached 15.5-17.9 million learners in 2025-26. Teacher preparation is free: one-day workshops run by Regional Partners plus self-paced online courses. Code.org says more than 100,000 teachers have attended CS Fundamentals workshops.

### Cited Findings
**CS Fundamentals (K-5) structure**
- The course-to-grade map is: Course A for K-1 (loops and events), B for grades 1-2 (ends with games in Play Lab), C for grades 2-3 (sequencing, loops, events, interactive games), D for grades 3-4 (algorithms and conditionals), E for grades 4-5 (introduces Sprite Lab), and F for grades 4-5 (introduces variables). There are also self-paced options: Pre-reader Express (K-3), Express (grades 3-8) and CS Fundamentals Unplugged (K-5, no devices) — [Code.org CSF page](https://code.org/educate/csf)
- Each course has 13-17 lessons designed for 45-minute periods. A course runs about a month, or can be spread over a quarter — [Code.org CSF page](https://code.org/educate/csf)
- Courses A-F are teacher-led and online, with unplugged activities and whole-class discussion mixed in. Supported devices are computers, Chromebooks and tablets — [Code.org CSF page](https://code.org/educate/csf)
- Older Code.org guides describe the course cores differently: "approximately 12 lessons in A & B, to nearly 20 lessons in Course F". Lessons run "20 minutes to more than 45 minutes" with extensions, or "30-45 minutes", and some classrooms spread one lesson over several days — [Code.org support: Teaching CSF Courses A-F](https://support.code.org/hc/en-us/articles/26001058366093-Teaching-Computer-Science-Fundamentals-Courses-A-F); [CSF Curriculum Guide](https://code.org/files/CSF_CoursesA-F_Curriculum_Guide.pdf)
- A Code.org forum post says Courses A and B assume about 30-minute class periods and C and D about 45 minutes — [Code.org forum](https://forum.code.org/t/amount-of-time-for-courses-a-f/7860)
- In Code.org's usage, "unplugged" means a lesson where students are off the computer. The full unplugged list is at code.org/curriculum/unplugged — [Code.org Unplugged](https://code.org/curriculum/unplugged)
- Every CSF lesson is mapped to CSTA standards and written with the K-12 CS Framework as guidance. The CSF page describes no AI-specific content inside Courses A-F — [Code.org CSF page](https://code.org/educate/csf)
- Code.org recommends about 20 classroom hours of CS Fundamentals per year for K-5 teachers — [Code.org PL Program for Me (PDF)](https://code.org/files/PL-Program-for-Me.pdf)

**Code.org elementary AI add-ons (outside Courses A-F)**
- "How AI Makes Decisions" (grades 3-5) is a week-long unit of hands-on predictive-data-model activities: sorting and categorising data. "AI for Oceans" (grades 3-12) has students train a machine-learning model to tell sea creatures from trash. "Dance Party: AI Edition" covers grades 3-8. There is a matching teacher module, "Teaching How AI Makes Decisions" (grades 3-5) — [Code.org AI page](https://www.code.org/ai)

**Middle school: CS Discoveries becomes AI Discoveries (grades 6-8)**
- AI Discoveries covers grades 6-8 and can be taught as a full year (36 weeks), a semester (18 weeks) or a quarter (9 weeks). Many units can stand alone — [Code.org AI Discoveries](https://code.org/curriculum/artificial-intelligence-discoveries)
- Its units are: 1 Thinking Critically About AI; 2 AI and Machine Learning; 3 Web Development; 4a Programming with Music Lab; 4b Interactive Animations and Games; 5 The Design Process; 6 Data and Society; 7 Creating Apps with Devices (Circuit Playground or BBC micro:bit). Tools are AI Chat Lab, Web Lab, Game Lab, App Lab, Sketch Lab and Music Lab — [Code.org AI Discoveries](https://code.org/curriculum/artificial-intelligence-discoveries)
- Students get an embedded AI Tutor in programming units. It gives hints without giving away answers, its interactions are deleted after 90 days, and they are not used to train third-party models. Teachers get an AI Teaching Assistant for planning, differentiation and pacing — [Code.org AI Discoveries](https://code.org/curriculum/artificial-intelligence-discoveries)
- AI Discoveries "grows out of CS Discoveries", which Code.org says has reached "hundreds of thousands" of students. The course aligns with the 2026 CSTA Standards and the TeachAI AI Literacy Framework, and more units are planned for 2027. Teachers need no prior CS background — [CodeAI announcement](https://code.org/codeai-launches-ai-foundations-and-ai-discoveries)

**High school: AI Foundations (grades 9-12)**
- AI Foundations Semester 1 has been in classrooms since May 2025. Semester 2, "Designing and Building with AI", has six units in which students learn JavaScript and build web apps with AI as a "creative partner". Either semester can be taught alone, or both together as a full year. Code.org says completers are prepared for AP CS A, AP CSP or workforce pathways — [CodeAI announcement](https://code.org/codeai-launches-ai-foundations-and-ai-discoveries)

**Other Code.org AI units and videos (6-12)**
- Other AI units include Exploring Generative AI (8-12), Generative AI for Humanities (6-12, 2 lessons), Coding with AI (6-12), Societal Impact of Generative AI (7-12), Computer Vision (9-12), AI and Machine Learning (6-12) and Our AI Code of Ethics (7-12) — [Code.org AI page](https://www.code.org/ai)
- "How AI Works" is a video series of nine videos plus an introduction, covering machine learning, training data and bias, neural networks, computer vision, chatbots and LLMs, creativity, privacy and algorithmic bias. Each video has a paired lesson for grades 6-12 — [Code.org AI page](https://www.code.org/ai)
- "AI Foundations" is the name of the high-school course, not an elementary product — [Code.org AI page](https://www.code.org/ai)

**Hour of Code becomes Hour of AI (2025)**
- Code.org and CSforALL launched Hour of AI as a free global initiative that replaces the December Hour of Code. It was anchored in CS Education Week, 8-12 December 2025 — [THE Journal, Oct 2025](https://thejournal.com/articles/2025/10/02/codeorg-reinvents-hour-of-code-as-hour-of-ai.aspx)
- The activity catalogue was released on 12 November 2025 with 100+ self-guided activities. One example is "Mix & Move with AI", where students design a dancer, remix music and choreograph a routine. Student activities were pitched at grade 3 and up. Backers include LEGO Education, Anthropic and Amazon — [THE Journal](https://thejournal.com/articles/2025/10/02/codeorg-reinvents-hour-of-code-as-hour-of-ai.aspx); [EdTech Innovation Hub](https://www.edtechinnovationhub.com/news/more-than-50-tech-companies-and-nonprofits-create-free-activities-for-the-hour-of-ai)
- CSforALL reports these 2025-26 results: 17.9 million learners engaged (a bullet list on the same page says 15.5 million), 2.6 million during CSEdWeek alone, 69,500+ educators, 175+ countries, 500+ partners, 130+ activities in plugged and unplugged formats, 1,750+ US district events, and CSEdWeek proclamations from 43 states plus DC — [CSforALL](https://csforall.org/news/the-hour-of-ai-a-global-movement-just-getting-started)
- A mid-week interim figure put participation at 13.1 million, 52.4% of a 25 million goal — [Slashdot summary of CSforALL](https://tech.slashdot.org/story/25/12/21/1724209/)

**Professional learning model**
- CSF workshops are facilitator-led one-day sessions run by Regional Partners, for individual teachers or whole schools. There is also a free self-paced online course, "Teaching Computer Science Fundamentals". Curriculum and platform are free. Code.org says most CSF teachers have no prior CS teaching experience and "no specialists are needed" — [Code.org CSF page](https://code.org/educate/csf)
- Code.org reports that more than 100,000 teachers have attended CSF workshops and that 90% would recommend them — [Code.org CSF page](https://code.org/educate/csf)
- AI Discoveries and AI Foundations use self-paced modules plus facilitator-led summer workshops, which start 8 June. A free CodeAI account is required — [CodeAI announcement](https://code.org/codeai-launches-ai-foundations-and-ai-discoveries)
- Free self-paced "AI 101" (K-12) and course-specific AI teacher modules are offered — [Code.org AI page](https://www.code.org/ai)

### Inferences
- A typical US elementary Code.org lesson is about 30-45 minutes: a short warm-up or unplugged discussion, then students solving progressive block-coding puzzles on devices. A course takes about a month, and Code.org's guidance is about 20 hours a year. The CBSE CT&AI books are printed puzzle sets with no coding environment. The closest US equivalent is the CS Fundamentals Unplugged track, which in the US is a supplement, not the main route.
- Code.org has moved its AI content mainly into grades 3+ (short units) and 6-12 (full courses). K-2 remains mostly classic sequencing, loops and events.

### Gaps
- I found no current, exact count of unplugged versus online lessons per course (A-F). The ratio varies by course and curriculum year.
- I found no Code.org-published 2025-26 count of active K-5 classrooms or students for CSF. Reach figures beyond "100,000+ teachers trained" and "hundreds of thousands" of CSD students were not found.
- Hour of AI participation numbers are self-reported by Code.org/CSforALL and internally inconsistent (15.5M vs 17.9M). There is no independent audit.

## MIT, Google, Raspberry Pi and other AI/CT tools and curricula (Scratch, ScratchJr, Day of AI, Experience AI, Common Sense, ISTE, AI4GA, Khanmigo, Google/OpenAI/Microsoft teacher academies)

### Takeaway
Beyond Code.org, US K-8 teachers draw on free, modular resources. MIT tools cover the early years (ScratchJr ages 5-7, Scratch roughly 8-16). MIT RAISE's Day of AI offers about 4-8 hours per grade band of 30-60-minute lessons. Experience AI, from the UK Raspberry Pi Foundation with Google DeepMind, targets ages 11-14 and is now promoted in the US via Raspberry Pi Foundation North America. Common Sense has 20-minute AI-literacy lessons. ISTE publishes "Hands-On AI Projects" guides, including one for K-5. Georgia's AI4GA built a nine-week middle-school elective. Google CS First was shut down on 30 June 2025 and replaced by the Raspberry Pi Foundation's Experience CS. Big-tech-funded teacher academies arrived in 2025-26: AFT's National Academy for AI Instruction ($23M, target 400,000 educators) and the Google + ISTE+ASCD AI training offered to all 6 million US educators.

### Cited Findings
**Scratch / ScratchJr (MIT)**
- ScratchJr is designed for ages 5-7 — [MIT Media Lab ScratchJr overview](https://www.media.mit.edu/projects/scratchjr/overview/)
- ScratchJr had more than 45 million users and more than 194 million projects as of August 2023 — [Boston College DevTech](https://sites.bc.edu/devtech/?p=409)
- Scratch is commonly described as designed for ages 8-16 (secondary source). One third-party site reports more than 121 million registered Scratch accounts as of November 2023, but this was not verified against Scratch's official statistics — [JetLearn (third-party)](https://jetlearn.com/blog/scratch-statistics)

**Google CS First (retired) becomes Experience CS (Raspberry Pi Foundation)**
- Google CS First, the long-running free Scratch-based curriculum used in US elementary and middle schools, was retired on 30 June 2025 and its data deleted. Google points teachers to Raspberry Pi Foundation's Experience CS, which offers Scratch-based units for ages 8-14 — [Google CS First Help](https://support.google.com/csfirst/answer/15848549); [Raspberry Pi Foundation blog](https://www.raspberrypi.org/blog/experience-cs-a-new-way-to-teach-computer-science/)

**MIT RAISE Day of AI**
- The curriculum is free, open source and covers "all K-12 grade bands", from "What is AI?" to generative AI, ethics, bias and privacy. It can be run over a day, a week or across the year. Free online workshops and training videos are offered for teachers — [Day of AI teachers page](https://dayofai.org/teachers)
- The 2023 impact report lists five grade bands (K-2, 3-5, 6-8, 9-12, and 9-12 AP CS), each with 4-8 hours of modular content — [2023 Day of AI Impact Report (PDF)](https://raise.mit.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/2023-Day-of-AI-Impact-Report-Final1.pdf)
- Each grade-level curriculum has about four hours of 30-60-minute lessons, released under CC-BY-SA — [THE Journal, 2023](https://thejournal.com/Articles/2023/06/06/Day-of-AI-K12-Curriculum-Freely-Available-Online-Anytime.aspx); [2022 Day of AI Impact Report](https://openlearning.mit.edu/2022-day-ai-impact-report)
- Day of AI also has an India-adapted programme page — [Day of AI Global India](https://dayofai.org/global-india)

**Experience AI (UK: Raspberry Pi Foundation and Google DeepMind; used in US)**
- Experience AI is a free programme for ages 11-14 that teachers of any subject can deliver. "Foundations of AI" is a six-lesson unit taught in order. "Understanding AI" is a four-lesson introduction, and "Solving problems with AI" is a follow-on module — [Experience AI units](https://experience-ai.org/en-IN/units); [Raspberry Pi Foundation blog](https://www.raspberrypi.org/blog/experience-ai-launch-lessons/)
- It was launched for UK learners. Google.org funding expanded it to 17 countries — [Google blog](https://blog.google/technology/families/improving-ai-literacy-in-young-people/)
- A 2026 job listing says the Raspberry Pi Foundation is focusing on AI literacy for ages 11-14 "across the US" through Raspberry Pi Foundation North America, a 501(c)(3) — [EdTech.com job listing](https://www.edtech.com/jobs/learning-manager-4440)

**Common Sense Education AI literacy lessons**
- The AI literacy collection spans K-12. Grades 6-12 lessons are about 20 minutes (for example, on AI bias), and 9-12 lessons are about 45 minutes (chatbots, deepfakes, checking information). A free educator account is required — [Common Sense Education AI literacy collection](https://www.commonsense.org/education/collections/ai-literacy-lessons-for-grades-k-12)
- District example: Charlotte-Mecklenburg Schools built K-5 AI literacy on Common Sense content and requires all 3rd-5th graders to complete a "What is AI?" lesson — [CMS district page](https://www.cmsk12.org/Page/16926)

**ISTE**
- The "Hands-On AI Projects for the Classroom" guide series (2021, ISTE with General Motors, by Nancye Blair Black and Susan Brooks-Young) has guides for elementary, secondary, electives, CS and ethics, available in English, Spanish and Arabic. The activities run from unplugged work to building chatbots — [AI4K12 listing](https://ai4k12.org/hands-on-ai-projects-for-the-classroom); [ISTE AI lessons](https://iste.org/ai-lessons)
- The elementary guide has four K-5 projects: What AI Does Well and Does Not Do as Well; Training Data and Machine Learning; Senses vs. Sensors; and Navigation and AI — [Williamsburg-James City district page](https://www.wps.k12.va.us/departments/technology/generative-ai-guidance/hands-on-ai-resources-for-the-classroom)
- Google partnered with ISTE+ASCD to offer free Gemini AI training to all 6 million US K-12 teachers and higher-ed faculty. Content launched on 13 May 2026 with monthly modules and microcredentials, aligned to ISTE+ASCD's "Profile of an AI Ready Graduate" — [Google blog](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/education/teacher-ai-literacy-training/); [EdTech Innovation Hub](https://www.edtechinnovationhub.com/news/google-and-isteascd-launch-free-gemini-ai-training-for-6-million-us-educators)
- Google's separate $1B "AI for Education Accelerator" mainly targets college students: free AI training and Career Certificates at more than 100 universities and community colleges — [Longstories (secondary)](https://longstories.ai/blog/google-invests-1b-in-ai-training-for-us-college-students)

**AI4GA (AI for Georgia) middle-school elective**
- AI4GA is an NSF-funded nine-week middle-school elective, "Living and Working with Artificial Intelligence". It is aligned to AI4K12's Five Big Ideas, mixes plugged and unplugged lessons (for example, breadth-first search taught through Georgia highway route-finding), and was built with four universities, the Georgia DOE and nine districts — [NSF PAR poster](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10405939); [AI4K12](https://ai4k12.org/ai-for-georgia/)
- The pilot had 299 students, 5 teachers and 4 districts. Later totals are 20 teachers given PD and more than 1,600 students. Teacher PD was a book club plus a two-week summer-camp co-teaching practicum — [NSF PAR poster](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10405939); [AI Magazine abstract via AAAI list](https://lists.aaai.org/ai4k12/msg/31246992/)
- Thomas County Middle School (Georgia) teaches the elective, and a teacher there helped develop AI curriculum for the GaDOE — [Times-Enterprise, June 2025](https://timesenterprise.com/2025/06/09/thomas-county-schools-at-the-forefront-of-ai-education-through-ai4ga-partnership)

**Khan Academy Khanmigo**
- Newark Public Schools' partnership covers 29,000 students in 66 schools (undated case study). Students in the 2023-24 pilot showed maths gains, according to the district — [Khan Academy case study](https://www.khanacademy.org/schools/case-studies/newark-public-schools); [Chalkbeat](https://chalkbeat.org/newark/2024/11/15/newark-receives-25k-gates-foundation-grant-to-expand-khanmigo-ai-tutor-chatbot)
- Khanmigo was being piloted in 266 districts as of December 2024 — [CBS 60 Minutes](https://www.cbsnews.com/video/khanmigo-ai-tutor-60-minutes-video-2024-12-08/)
- Khan Academy has said only about 15% of students with access actually use Khanmigo. A redesigned version is to roll out to all district partners in summer 2026 — [EdTech Innovation Hub](https://www.edtechinnovationhub.com/news/only-15-percent-of-students-with-access-to-khanmigo-actually-use-it-khan-academy-admits)
- New Hampshire's DOE gave free Khanmigo access to all its educators — [NH DOE](https://education.nh.gov/news-and-media/new-hampshire-department-education-enables-free-access-khanmigo-ai-pilot-all-new-hampshire-educators)

**National Academy for AI Instruction (AFT, 2025)**
- Announced on 8 July 2025 by AFT and the United Federation of Teachers. Funding is $23M: Microsoft $12.5M, OpenAI $10M, and Anthropic $500K in year one. The goal is to train 400,000 K-12 educators (about 10% of the US workforce) over five years, reaching 7.2M+ students. The flagship hub is in New York City, with more hubs planned by 2030. Training and curriculum are free to AFT's 1.8M members — [Chalkbeat](https://www.chalkbeat.org/2025/07/08/openai-microsoft-teachers-union-ai-training-partnership); [Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/microsoft-openai-anthropic-invest-in-teacher-ai-training/494372); [OpenAI](https://openai.com/global-affairs/aft)

### Inferences
- In US middle schools, AI literacy is typically delivered as short, plug-in modules: Day of AI (about 4 hours), Experience AI (4-6 lessons), Common Sense (20-minute lessons) and Code.org's single-week units. Dedicated electives exist (AI4GA at nine weeks; AI Discoveries at a quarter to a full year) but are less common. Most of these modules include hands-on model-training or chatbot use, unlike printed workbooks.
- The CS First retirement shows how US elementary coding depends on free platforms run by philanthropy or companies, which can disappear.

### Gaps
- I did not verify the current details of Teachable Machine (Google), Machine Learning for Kids (UK, Dale Lane) or MIT App Inventor's AI extensions (Personal Image Classifier and similar) in this session. They are widely referenced inside Day of AI, AI4GA and ISTE materials, but I have no fetched source for usage numbers.
- I found no current (2025-26) Day of AI reach numbers, and no official current Scratch statistics.
- I did not confirm the current content or US uptake of ISTE's paid online AI courses.
- No count of Khanmigo districts for 2025-26 was found.

## How elementary CS is delivered: integrated vs specials, minutes per week, who teaches

### Takeaway
There is no single US model. Code.org designs CS Fundamentals for the regular classroom teacher with no specialist, at about 20 hours a year. Many districts instead deliver CS through a weekly "specials" or STEM rotation, typically 50-150 minutes a week where reported. Some states, such as Arkansas, require CS to be embedded across content areas. National data on minutes per week and who teaches K-5 CS is thin.

### Cited Findings
- Code.org says CS Fundamentals is taught mostly by teachers with no prior CS experience and that "no specialists are needed" — [Code.org CSF page](https://code.org/educate/csf)
- Code.org's suggested load is about 20 classroom hours a year of CSF for K-5 — [Code.org PL Program for Me](https://code.org/files/PL-Program-for-Me.pdf)
- A Hanover Research brief (undated) found elementary CS programs dedicating 50-150 minutes a week. For example, Charles County Public Schools (Maryland) gives 50 minutes of computer class per week — [Hanover Research brief (PDF)](https://insights.hanoverresearch.com/hubfs/Elementary%20School%20Computer%20Science%20Programs.pdf)
- Gwinnett County (Georgia) uses a specials model. At Sugar Hill Elementary, "all students in grades Kindergarten through Fifth Grade receive Computer Science Instruction through Computer Science Specials and STEM Specials". Twenty-one GCPS schools received the CS100 award (CSisElementary and CSforALL), which recognises schools that commit to teaching CS to every K-5 student — [CSforALL member page](https://csforall.org/members/sugar_hill_elementary_school); [GCPS news](https://www.gcpsk12.org/get-connected/district-news-and-communication/all-news/gcps-full-news-article/~board/news-releases/post/21-gcps-schools-receive-cs100-award)
- In NYC CS4All, CS can be put into core classes or into "cluster" classes such as art, music or technology — [NYC CS4All overview](https://www.nyc.gov/site/partnerships/initiatives/computer-science-for-all.page)
- Arkansas' K-8 CS standards are to be embedded across content areas. A coding block appears in grades 7 or 8 — [Arkansas DESE CS standards](https://dev-dese.ade.arkansas.gov/Offices/ar-comp-sci-initiative/computer-science-standards-and-courses)
- The University of Chicago Outlier "Time for CS" study looked at how elementary schools make time for CS (findings page not read in detail) — [UChicago Outlier Time for CS](https://outlier.uchicago.edu/TimeforCS/findings)
- The 2025 CS Teacher Landscape survey (CSTA/AiiCE) covered 2,882 PK-12 CS teachers in fall 2024. It reports teachers are constrained by "limited preparation, heavy workloads, time restrictions, and inconsistent support". Grade-level breakdowns are in the dashboard and full report, which I did not read — [CSTA Landscape](https://landscape.csteachers.org/?p=1316); [2025 CS Teacher Landscape PDF](https://landscape.csteachers.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/2025-CS-Teacher-Landscape.pdf)
- RAND (spring 2025 American Educator Panels) found elementary teachers used AI less than English or science teachers. Overall, 25% of teachers reported using AI — [RAND coverage via Sinclair/NewsChannel 9](https://newschannel9.com/news/nation-world/25-of-teachers-use-ai-but-its-not-clear-if-students-are-benefiting-rand-research-schools-education-technology)

### Inferences
- A realistic US K-5 benchmark is one 30-50-minute CS session a week, in a specials rotation or inside classroom time, or a concentrated Code.org course of about a month (13-17 lessons of 45 minutes, about 10-13 hours). In both cases students use devices for most sessions.

### Gaps
- I found no nationally representative 2025-26 figure for weekly minutes of elementary CS, or for the split between classroom teachers and specialists. The CSTA 2025 Landscape dashboard probably has it, but it sits behind a download form.
- The national share of elementary schools offering CS in 2025 was not found. Code.org's 2025 report headlines high schools: 60% offer foundational CS and 6.1% of students enrol — [Code.org 2025 State of AI + CS (coverage)](https://advocacy.code.org/stateofcs/); [K-12 Dive](https://www.k12dive.com/news/most-states-still-dont-tie-ai-literacy-and-computer-science-together/807525/). The 2024 report said 37% of middle schools offered CS (secondary citation via search snippet; not verified in the full PDF).

## Example state and district implementations at elementary level

### Takeaway
Most large US implementations combine state K-8 standards with Code.org or Scratch-based curricula and a district CS office that trains teachers. Arkansas embeds K-8 standards across subjects. Chicago CS4All has a K-8 skills progression plus a high-school graduation requirement. NYC CS4All set out to put CS in every school by 2025 and trains teachers through district PD. Gwinnett County uses dedicated CS and STEM specials for every K-5 student at many schools.

### Cited Findings
- **Arkansas:** A legislative task force led to K-8 CS standards adopted in 2015, with implementation from 2017-18. Revised K-8 standards apply from July 2021, with separate K-4 and 5-8 documents. Standards are embedded across content areas, with a coding block in grade 7 or 8 — [Talk Business](https://talkbusiness.net/2015/12/arkansas-developing-k-8-computer-coding-standards-would-be-first-in-the-nation/); [Arkansas DESE](https://dev-dese.ade.arkansas.gov/Offices/ar-comp-sci-initiative/computer-science-standards-and-courses)
- **Chicago CS4All:** The 2013 plan included a K-8 CS pathway. CPS publishes a "Progression of Essential K-8 Computer Science Skills" adapted from the 2017 CSTA standards. CS has been a high-school graduation requirement since the class of 2020, approved in 2016. Reach figures conflict: "89 elementary schools and 74 high schools" in one source, "almost a third" of schools in another — [CPS CS4All elementary standards](https://sites.google.com/cps.edu/cs4all/for-teachers/elementary-school/standards); [Chicagoist 2016](https://www.chicagoist.com/2016/02/25/on_wednesday_the_chicago_board.php); [CS4All CPS parents page](https://cs4allcps.github.io/parents)
- **NYC CS4All:** The goal was CS in every elementary, middle and high school by 2025, with about 5,000 teachers trained to reach more than 245,000 students a year. In its sixth year it had trained more than 2,025 teachers across 825 schools (undated). NYU's Research Alliance published evaluation reports in 2025 on teacher PD and student outcomes, which I did not read in detail — [Fund for Public Schools](https://fundforpublicschools.org/computerscienceforall); [NYU Research Alliance](https://steinhardt.nyu.edu/research-alliance/research/building-teacher-capacity-equitable-computer-science-education)
- **Gwinnett County, Georgia:** CS is taught through CS specials and STEM specials for all K-5 students at schools such as Sugar Hill Elementary. Twenty-one schools have the CS100 award (every K-5 student taught CS). The district reports that 100% of K-12 cluster schools offer CS programs. Grade 3-5 students used Code.org Hour of Code activities at district Code Jam events — [CSforALL Sugar Hill](https://csforall.org/members/sugar_hill_elementary_school); [GCPS news](https://www.gcpsk12.org/get-connected/district-news-and-communication/all-news/gcps-full-news-article/~board/news-releases/post/21-gcps-schools-receive-cs100-award); [GCPS CS for All](https://www.gcpsk12.org/families/back-to-school-guide/back-to-school-news/news/~board/back-to-school-with-ctae/post/computer-science-for-all)
- **Charlotte-Mecklenburg (North Carolina):** K-5 AI literacy uses Common Sense content, and all students in grades 3-5 must complete "What is AI?" — [CMS](https://www.cmsk12.org/Page/16926)
- **Alabama:** 99% of elementary schools offer CS, per state-level data cited in Code.org reporting (search snippet) — [Code.org advocacy data](https://advocacy.code.org/report-data/)

### Inferences
- US districts typically start real coding in kindergarten or grade 1 (Code.org Course A, ScratchJr) and reach block-based app or game projects by grades 4-5. Middle school brings semester or year electives (CS Discoveries / AI Discoveries, AI4GA). That is a far earlier and more device-centred progression than CBSE's Classes 3-8 puzzle books.

## Teacher professional development, certification, and teacher AI training (2025-2026 surveys)

### Takeaway
US K-8 CS teaching relies on short, free, provider-run PD rather than certification. Code.org's one-day workshops have reached more than 100,000 CSF teachers. CS certification or endorsement mostly targets secondary teachers and varies by state. Teacher AI training rose quickly but remains incomplete. In Gallup-Walton's 2024-25 survey, 68% of teachers had received no school-provided AI training. EdWeek Research Center found 50% had at least one AI PD session by fall 2025, up from 43% in fall 2024. RAND found district AI training rose from 23% (fall 2023) to 48% (fall 2024) of districts, almost always optional.

### Cited Findings
- Code.org: one-day Regional Partner workshops plus free self-paced courses; 100,000+ CSF teachers trained — [Code.org CSF](https://code.org/educate/csf)
- Code.org's state policy tracking includes a "teacher certification" item: an endorsement, certification or licence named "computer science" that lets a teacher teach CS courses. Code.org recommends a full secondary CS endorsement and notes common "test-out" routes such as Praxis — [Code.org Teacher Pathway Recommendations](https://code.org/files/TeacherPathwayRecommendations.pdf)
- State examples: New York will require credentials for all CS teachers (reported February 2024); Washington must develop a K-12 CS endorsement; Nevada has two add-on CS endorsements; Rhode Island has a RIDE CS endorsement add-on — [Slashdot (NY)](https://news.slashdot.org/story/24/02/24/1633203/new-york-will-start-requiring-credentials-for-all-cs-teachers); [WA RCW 28A.410.224](https://codes.findlaw.com/wa/title-28a-common-school-provisions/wa-rev-code-28a-410-224/); [Nevada CS for NV](https://www.stemhub.nv.gov/csfornv/educators); [CS4RI](https://www.cs4ri.org/cs-endorsement)
- AI4GA teacher prep was a book club plus a two-week summer-camp co-teaching practicum — [NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10405939)
- **Gallup-Walton** ("Teaching for Tomorrow", June 2025, n=2,232 public-school teachers): 32% use AI at least weekly, 28% infrequently and 40% not at all. Weekly users save 5.9 hours a week, about six weeks a year. 68% got no school or district AI training in 2024-25. Among AI users, 52% taught themselves versus 31% trained by their school. Only 19% say their school has an AI policy — [Walton Family Foundation](https://www.waltonfamilyfoundation.org/the-ai-dividend-new-survey-shows-ai-is-helping-teachers-reclaim-valuable-time); [Walton NextGen Insights](https://nextgeninsights.waltonfamilyfoundation.org/resources/how-ai-gives-teachers-time-back/)
- A later Gallup-Walton report ("Closing the Expectations Gap") found only 18% of teachers received any formal AI guidance from administrators — [Peoria Times](https://www.peoriatimes.com/news/educators-flying-blind-on-ai/article_00c039e5-6710-4096-b16f-4f198f28bafb.html)
- **EdWeek Research Center:** 50% of teachers reported at least one AI PD session (nationally representative, n=693, 24 September-3 November 2025), up from 43% in fall 2024 (n=731 teachers). In 2024, about 58% had no training — [EdWeek, Nov 2025](https://www.edweek.org/technology/teacher-ai-training-is-rising-fast-but-still-has-a-long-way-to-go/2025/11); [EdWeek, Jan 2026](https://www.edweek.org/technology/more-teachers-are-using-ai-in-their-classrooms-heres-why/2026/01)
- EdWeek's May 2026 special report on the quality of AI teacher training exists, but I did not read its figures — [EdWeek, May 2026](https://edweek.org/technology/more-schools-are-providing-ai-training-for-teachers-is-it-any-good/2026/05)
- **RAND (district surveys):** 48% of districts provided teacher AI training by fall 2024, up from 23% in fall 2023, with 74% planned by fall 2025. The rate was 67% in low-poverty districts versus 39% in high-poverty districts. Training was almost always optional — [AI for Education summary of RAND](https://www.aiforeducation.io/blog/rand-research-reveals-growing-ai-training-gap)
- **RAND (spring 2025 American Educator Panels, reported July 2026):** 25% of teachers used AI, nearly two-thirds of them only for lesson planning; about 60% of principals used AI. Elementary and maths teachers reported lower use — [NewsChannel 9 / Sinclair](https://newschannel9.com/news/nation-world/25-of-teachers-use-ai-but-its-not-clear-if-students-are-benefiting-rand-research-schools-education-technology)
- RAND's 2025 maths educator survey: in Texas, 52% of maths teachers used AI in instruction, but more than 20% had no training — [GovTech](https://www.govtech.com/education/k-12/texas-educators-professional-development-lags-behind-ai-use)
- Large-scale AI PD offers: AFT National Academy for AI Instruction (400,000 educators over 5 years) — [Chalkbeat](https://www.chalkbeat.org/2025/07/08/openai-microsoft-teachers-union-ai-training-partnership). Google + ISTE+ASCD free training for 6M educators from May 2026 — [Google blog](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/education/teacher-ai-literacy-training/). Code.org "AI 101" self-paced — [Code.org AI](https://www.code.org/ai)

### Inferences
- The figures differ by measure: share of districts offering training (RAND), share of teachers with at least one session (EdWeek), and share with school-provided training (Gallup). Together they suggest roughly half of US teachers had some AI PD by late 2025, mostly short, optional and of uneven quality.
- The US model is "free curriculum + short PD + no specialist required" at elementary level. That is the scalable route most comparable to what CBSE might need for Classes 3-8.

### Gaps
- I found no national count of states with a CS teacher certification or endorsement in 2025. The figure is in Code.org's state fact sheets, which I did not read.
- Several aggregator sites cite RAND figures I could not verify (for example "55% of teachers received AI training" or "68% weekly use, January 2026"). I excluded them.
- A 2026 Gallup update on teacher AI training rates was not found.

## Assessment in practice for K-8 CS/AI

### Takeaway
US K-8 CS and AI is largely unassessed by standardized tests. Assessment is formative and project-based: lesson-level checks and rubric-scored projects inside curricula like Code.org's. Bebras is an optional international CT challenge (held in the US each November) and the closest analogue to CBSE's puzzle style. I found no state that tests K-8 CS on a statewide test.

### Cited Findings
- Code.org maps every CSF lesson to CSTA standards. Courses use projects (for example, Course B ends with student-built Play Lab games) — [Code.org CSF](https://code.org/educate/csf)
- AI Discoveries is organised around projects such as web apps, Music Lab programs, animations and games, and device apps — [Code.org AI Discoveries](https://code.org/curriculum/artificial-intelligence-discoveries)
- Bebras USA is typically held in November. A 2020 report described the US challenge format as 45 minutes for 15 multiple-choice CT questions. Internationally, age categories run from "Beavers" (grades 3-4) to "Seniors" (grades 11-12). Global participation in 2024 was over 2.5-3 million across 80-93 countries (sources conflict) — [Bebras USA](https://bebraschallenge.org/); [KodeKiddo](https://kodekiddo.com/bebras-challenge-2023-result/); [Wikipedia: Bebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bebras_Competition)
- AI4GA evaluated its pilot with research instruments (299 students, five teachers), not state tests — [NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10405939)

### Inferences
- CBSE's puzzle-book approach looks most like Bebras-style CT tasks. In the US, those are an optional enrichment contest, not the core curriculum.

### Gaps
- I found no US Bebras participation numbers for 2024-25.
- I did not research state K-8 CS assessments in detail. I found no evidence of any state including K-8 CS on statewide summative tests, but I have not confirmed this.
- I found no published rubric or portfolio requirements from the districts named above.
