# India school system size and shape: schools, enrolment and teachers by management, level and state (UDISE+ 2025-26 latest, with 2024-25 and earlier for trend)

**Data year note (read first).** The latest UDISE+ year is **2025-26**, not 2024-25. The Department of School Education & Literacy (DoSEL), Ministry of Education released it on **7 July 2026** (data cut-off 31 March 2026), in two versions: "Existing Structure" and "NEP Structure" ([Vision IAS, 8 Jul 2026](https://visionias.in/current-affairs/news-today/2026-07-08/society/union-ministry-of-education-released-the-unified-district-information-system-for-education-plus-udise-2025-26-report); [EFA analysis](https://educationforallinindia.com/udiseplus-2025-26-analysis-by-aruncmehta-educationforallinindia/)). UDISE+ 2024-25 came out on 28 Aug 2025 ([EFA](https://educationforallinindia.com/udiseplus-2025-26-analysis-by-aruncmehta-educationforallinindia/)).

**Where the figures come from.** The official 2025-26 booklet is [UDISE+ 2025-26 Booklet (NEP)](https://dashboard.udiseplus.gov.in/report2026/static/media/UDISE+2025_26_Booklet_nep.94ceae1e8c2210549d21.pdf) and the 2024-25 one is [UDISE+ 2024-25 Booklet (Existing)](https://dashboard.udiseplus.gov.in/report-new-v.6-demo2025/static/media/UDISE%2B2024_25_Booklet_existing.118ba29d4773e6372f72.pdf). Both files were over 10 MB and could not be fetched directly, and PIB returned 403. Most national figures below therefore come from a year-by-year key-indicator compilation of DoSEL data by Prof. Arun C. Mehta, a former NIEPA professor ([EFA key indicators PDF, 2020-21 to 2025-26](https://educationforallinindia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/UDISEPlus-all-india-national-key-indicators-2020-21-to-2025-26-aruncmehta.pdf); "EFA-KI" below). I checked these internally: the management rows add exactly to the totals. State figures for 2024-25 come from a reproduction of **official UDISE+ 2024-25 Table 2.1 (report p. 31)** ([EFA upload of Table 2.1](https://educationforallinindia.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/State-wise-share-of-schools-enrolment-and-teachers-UDISEPlus-2024-25-Ministry-of-Education.pdf)).

**Methodology break (applies throughout).** From **2022-23**, UDISE+ moved from school-level consolidated returns to **student-wise records**: name, address, parents' names and Aadhaar, with more than 60 items per student. Data from 2022-23 to 2025-26 are comparable with each other but **not with 2021-22 and earlier** ([Outlook](https://www.outlookindia.com/national/enrolment-in-schools-declined-by-over-a-crore-shows-udise-report); [EFA](https://educationforallinindia.com/udiseplus-2025-26-analysis-by-aruncmehta-educationforallinindia/)). Pre-primary enrolment is not comparable across years ([EFA](https://educationforallinindia.com/udiseplus-2025-26-analysis-by-aruncmehta-educationforallinindia/)). The reference date also changed: 30 Sep for 2020-21 and 2021-22, then a 31 March cut-off from 2022-23 ([EFA-KI](https://educationforallinindia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/UDISEPlus-all-india-national-key-indicators-2020-21-to-2025-26-aruncmehta.pdf)).

## 1. Total schools and split by management; trend 2020-21 to 2025-26

### Takeaway
India has **14,66,682 schools (UDISE+ 2025-26)**:
- Government (state plus central): 10,05,245 (68.5%)
- Government-aided: 79,261 (5.4%)
- Private unaided recognised: 3,41,689 (23.3%)
- Others: 40,487 (2.8%). This includes unrecognised schools and some madrasas.

The total fell by about 42,000 (-2.8%) since 2020-21, almost all from consolidation of government schools. The private unaided count dipped in 2022-23 and has since recovered to about the 2020-21 level.

### Inferences
- **Government schools:** the net decline of about 27,000 since 2020-21 (-2.6%) fits state-level merger/"pairing" and rationalisation drives. Closures of zero- and low-enrolment schools appear to have continued into 2025-26 (zero-enrolment schools fell 29% in one year).
- **Addressable private segment:** about 3.4 lakh recognised private unaided schools plus about 0.4 lakh "others". Unrecognised schools are under-counted in UDISE+ because only schools with a UDISE code report.

### Gaps
- No separate official count of **unrecognised** schools versus other "Others" schools. The UDISE+ "Others" category mixes unrecognised schools with some madrasas.
- No list of school closures or mergers by state from UDISE+. The trend above is a net figure.

## 2. Student enrolment: total, by management, by level, Classes 3-8, and the post-2022 drop

### Takeaway
**2025-26 enrolment:**
- 24.72 crore students from pre-primary to Class 12 (24,72,19,766).
- 23.23 crore in Classes 1-12 (23,23,06,857).
- Classes 1-5: 10.19 crore. Classes 6-8: 6.32 crore.

Classes 1-12 enrolment by management:
- Government: 49.7%
- Aided: 10.5%
- Private unaided: 38.0%. This rises to 40.0% if pre-primary is included.
- Others: 1.9%

Estimated enrolment in **Classes 3-8 is about 12.4 crore** (my inference; not an official figure).

Primary enrolment has fallen by about 2 crore since 2021-22. Part of this is a data artefact: de-duplication after the 2022-23 switch to student-wise, Aadhaar-linked records. Part is real demographic decline.

- **Two private-share figures, and why they differ.** Secondary analyses quote "38.8% (2024-25)" and "39.99%, private crosses 40% (2025-26)" ([EFA private 40%](https://educationforallinindia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/private-schools-cross-40-percent-enrolment-mark-udise-2025-26.html)). Both include pre-primary. On a Classes 1-12 basis the shares are 36.8% and 38.0%. I reconciled this by arithmetic. Pre-primary is about 71% private ([EFA private 40%](https://educationforallinindia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/private-schools-cross-40-percent-enrolment-mark-udise-2025-26.html)).
- **Government enrolment fell by about 59 lakh from 2023-24 to 2024-25.** Careers360 gives government-school enrolment of "12,74,90,199" (2023-24) against "12,15,89,911" (2024-25) ([Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/udise-plus-2024-2025-report-sharp-drop-sc-st-obc-enrolment-government-schools-59-lakh-education-ministry-data)). The same article's text says "5.9 lakh", which looks like a typo for 59 lakh.
- **Why enrolment dropped after 2022-23.** Enrolment was above 26 crore from 2018-19 to 2021-22, then 25.17 crore (2022-23) and 24.8 crore (2023-24). The Ministry attributes this mainly to the 2022-23 shift to student-wise data with Aadhaar and verification at cluster, district and state levels. It says this "weeded out ghost students", i.e., children enrolled in both government and private schools ([Outlook](https://www.outlookindia.com/national/enrolment-in-schools-declined-by-over-a-crore-shows-udise-report)). Bihar (-35.65 lakh) and UP (-28.26 lakh) had the largest declines between 2018-19 and 2023-24 ([Outlook](https://www.outlookindia.com/national/enrolment-in-schools-declined-by-over-a-crore-shows-udise-report)). Prof. Mehta notes that the reports themselves do not explain the decline, and that it continued after 2022-23 ([search summary of legacyias/EFA explainers](https://www.legacyias.com/why-is-there-a-drop-in-school-enrolments/)).
- **Demographics also play a part.** The projected 6-10 population fell from 11.49 crore (2024-25) to 11.40 crore (2025-26), and 6-13 from 18.54 crore to 18.45 crore. Primary GER fell from 90.9 to 89.4 ([EFA](https://educationforallinindia.com/udiseplus-2025-26-analysis-by-aruncmehta-educationforallinindia/)).
- **Gender:** boys' enrolment fell by 11.46 lakh in 2024-25 while girls' rose slightly. Girls are about 48% of enrolment ([Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/udise-plus-2024-2025-report-sharp-drop-sc-st-obc-enrolment-government-schools-59-lakh-education-ministry-data)).
- **Comparison surveys:**
  - **NSS CMS: Education 2025** (NSS 80th round, Apr-Jun 2025, household survey): government schools hold 55.9% of enrolment and private unaided recognised 31.9% ([News on AIR](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/government-schools-account-for-56-of-enrolments-says-education-survey-2025)).
  - **ASER 2024 (rural, ages 6-14):** government school share 66.8%, down from 72.9% in 2022 (65.6% in 2018), with about 33% in private schools ([ASER 2024 via PARI](https://ruralindiaonline.org/en/library/resource/annual-status-of-education-report-rural-2024)).

### Inferences
- **Classes 3-8 enrolment, 2025-26: about 12.4 crore.** Calculation: 3/5 × 10.19 crore (Classes 3-5, about 6.1 crore) + 6.32 crore (Classes 6-8). For 2024-25 the same method gives about 12.6 crore. Applying the 38% private unaided share for Classes 1-12 suggests about 4.5-4.8 crore private-school students in Classes 3-8. The private share may differ by grade.
- The primary cohort is shrinking about 2-2.5% a year while upper primary is roughly flat. The Classes 3-8 pool will likely drift down slowly rather than grow.
- The 2021-22 to 2022-23 fall in Classes 1-12 (-1.41 crore) is mostly a data break, not real dropout. Use 2022-23 onwards for any trend line.

### Gaps
- **Grade-wise enrolment** (Class 3, 4, 5 ... separately) was not obtained. It is in the UDISE+ booklet tables and on the dashboard, but those were not readable here. The Classes 3-8 figure is an estimate.
- No official breakdown of enrolment by management × level, so the private share of Classes 3-8 specifically is unknown.
- No official quantification of how much of the decline is duplicate removal and how much is real change.

## 3. Schools by school category; schools with Classes 3-8

### Takeaway
UDISE+ classifies schools by the **highest level offered**. In 2025-26:
- Primary: 7,22,498 (about 49%)
- Upper primary: 4,32,374 (about 29%)
- Secondary: 1,43,200
- Higher secondary: 1,68,610

All primary-category schools reach Class 5, and every upper-primary-category school teaches Classes 6-8. A large share of India's ~14.7 lakh schools therefore teach at least part of the Class 3-8 band. Only the composite schools (1-8, 1-10, 1-12) teach the full band; I found no exact count of these.

### Cited Findings
- **Schools by category, 2025-26:** Primary 7,22,498; Upper primary 4,32,374; Secondary 1,43,200; Higher secondary 1,68,610; total 14,66,682 ([EFA NEP distribution](https://educationforallinindia.com/analysing-distribution-of-udise-2025-26-schools-enrolment-and-teachers-towards-nep-2020-goals/)).
- **Teachers in schools of each category, 2025-26:** Primary 23,60,500; Upper primary 28,74,027; Secondary 16,29,223; Higher secondary 34,09,270 ([EFA NEP distribution](https://educationforallinindia.com/analysing-distribution-of-udise-2025-26-schools-enrolment-and-teachers-towards-nep-2020-goals/)).
- **Official Table 2.1, UDISE+ 2024-25 (p. 31), all-India shares:**

  | Category | Schools | Enrolment | Teachers |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Primary | 49.6% | 48.0% | 23.4% |
  | Upper primary | 29.5% | 25.8% | 28.6% |
  | Secondary | 9.7% | 15.1% | 15.8% |
  | Higher secondary | 11.2% | 11.2% | 32.2% |

  ([UDISE+ 2024-25 Table 2.1](https://educationforallinindia.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/State-wise-share-of-schools-enrolment-and-teachers-UDISEPlus-2024-25-Ministry-of-Education.pdf)).
- **State variation in primary-only share** (Table 2.1, 2024-25): West Bengal has 79.3% primary-category schools; Bihar 41.4% primary and 45.4% upper primary; Gujarat 25.1% primary and 50.3% upper primary ([Table 2.1](https://educationforallinindia.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/State-wise-share-of-schools-enrolment-and-teachers-UDISEPlus-2024-25-Ministry-of-Education.pdf)).
- **Underlying DCF codes:** 1 = Primary only (1-5); 2 = Primary with upper primary (1-8); 4 = Upper primary only (6-8); further codes for 1-10, 1-12, 6-10 and so on. Each school also carries a location code (1 = Rural, 2 = Urban) ([UDISE+ DCF 2024-25 guidelines](https://kolkatassm.in/guide24.pdf), via search summary).
- **Small schools:** 36.4% of schools in 2024-25 had 50 or fewer students ([EFA small schools](https://educationforallinindia.com/challenges-of-small-schools-in-india-analysis-of-udise-2024-25-enrolment-data/), via search summary).

### Inferences
- Schools teaching some of Classes 3-8: about 7.2 lakh primary-category schools (Classes 3-5) plus 4.3 lakh upper-primary-category schools (mostly 1-8 composites, which cover the full Class 3-8 band), plus secondary and higher-secondary schools that start at Class 1 or 6. In total, roughly 12-14 lakh schools (85-95%) teach part of the band. Full-band (3-8) schools are probably in the 4.5-7 lakh range. **This is my estimate, not an official count.**
- Primary-only schools are numerous but small: 49.6% of schools hold 48% of enrolment but only 23.4% of teachers. A programme priced per school will meet very small units in this segment.

### Gaps
- No exact national count by detailed category (1-5 only, 1-8, 6-8 only, 1-10, 1-12, 6-12 and so on) was retrieved. It is in the UDISE+ booklet (Table 3.1 "schools by level of school education"), which was not readable here.

## 4. Teachers: total, by management, PTR, single-teacher schools

### Takeaway
**2025-26:** 1,02,73,020 teachers.
- Government: 51.3 lakh (50.0%)
- Private unaided: 41.1 lakh (40.0%)

### Inferences
- **PTR by management, 2025-26** (my arithmetic, pre-primary-to-12 enrolment ÷ teachers): Government about 23, Aided about 32, Private unaided about 24, Others about 18. The 2024-25 aided result (32.7) matches the published figure.
- **Upper bound for a teacher-training component:** about 23.6 lakh teachers in primary-category schools plus 28.7 lakh in upper-primary-category schools, about 52 lakh in total. Not all of them teach Classes 3-8.

### Gaps
- No split of single-teacher schools by management. They are overwhelmingly government primary schools, but I found no source giving the figure.
- No count of teachers by class taught (Classes 3-8).

## 6. Rural vs urban split

### Takeaway
No official UDISE+ rural/urban split of schools or enrolment was retrieved. Household surveys show rural schooling is about two-thirds government, against about 30% government in urban areas.

### Cited Findings
- **NSS CMS: Education 2025:** 66.0% of rural students are in government schools, against 30.1% of urban students ([search summary of NSS CMS:E coverage](https://insights.dataful.in/articles/private-schooling-costs-nearly-9-times-more-than-government-education-nss-survey); [News on AIR](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/government-schools-account-for-56-of-enrolments-says-education-survey-2025)).
- **ASER 2024 (rural, ages 6-14):** 66.8% in government schools, about 33% private ([PARI/ASER 2024](https://ruralindiaonline.org/en/library/resource/annual-status-of-education-report-rural-2024)).
- UDISE+ records a Rural/Urban location code for every school ([DCF 2024-25](https://kolkatassm.in/guide24.pdf)), so the split exists in the official data.

### Inferences
- In rural India the private segment is about one-third of children aged 6-14. In urban India, private schools serve roughly two-thirds or more of students.

### Gaps
- No UDISE+ 2024-25 or 2025-26 counts of rural and urban schools, enrolment or teachers were found in accessible sources. The booklet tables and dashboard were not readable here. Older public figures (roughly 85% of schools rural) are not cited because no source was verified.

## 7. Private school segments: low-fee/budget private schools and fee distribution

### Takeaway
Most Indian private schools are low-fee.
- **CSF/Omidyar State of the Sector (2020), using NSS 75th round 2017-18:** 70% of private-school students pay under Rs 1,000 a month and 45% under Rs 500. The report counts about 4.5 lakh private schools serving about 12 crore students.
- **NSS CMS: Education 2025:** households spend on average about Rs 25,000 a year per student in non-government schools. That is about Rs 19,550 rural and about Rs 31,800 urban, against about Rs 2,900 in government schools.

### Cited Findings
- **CSF fact sheet:**
  - About 12 crore students attend private schools; there are about 4.5 lakh private schools.
  - About 9 crore children (75% of private-school students) are in private unaided schools.
  - "70% students pay less than ₹1000 per month in fees"; 45% pay less than ₹500 a month.
  - Aggregate household spend on private schools is about ₹1.75 lakh crore.
  - 73% of parents believe private schools give better learning outcomes.
  - ASER 2018: 60% of rural private-school Class 5 students cannot do simple division.

  ([CSF State of the Sector fact sheet](https://centralsquarefoundation.org/Fact-sheet-State-of-the-Sector-Report-on-Private-Schools-in-India.pdf); report launched 22 July 2020 with Omidyar Network India: [Omidyar](https://omidyarnetwork.in/insights/state-of-the-sector-report-private-schools-in-india)). The private-school counts include aided schools and come from roughly 2017-18 data.
- **NSS CMS: Education 2025** (NSS 80th round, Apr-Jun 2025):
  - Average annual spend per student: government ₹2,863, non-government ₹25,002 (about 9 times as much).
  - Rural: government ₹2,639, private ₹19,554. Urban: government ₹4,128, private ₹31,782.
  - 95.7% of non-government students pay course fees, against 26.7% of government students.
  - Course fees are the largest household education expense.

  ([Dataful](https://insights.dataful.in/articles/private-schooling-costs-nearly-9-times-more-than-government-education-nss-survey); [News on AIR](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/government-schools-account-for-56-of-enrolments-says-education-survey-2025); [PIB release copy, 26 Aug 2025](https://educationforallinindia.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/PIB-Press-Release-26-08-2025-NSSO-Comrephensive-Modular-Survey-on-Education.pdf)). Education World cites an analysis suggesting some respondents counted coaching payments as tuition fees. CMS:E results are not directly comparable with the NSS 75th round (2017-18) ([search summary](https://educationworld.in/?p=157988)).

### Inferences
- Average private spend of about ₹19.5k a year in rural areas, including all costs, means a typical rural private school charges well under ₹1,500 a month in tuition. This is consistent with the CSF/NSS-75 picture that most private-school students are in budget schools. A Classes 3-8 CT/AI programme priced per student for private schools would need to sit within a small fraction of that spend: likely a few hundred rupees a year per student for the budget segment.
- The private unaided segment in 2025-26 is about 3.4 lakh schools, about 8.8 crore students in Classes 1-12 (9.9 crore including pre-primary) and about 41 lakh teachers (UDISE+). This updates CSF's older "4.5 lakh / 12 crore", which included aided schools.

### Gaps
- No post-2018 fee-band distribution (share of students under ₹500 / ₹1,000 / ₹2,500 a month) was found. CMS:E 2025 publishes averages; its detailed tables may allow fee bands but were not accessed.
- No current count of low-fee private schools by state. NISA/CSF estimates were not found in this pass.
