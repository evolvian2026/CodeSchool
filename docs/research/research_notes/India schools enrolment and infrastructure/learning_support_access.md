# Learning support channels and home digital access for Indian schools and students (focus Classes 3-8)

Reference date of compilation: 6 November 2026. Each figure carries its own reference date. Some figures come from news or secondary summaries rather than the primary report; these are flagged.

## DIKSHA: content, languages, energised textbooks, usage, status and offline availability

### Takeaway
DIKSHA is very large in content (7,687 QR-coded energised textbooks, 3.67 lakh e-content items, 135 languages) and in cumulative use (575 crore learning sessions). Its day-to-day reach is modest, though: about 3 lakh daily active users against 2.25 crore registered users (27 June 2026). It is officially offline-capable through downloads and preloading onto smart-class boards. In practice, its biggest role at scale is as a teacher-training channel (18.77 crore course enrolments) and a QR link from printed textbooks, more than a daily student learning tool.

### Cited Findings
- DIKSHA launched in 2017. NCERT runs it with CIET, as a key component of PM e-Vidya, and the government calls it "One Nation, One Digital Platform" for school education. It covers K-12, from FLN to senior secondary, and has been adopted by boards in almost all states and UTs — [PIB Research factsheet, "DIKSHA: Powering India's Digital Learning Ecosystem", 28 June 2026](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2026/jun/doc2026628904201.pdf)
- Key statistics as on 27 June 2026: registered users 2.25 crore; active users "around 3 lakh daily"; learning sessions 575.25 crore; learning minutes 6,691.82 crore; energised textbooks 7,687; electronic contents 3.67 lakh. The course metrics table lists 18.77 crore enrolments, 14.82 crore completions, 12.79 crore certificates issued and 347 courses (the PDF layout is garbled, so this mapping of the four figures to labels is inferred from their order) — [PIB Research factsheet, 28 June 2026](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2026/jun/doc2026628904201.pdf)
- Languages: the platform supports 135 languages (128 Indian, 7 foreign). NCERT translates textbooks for Classes 1, 2, 3 and 6 into 22 scheduled languages, also available on the NCERT portal — [PIB Research factsheet, 28 June 2026](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2026/jun/doc2026628904201.pdf)
- Offline: "DIKSHA platform supports both online and offline accessibility. Learners can download content in advance". States and UTs preload content on smart-class boards for offline access — [PIB Research factsheet, 28 June 2026](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2026/jun/doc2026628904201.pdf)
- Features: 2D/3D animations, AR, simulations, virtual labs (more than 614 virtual labs), sign-language videos, QR-coded energised textbooks linking NCERT books to videos and teacher guides, question banks, adaptive assessments, a chatbot, "Phygital", and Sunbird Registry and Credential. Accessibility features include the DAISY format, text-to-speech and Indian Sign Language videos — [PIB Research factsheet, 28 June 2026](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2026/jun/doc2026628904201.pdf)
- Architecture is federated: each participant (state, board) uploads and manages its own content in regional languages, and CIET-NCERT periodically validates it — [PIB Research factsheet, 28 June 2026](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2026/jun/doc2026628904201.pdf)
- Earlier figures for trend: about 6,600 energised textbooks (April 2025, secondary) and more than 5,620 million (562 crore) learning sessions as of May 2025 (academic article) — [Wikipedia: DIKSHA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_Infrastructure_for_Knowledge_Sharing); [Pachori, British Journal of Educational Technology, 2026](https://bera-journals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/bjet.70042)
- News coverage of the June 2026 release: "crosses 575 crore learning sessions with 2.25 crore users" — [ANI, 29 June 2026](https://aninews.in/news/business/dikshas-digital-learning-ecosystem-crosses-575-crore-learning-sessions-with-225-crore-users20260629160812/)

### Inferences
- Sessions grew only about 2% (562 crore to 575 crore) between May 2025 and June 2026, which suggests post-COVID use has plateaued. About 3 lakh daily active users is small next to roughly 25 crore school students.
- DIKSHA's QR-linked textbooks and offline/preload model make it a natural free distribution and supplementary layer for a CT&AI programme (for example, QR codes in printed workbooks that link to videos). A programme should not, however, assume students use DIKSHA at home every day.

### Gaps
- No official monthly active user figure was found; only "around 3 lakh daily" active users.
- No authoritative source was found on "DIKSHA 2.0" or a re-platforming announcement. The June 2026 factsheet mentions Sunbird Registry and Credential components but no version upgrade.
- The state-wise registered-user table in the factsheet was garbled in text extraction and is not reproduced.

## PM eVidya: components, 200 DTH channels, SWAYAM Prabha, radio and podcasts, content for disabled learners

### Takeaway
PM eVidya (launched 2020) bundles DIKSHA, 200 "one class, one channel" DTH TV channels (expanded from 12 in Budget 2022-23), around 400 radio stations including 257 community radio stations, iRadio/JioSaavn podcasts, and accessible content (ISL channel, DAISY). Official data counts channels, stations and programmes. No measured viewership or listenership was found.

### Cited Findings
- PM eVidya has six components, including DIKSHA, SWAYAM, the SWAYAM Prabha DTH channels, radio and podcasts, and content for children with disabilities — [Smile Foundation explainer](https://www.smilefoundationindia.org/blog/what-is-pm-evidya-meaning-and-role-in-india/); [SWAYAM Prabha: PM eVidya](https://www.swayamprabha.gov.in/about/pmevidya)
- The TV component began with 12 channels and was expanded to 200 PM eVidya channels on a "One Class-One Channel" basis for Classes 1-12. The expansion was announced in the Budget of 1 February 2022 — [SWAYAM Prabha: PM eVidya](https://www.swayamprabha.gov.in/about/pmevidya); [NCERT-CIET SOP for 200 DTH TV channels](https://ciet.ncert.gov.in/storage/app/public/files/17/DTH/SOP_for_200_DTH_TV_Channels_for_School_Education.pdf)
- The channels are carried on DD Free Dish; Prasar Bharati publishes the channel list (as on 1 January 2026) — [Prasar Bharati DD Free Dish channel list, 1 Jan 2026](https://prasarbharati.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/List-of-DD-Free-Dish-channels-as-on-01-01-2026.pdf)
- Lok Sabha reply (February 2025): QR-code-embedded educational audio/video programmes are disseminated "through 200 PM eVidya DTH TV Channels and 400 radio stations and on iRadio in a simultaneous manner" — [Lok Sabha unstarred question AU188](https://eparlib.sansad.in/bitstream/123456789/2989003/1/AU188_9ULJBa.pdf)
- The radio breakdown in CIET material is 3 GyanVani FM, 257 community radio and 132 All India Radio stations plus iRadio (393 in total), with podcasts on iRadio and JioSaavn. More than 4,476 curriculum-based radio programmes (Classes 1-12) and more than 5,737 live programmes on iRadio/JioSaavn are reported (undated CIET reports) — [CIET PM eVidya mobile app brochure](https://ciet.ncert.gov.in/storage/app/public/files/19/Brochure/pmevidyamobile_app.pdf); [CIET PM eVidya reports](https://ciet.ncert.gov.in/storage/app/public/files/19/Reportpdf/PmevidyaReports.pdf)
- CIET has published a critical review of the use of the 200 DTH channels — [CIET, "Utilization of 200 PM eVidya DTH TV Channels: A Critical Review"](https://ciet.ncert.gov.in/storage/app/public/files/19/Transmission_Schedule/Utilization_of_200_PM_eVidya_DTH_TV_Channels-A_Critical_Review.pdf)
- PM eVidya DTH channel 31 for Indian Sign Language was launched in December 2024 — [Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/dharmendra-pradhan-launches-pmevidya-dth-channel-for-indian-sign-language-promotes-inclusivity-in-education/amp)
- Spending: over Rs 14.75 crore was spent on PM eVIDYA in FY 2020-21 (Parliament reply, as reported) — [Udayavani](https://udayavani.com/english-news/over-rs-14-75-crore-spent-on-pm-evidya-initiative-in-fy-2020-21-govt-in-parliament)
- SWAYAM Prabha channel counts differ across secondary sources: 34, 40 or 48 higher-education/DTH channels. No authoritative current count was found — [Scribd: SWAYAM Prabha overview](https://www.scribd.com/document/727755260/Sawayam-Prabha); [NCERT-CIET SOP](https://ciet.ncert.gov.in/storage/app/public/files/17/DTH/SOP_for_200_DTH_TV_Channels_for_School_Education.pdf)

### Inferences
- TV (DD Free Dish) and community radio are the government's main no-internet channels. A CT&AI programme could use them for broadcast "unplugged" lessons, but there is no evidence of how many Class 3-8 students actually watch.
- Because there is a dedicated channel per class, a CT&AI segment could in principle sit within each class channel (Classes 3-8). This would need NCERT/CIET partnership.

### Gaps
- No viewership (BARC or other) or listenership data was found for the PM eVidya DTH channels or radio.
- No current authoritative SWAYAM Prabha channel count was found (figures conflict).

## NDEAR, Vidya Samiksha Kendras, e-Jaadui Pitara, Bharatiya Bhasha, AI initiatives and CBSE/NCERT CT&AI resources

### Takeaway
Policy momentum for CT&AI in Classes 3-8 is now concrete. CBSE and the Ministry of Education made Computational Thinking and AI part of the curriculum for Classes 3-8 from the 2026-27 session, with NCERT, KVS and NVS involved and student and teacher resource books published. Budget 2025-26 added a Rs 500 crore AI Centre of Excellence for education, 50,000 Atal Tinkering Labs, BharatNet broadband for all government secondary schools, and the Bharatiya Bhasha Pustak (digital Indian-language books) scheme. Budget 2026-27 added AVGC content creator labs in 15,000 secondary schools.

### Cited Findings
- CBSE and the Ministry of Education launched a Computational Thinking and AI curriculum for Classes 3 to 8 from the 2026-27 academic session (reported 1 April 2026) — [Business Standard, 1 April 2026](https://www.business-standard.com/education/news/cbse-launches-ai-computational-thinking-curriculum-classes-3-8-126040101366_1.html)
- Earlier announcement: students will learn AI and computational thinking from Class 3 from 2026-27. The Department of School Education and Literacy will develop the framework with NCERT, CBSE, KVS and NVS — [Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/students-learn-ai-computational-thinking-from-class-3-starting-2026-27-cbse-ncert-frame-curriculum)
- Third-party guides cite CBSE Circular Acad-15/2026 (1 April 2026), making CT&AI compulsory for Classes III-VIII from 2026-27, and a follow-up Circular Acad-18/2026 that put student and teacher resource books on the CBSE academic website. Ethics (bias, fairness, digital citizenship) is reportedly part of every class. Some guides say CT&AI is integrated into the existing curriculum rather than a standalone subject. I could not see the circular text, so treat this as unverified — [Target Publications blog](https://targetpublications.org/blog/cbse-launches-framework-for-computational-thinking-and-artificial-intelligence); [Codju guide](https://codju.com/blog/cbse-computational-thinking-ai-curriculum-2026-27/); [Thinking Juggernaut](https://thinkingjuggernaut.in/blog/cbse-computational-thinking-ai-curriculum-classes-2026-27)
- Union Budget 2025-26 (1 February 2025):

  - 50,000 Atal Tinkering Labs in government schools over five years;
  - broadband connectivity to all government secondary schools under BharatNet in the next three years;
  - the Bharatiya Bhasha Pustak Scheme, providing digital-form Indian-language books for school and higher education;
  - a total education budget of about Rs 1,28,650 crore, up 6.22%.

- Union Budget 2026-27 (1 February 2026): total education allocation Rs 1,39,289.48 crore, of which school education is Rs 83,562.26 crore and higher education Rs 55,727.22 crore — [EducationWorld, 2 February 2026](https://educationworld.in/whats-in-union-budget-2026-27-for-education/)
- Union Budget 2026-27 also proposed support for IICT Mumbai to set up AVGC content creator labs in 15,000 secondary schools and 500 colleges. One outlet reports Rs 250 crore for AVGC talent development (unverified) — [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/economy/budgets-orange-economy-push-why-govt-is-backing-animation-gaming-vfx-comics/2842625/); [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/education/union-budget-2026-fm-sitharaman-announces-content-creator-labs-in-15000-schools-500-colleges-supported-by-iict-mumbai-details)

### Inferences
- The CBSE CT&AI mandate creates immediate demand from about 30,000 CBSE-affiliated schools (affiliation count not verified here) for Class 3-8 materials and teacher training from 2026-27. Many of these schools have limited devices, so resources that work unplugged or offline will matter.
- The Budget infrastructure items (ATLs, BharatNet for secondary schools, content creator labs) all target government secondary schools rather than primary or upper-primary. Class 3-8 classrooms are therefore least likely to gain devices or connectivity from these schemes in the near term.

### Gaps

- No operational status was found for the Rs 500 crore AI CoE for education (host institution, launch date).

- No implementation status was found for the Bharatiya Bhasha Pustak scheme or BharatNet school broadband.
- The National Digital Library of India's school-level usage was not researched (out of budget).

## Offline support: free textbooks, NCERT print volumes and prices, workbooks and materials

### Takeaway
Printed textbooks remain the universal channel. Samagra Shiksha funds free textbooks for Classes 1-8 in government and aided schools (states distribute them, often on the first day of the session). NCERT prices Class 1-8 books at Rs 65 per copy and has been scaling its print run from about 4-5 crore to a targeted 15 crore books a year.

### Cited Findings
- NCERT textbooks for Classes 1-8 are priced at Rs 65 per copy. NCERT announced a 20% price cut for Classes 9-12, effective 2026-27, attributed to paper-procurement efficiencies and modern printers — [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/education/ncert-announces-20-price-cut-on-class-9-12-textbooks-starting-from-2026-27); [Business Standard, 17 December 2024](https://www.business-standard.com/education/news/ncert-textbooks-price-cut-2025-printing-capacity-increase-124121700746_1.html)

## Home access: devices and internet (ASER 2024, NSS/CAMS, NFHS-5, TRAI)

### Takeaway
Household smartphone access is now near-universal: 85.5% of households own a smartphone and 86.3% have internet in the premises (CAMS Telecom, January-March 2025). Rural households about 90% of rural 14-16-year-olds report a smartphone at home (ASER 2024). Personal ownership, computers and fixed broadband remain scarce, however: fixed or Wi-Fi internet reaches 9.1% of rural households, and only about 31% of rural 14-16s own a phone. Gender gaps persist. Younger children in Classes 3-8 will mostly depend on a shared parental phone.

### Cited Findings
- **CAMS Telecom 2025 (NSS 80th round, January-March 2025, 34,950 households):**
  - 85.5% of households have at least one smartphone;
  - 86.3% have internet access in the premises (rural 83.3%, urban 91.6%);
  - fixed or Wi-Fi connectivity: rural 9.1%, urban 24%;
  - among those aged 15-29, mobile phone use is 96.8% rural and 97.6% urban;
  - among people aged 15+, 70% used the internet in the past three months (men 76.7%, women 63.1%);
  - mobile data was the access mode for 98.1% of rural and 88.6% of urban internet users.

  Sources: [PIB/MoSPI document, May 2025](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/may/doc2025529560001.pdf); [IANS](https://ianslive.in/855-pc-of-indian-households-now-own-at-least-1-smartphone-centre--20250529163413); [PARI library: CMS Telecom 2025](https://ruralindiaonline.org/en/library/resource/comprehensive-modular-survey-telecom-2025)
- **ASER 2024 digital module** (rural, 605 districts, 14-16-year-olds; the module was administered to 114,553 youth; report released January 2025):
  - nearly 90% have a smartphone at home;
  - 82.2% can use a smartphone (boys 85.5%, girls 79.4%);
  - about 31% own one, up from 19% in 2022;
  - ownership by gender: boys 36.2%, girls 26.9%;
  - on the survey day, 70.2% of boys and 62.2% of girls could get a smartphone for the tasks;
  - 57% used a phone for education in the past week, and 76% for social media.

  These figures come from news and secondary summaries, not the ASER report PDF — [Business Standard, 29 January 2025](https://www.business-standard.com/education/news/aser-2024-india-education-report-learning-covid-recovery-digital-literacy-125012900451_1.html); [Deccan Chronicle](https://www.deccanchronicle.com/news/smartphone-access-education-recovery-in-aser-2024-1857277); [National Herald](https://www.nationalheraldindia.com/national/only-57-percent-of-rural-indian-children-use-phones-to-study-aser-report); [Ideas for India](https://www.ideasforindia.in/topics/human-development/youth-s-digital-readiness-in-rural-india)
- **CAMS 2022-23 (NSS 79th round):** among people aged 15-24, 82.1% rural and 91.8% urban could use the internet — [India Education Diary](https://indiaeducationdiary.in/?p=560692); [LatestLY/PTI](https://www.latestly.com/agency-news/latest-news-over-82-pc-of-rural-youth-can-use-internet-says-govt-survey-6331053.html)
- **NSS 78th round (Multiple Indicator Survey, 2020-21):** computer literacy among people aged 15+ was 24.7%. Among those aged 15-29, about 24% rural and 56% urban could operate a computer (secondary summary) — [Unacademy](https://unacademy.com/content/upsc/daily-news-analysis-computer-literacy-in-india-5-july/); [KSG India editorial, 5 July 2024](https://ksgindia.com/study-material/today-s-editorial/todays-editorial-05-july-2024.html)
- **NFHS-5 (2019-21), Phase 1 states:** "ever used the internet" was 33.94% for rural women vs 55.6% for rural men, and 56.81% for urban women vs 73.76% for urban men. In 10 of 17 Phase 1 states, fewer than 30% of rural women had ever used the internet — [Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/science-technology/nfhs-5-how-did-indian-states-fare-in-the-internet-usage-question--74843); [Factly](https://factly.in/less-than-30-of-rural-women-have-ever-used-the-internet-in-10-states-as-per-nfhs-5)
- **TRAI (July 2026):**
  - total telephone subscribers 1,354.28 million;
  - tele-density 94.68% overall, 154.12% urban, 61.05% rural;
  - broadband subscribers 1,005.72 million (excluding M2M);
  - rural share of subscribers about 41%;
  - 5G fixed wireless access subscribers 12.94 million (June 2026).

  Sources: [Free Press Journal on TRAI July 2026 data](https://www.freepressjournal.in/tech/indias-telecom-subscriber-base-rises-to-1354-billion-in-july-trai); [TRAI PR No. 135 of 2026](https://trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2026-10/PR_No135of2026.pdf); [Fonearena](https://www.fonearena.com/blog/?p=488465)
- Rural wireline tele-density was about 0.57% (March 2026 quarter) — [TRAI PR No. 127 of 2026](https://trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2026-10/PR_No127of2026.pdf)

### Inferences
- For Classes 3-8 (ages about 8-14), home digital learning will almost always mean a shared parent's smartphone on mobile data, available intermittently, with girls less likely to get it. Home computers and fixed broadband are rare in rural areas. A home-online component should be optional, mobile-first, low-bandwidth and offline-downloadable, not the core.
- Even ASER's older cohort (14-16) has a gap of about 8 points between boys and girls in device access on the day and about 9 points in ownership, so an online-dependent design would likely widen gender inequity.

### Gaps
- No direct national data was found on device access specifically for children aged 8-13 (Classes 3-8). ASER's digital module covers ages 14-16 only.
- The NSS 78th round household computer/internet ownership figures were not retrieved from the primary MoSPI report. A "24% of households with internet" figure appears in secondary sources but may come from the earlier 75th round (2017-18); it is unverified.
- The ASER 2024 figures were not checked against the ASER report PDF.
- No official CAMS figure for household computer ownership in 2025 was found.

## Private edtech after the 2022-24 correction: scale and school-integrated vs direct-to-consumer

### Takeaway
After the downturn, the strongest K-12-relevant private players are school-integrated (B2B2C) platforms selling to affordable private schools: LEAD Group at about 8,500 schools (January 2026) targeting about 10,000 in 2026, and Uolo, raising US$7 million in 2025 for school partnerships. In direct-to-consumer, PhysicsWallah (test prep, mostly Class 11-12 and above) listed publicly in November 2025, while BYJU's is in insolvency.

### Cited Findings
- LEAD Group works with about 8,500 schools, onboards about 1,000 new schools a year, and targets more than 1,200 additions in calendar 2026 to reach nearly 10,000. Ninety per cent of partner schools are in non-metro areas and 50% in Tier-III/IV cities (January 2026) — [Business Standard, 7 January 2026](https://www.business-standard.com/companies/start-ups/ed-tech-firm-lead-group-targets-partnering-with-1-200-schools-in-2026-126010700826_1.html)
- LEAD acquired Pearson India's K-12 business in 2023. Its CEO said in February 2025 that LEAD would be EBITDA break-even for FY25. Entrackr (September 2025) headlined that LEAD cut losses by 70% with flat revenue — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/companies/start-ups/ed-tech-firm-lead-group-targets-partnering-with-1-200-schools-in-2026-126010700826_1.html); [YourStory, February 2025](https://yourstory.com/2025/02/edtech-lead-sumeet-mehta-advocates-public-private-partnerships-india-schools)
- Earlier LEAD profile: more than 2,000 affordable private schools — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/startups/school-edtech-platform-lead-is-driving-learning-outcomes-of-schools/)
- Uolo raised US$7 million (2025, led by Five Sigma) to expand school partnerships and GenAI learning companions. It had grown from 150 to 5,600 schools by June 2022 and targeted 25,000 schools. A later undated profile claims more than 500 schools and 2 million students; these figures conflict — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/edtech-startup-uolo-bags-7-mn-to-expand-school-partnerships); [YourStory, June 2022](https://yourstory.com/2022/06/edtech-startup-uolo-schools-students-growth-online-offline/amp)
- PhysicsWallah:
  - FY25 revenue Rs 2,886.6 crore, up 48.7% from Rs 1,940.7 crore in FY24;
  - FY25 loss Rs 243.26 crore, against Rs 1,131.13 crore in FY24;

## Teacher digital readiness: NISHTHA, ICT training and teacher devices

### Takeaway
Teacher training at scale runs through DIKSHA: 18.77 crore course enrolments and 12.79 crore certificates by June 2026. More than 63 lakh teachers were trained under NISHTHA between 2019 and 2022. Dedicated ICT or cyber-safety training is much smaller: about 3.83 lakh teachers trained in cyber safety since October 2021. No national data on teacher device ownership was found.

### Cited Findings
- More than 63.17 lakh teachers were trained under various NCERT NISHTHA programmes in 2019-22 (Lok Sabha, December 2024) — [News On AIR, 16 December 2024](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/over-63-lakh-teachers-trained-under-different-nishtha-programs-from-2019-22)
- More than 3.83 lakh teachers received online training in cyber safety and security since October 2021 — [News On AIR, 16 December 2024](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/over-63-lakh-teachers-trained-under-different-nishtha-programs-from-2019-22)
- NISHTHA certificates by programme (Lok Sabha annexure, March 2025, NCERT data): Elementary about 22.6 lakh, Secondary about 7.6 lakh, FLN about 13.6 lakh, ECCE about 1.55 lakh (figures as summarised by search; annexure not opened in full) — [Ministry of Education Parliament annexure 2582](https://education.gov.in/sites/upload_files/mhrd/files/parliament_annexure_en/2582-en.pdf); [annexure 1642](https://education.gov.in/sites/upload_files/mhrd/files/parliament_annexure_en/1642-en.pdf)
- NISHTHA moved online through DIKSHA after COVID disrupted face-to-face training — [Ministry of Education NISHTHA note](https://dsel.education.gov.in/sites/default/files/update/PR_NISHTHA_0.pdf)
- DIKSHA course metrics (27 June 2026): 18.77 crore enrolments, 14.82 crore completions, 12.79 crore certificates, 347 courses. The factsheet says it supports teacher professional development via NISHTHA and state TPD modules — [PIB Research factsheet, 28 June 2026](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2026/jun/doc2026628904201.pdf)

### Inferences
- Teachers can be reached online at scale through DIKSHA courses. A CT&AI programme could deliver certified teacher training via DIKSHA (online, self-paced) while keeping classroom delivery offline or unplugged, which suits a hybrid model.
- The small size of ICT-specific training relative to general NISHTHA suggests many Class 3-8 teachers will need foundational digital and CT orientation before teaching AI concepts.

### Gaps
- No national data on teachers' personal device or smartphone ownership or home internet was found.
- No figure was found for teachers trained specifically for the CBSE CT&AI curriculum (2026-27).
- No NISHTHA ICT-module enrolment figures were found.
