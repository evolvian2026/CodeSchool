# Digital and physical teaching infrastructure in Indian schools (computers, internet, smart classrooms, projectors, ICT labs, electricity)

Reference point: the latest official census is **UDISE+ 2025-26**. The Ministry of Education released it on about 7 July 2026, and a PIB backgrounder followed on 17 Aug 2026. It covers about 14.67 lakh schools. Figures from UDISE+ 2024-25 (released Aug 2025) and earlier are given for trend. Unless noted, all percentages are **share of schools** (school-weighted), not share of students.

Definitions matter. UDISE+ reports several different digital indicators, and secondary sources often mix them up:
- "computer facility": any computer, whether or not it works
- "computer functional for pedagogical/teaching purpose": a working computer used for teaching
- "functional desktop/PC", "functional laptop", "functional tablet": each device type counted separately
- "smart classroom"
- "functional ICT lab": asked only of government and aided schools with upper primary or higher sections

## 1. UDISE+ headline availability (electricity, computers, internet, smart classrooms, projectors, digital libraries, ICT labs), latest year and trend

### Takeaway
In 2025-26, 95.0% of Indian schools had electricity (93.5% functional). 69.9% reported a computer, but only 63.1% had one that works for teaching. 67.4% had internet, 33.9% a smart classroom and 22.1% a functional projector. Only 36.5% had a functional desktop. Coverage has risen steeply since 2019-20, when about 39% had computers and about 22% had internet. Still, about 4.4 lakh schools have no computer and about 4.8 lakh have no internet.

### Inferences
- **Upper bound for device-based lessons.** About 63% of schools have at least one computer usable for teaching. Only about 36.5% have a working desktop and about 21% a working laptop. A "teacher-led demo on one device or projector" model can therefore reach roughly 60%+ of schools. A "students on devices" lab model can reach far fewer: the functional ICT lab rate in government upper-primary-plus schools is about 23%.
- **Marginal figures overstate readiness.** IJERT bounds the share of schools with both a teaching-usable device and internet at 30.5%-63.1% nationally (24.0% lower bound once electricity is added). Its model estimate is about 54%. These are estimates, not published figures.
- **The gap is shrinking but slowly.** Growth is about 4-7 percentage points a year on computers and internet. At that rate, roughly a quarter to a third of schools will still lack a working teaching computer in 2027-28.

### Gaps
- No figure found on the share of schools with an ICT lab of any kind across all managements (Table 9.9 covers only government and aided schools).
- No reliable 2020-21 or 2021-22 national figures were verified.
- Tablets: 31% of schools report a functional tablet, but tablet counts per school were not found.
- The full UDISE+ 2025-26 booklet ([PDF](https://dashboard.udiseplus.gov.in/report2026/static/media/UDISE+2025_26_Booklet_existing.edd7cd16d934a10377ba.pdf)) and the 2024-25 booklet were too large to fetch. Figures here come from analysts who cite the booklet tables. The official PIB 2024-25 release page returned 403.
- Functional electricity is published as 95.0%, but IJERT gives 93.52%. IJERT notes this conflict in its own reconciliation section.

## 2. Breakdown by management (government, aided, private unaided, others) and by level

### Takeaway
Government schools are 68.5% of all schools and teach 48.1% of students, and they lag private and aided schools on every device measure. The gap is largest for actual working hardware:
- functional desktop: government 25.5% vs private unaided 63.2%
- laptop: 11.7% vs 44.8%

The gap is smaller on internet (63.1% vs 79.2%) and smart classrooms (32.2% vs 39.9%). Government schools lead only on tablets (39.3% vs 14.4%), reflecting state tablet schemes.

### Inferences
- For a Classes 3-8 programme, government schools are the binding constraint. About 41% have no computer usable for teaching. Only about a quarter have a working desktop, and the ICT-lab rate is about 23% (Class 6+ only). Primary-only government schools are likely worse, but this is not separately reported.
- Many government "computers" are tablets (39.3%), not desktops or laptops. Content must work on tablets and on a single teacher device or projector.
- Even in private unaided schools, about a quarter lack a teaching computer and roughly half lack a confirmed device-plus-internet combination. Budget private schools likely make up most of that gap (see section 6).

### Gaps
- No electricity-by-management figures for 2025-26 were found.
- No published breakdown by school category (primary only, upper primary, composite), apart from ICT labs (upper primary and above).
- Aided and "others" figures are incomplete for desktop, laptop and tablet.

## 3. Device counts per school, schools below N devices, student-to-computer ratio

### Takeaway
UDISE+ publishes **shares of schools with at least one** functional device of each type, not unit counts. No authoritative national figure was found for average computers per school or student-to-computer ratio.

### Cited Findings
- IJERT states that UDISE+ 2025-26 gives only the share of schools with a functional desktop or laptop, not unit counts, so student-to-computer ratios cannot be derived. It notes that a facility flag records a "yes" answer about a category of equipment, not a working machine or a child using one — [IJERT](https://www.ijert.org/coverage-is-not-capability-what-udise-2025-26-can-and-cannot-establish-about-digital-teaching-capacity-in-india-s-four-management-school-system-ijertv15is090379)
- Average school sizes for context: government 118 students per school, aided 311, private unaided 289 — [IJERT](https://www.ijert.org/coverage-is-not-capability-what-udise-2025-26-can-and-cannot-establish-about-digital-teaching-capacity-in-india-s-four-management-school-system-ijertv15is090379)
- Samagra Shiksha unit specification: each school is eligible for Rs 2.40 lakh non-recurring for **two** smart classrooms, plus Rs 38,000 recurring — [DD News](https://ddnews.gov.in/en/govt-approves-1-46-lakh-smart-classrooms-across-india-under-samagra-shiksha/); [Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/centre-smart-classrooms-samagra-shiksha-udise-plus-nep-2020-govt-lok-sabha-approves-over-176-lakh-up-mp-tops-list-35533-states-uts)
- Kerala, the one state with published device stocks:
  - 1,19,055 laptops and 69,944 multimedia projectors across about 16,000 public schools (2018-19 programme)
  - 62,677 laptops and 42,866 projectors in high schools
  - 79,571 devices in 11,226 primary and upper primary schools under the High-Tech Lab project
  - KITE later introduced an AMC (annual maintenance contract) for about 1.85 lakh hi-tech devices

  — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/asia/india/kerala-becomes-first-indian-state-to-go-digital-in-public-education-1.74517619); [The News Minute](https://www.thenewsminute.com/article/more-40000-kerala-classrooms-turn-hi-tech-84799); [News Arena India](https://newsarenaindia.com/technology/kite-brings-amc-for-185k-hi-tech-devices-in-kerala-schools/21601)

### Inferences
- Even where a government school has an ICT lab, the standard Samagra Shiksha lab is modest (the unit size was not verified here). A typical government school of about 118 students with one lab would need rotation for Classes 3-8. Plan for shared or rotating device time, not 1:1.
- Kerala's ratios work out to about 7 laptops and 4 projectors per public school (2018-19 stock). That makes Kerala an outlier, with device-based lessons broadly feasible.

### Gaps
- No national or state average of functional computers per school.
- No distribution of schools by device count (for example, fewer than 5 or fewer than 10 devices).
- No student-to-computer ratio. The UDISE+ school-level dashboards may hold unit counts, but they were not accessible.

### Inferences
- **Online-ready states** (device-based lessons broadly feasible in government schools): Delhi, Chandigarh, Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Punjab, Goa (devices partly), Gujarat, Haryana, Lakshadweep.
- **Offline-first states**, where a paper-based track is essential in most government schools: Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Madhya Pradesh, Assam, Meghalaya and West Bengal, where government desktop rates are 6-18%.
- UP, Bihar, MP, West Bengal and Assam together hold a very large share of India's schools, so nationally most government-school children in Classes 3-8 will need an offline mode.
- Bihar's high internet but low devices suggests phone- or tablet-based connectivity. A teacher-phone or projector delivery mode may work there.

## 5. Government schemes: Samagra Shiksha ICT labs and smart classrooms, PM SHRI, Atal Tinkering Labs, BharatNet, state programmes, audits

### Takeaway
Under Samagra Shiksha, sanctions far outrun completions. Parliament was told of 73,308 ICT labs and 1,36,382 smart classrooms approved for 2021-22 to 2025-26, with 37,511 labs (about 51%) and 82,856 classrooms (about 61%) completed. ## 6. Quality caveats (bandwidth, non-functional equipment, teacher ICT training) and private-school or vendor penetration

### Takeaway
UDISE+ records self-reported presence, not usability, so "has internet" or "has computer" overstates teaching capacity. About 1 lakh schools have a computer that does not work for teaching. ICT labs are often non-functional (Punjab 45.6% reported vs 9.8% working). No bandwidth data exists. No reliable survey of smart-board penetration in budget vs premium private schools, and no verified vendor reach figures, were found. Rural children's home phone access is high: about 89% of 14-16-year-olds have a smartphone at home (ASER 2024).

### Cited Findings
- Coverage is not capability. The UDISE+ flag is a school's self-reported "yes", validated by officers, not a hardware measurement. Across states, none of the infrastructure indicators is significantly associated with PGI 2.0 learning outcomes (r about 0.19-0.24) — [IJERT](https://www.ijert.org/coverage-is-not-capability-what-udise-2025-26-can-and-cannot-establish-about-digital-teaching-capacity-in-india-s-four-management-school-system-ijertv15is090379)
- Present-but-not-working gaps: 1,01,011 schools have a computer not functional for teaching (2025-26); Chhattisgarh 61.4% report vs 38.6% working; Punjab ICT labs 45.6% reported vs 9.8% working; 21,605 schools have an electricity connection that does not function — [Education for All in India](https://educationforallinindia.com/67-4-of-indian-schools-now-online-yet-over-73000-still-without-electricity/); [Elets](https://digitallearning.eletsonline.com/2026/10/udise-2025-26-one-in-three-schools-no-working-computer-ai-curriculum/)
- Of the 59.4% of government schools with a teaching-usable computer, at most 25.5% have a desktop and 11.7% a laptop. The rest are other or unidentified devices, mainly tablets — [IJERT](https://www.ijert.org/coverage-is-not-capability-what-udise-2025-26-can-and-cannot-establish-about-digital-teaching-capacity-in-india-s-four-management-school-system-ijertv15is090379)
- 27.3% of schools (3,99,984) use mobile phones for teaching — [Education for All in India](https://educationforallinindia.com/the-digital-divide-in-indian-schools-what-udise-2025-26-reveals-about-ict-infrastructure/)
- ASER 2024 (rural, ages 14-16; household, not school):

  - 82.2% know how to use one
  - about 65.9% could bring one for the survey tasks
  - ownership: boys 36.2%, girls 26.9%
  - about 57% use it for education, about 76% for social media
  - Bihar, Jharkhand and Madhya Pradesh lag

### Inferences
- About 60% of private unaided schools lack a smart classroom, and about 26% lack a teaching computer. Combined with the scale of budget private schools, this suggests low-fee private schools are far from uniformly "online-ready". The premium vs budget split cannot be quantified from sources found.
- For lesson design, "internet present" should be read as "some connection, possibly a teacher's phone". Device-based lessons should work offline or with low bandwidth, with paper fallback.
- High home smartphone availability but low ownership suggests take-home digital activities would reach only some children, especially girls.

### Gaps
- No national school bandwidth or speed data. UDISE+ does not record bandwidth.
- **NISHTHA ICT or teacher digital-training completion numbers were not found** in this search. A separate DIKSHA/NISHTHA lookup is needed.
- No verified reach figures for Extramarks, Teachmint, BYJU's Classes or Educomp legacy installations, and no survey of smart-board penetration by private-school fee tier.
- No UNESCO GEM 2023 India-specific technology figures or Central Square Foundation EdTech data were retrieved.
